# Ch 8 · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids — formulas and key facts

## Carbonyl group & preparation

- C=O: sp$^2$ C, trigonal planar ($\approx$120$^\circ$), polar (C$^{\delta+}$, O$^{\delta-}$) → nucleophiles attack C.

:::formula
**Aldehydes**: 1$^\circ$ alcohol + PCC; Cu/573 K; ozonolysis (O$_3$ then Zn/H$_2$O); ethyne + H$_2$O (HgSO$_4$/dil. H$_2$SO$_4$) → ethanal;<br>
**Rosenmund** (RCOCl + H$_2$, Pd/BaSO$_4$); **Stephen** (RCN, SnCl$_2$/HCl, H$_3$O$^+$); RCN or ester + DIBAL-H, then H$_2$O;<br>
toluene → benzaldehyde: **Etard** (CrO$_2$Cl$_2$); CrO$_3$/(CH$_3$CO)$_2$O → benzylidene diacetate → H$_3$O$^+$; Cl$_2$/h$\nu$ → benzal chloride → H$_2$O, 373 K;<br>
benzene → benzaldehyde: **Gatterman–Koch** (CO + HCl, anhyd. AlCl$_3$/CuCl)<br>
**Ketones**: 2$^\circ$ alcohol + K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$; alkynes (other than ethyne) + HgSO$_4$/H$_2$SO$_4$;<br>
$\ce{2RCOCl + R{'}2Cd -> 2RCOR{'} + CdCl2}$; nitrile + Grignard then H$_3$O$^+$ ($\ce{CH3CH2CN + C6H5MgBr}$ → propiophenone); Friedel–Crafts acylation ($\ce{C6H6 + RCOCl ->[anhyd. AlCl3] C6H5COR}$)
:::

- b.p.: between hydrocarbons/ethers and alcohols of similar mass (dipole–dipole, no intermolecular H-bonding). Methanal, ethanal, propanone mix with water (H-bond with water).

## Nucleophilic addition (aldehydes $>$ ketones)

- **Mechanism**: Nu$^-$ attacks the carbonyl C (perpendicular to the plane) → sp$^3$ tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate → takes H$^+$ → product.
- Aldehydes more reactive: one alkyl group (less steric hindrance; less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$). HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$. Benzaldehyde $<$ propanal (resonance with ring lowers C$^{\delta+}$).

| HCN (trace base) | cyanohydrin $\ce{{>}C(OH)CN}$ |
|---|---|
| NaHSO$_3$ | crystalline bisulphite adduct (separation, purification; regenerates carbonyl with acid/base) |
| RMgX, then H$_2$O | alcohols |
| ROH, dry HCl | aldehyde → hemiacetal → acetal; ketone + ethylene glycol → cyclic ketal |

**Ammonia derivatives** ($\ce{{>}C=O + H2N-Z -> {>}C=N-Z + H2O}$, weakly acidic medium):

| **Reagent** | **Z** | **Product** |
|---|---|---|
| hydroxylamine | –OH | oxime |
| hydrazine | –NH$_2$ | hydrazone |
| phenylhydrazine | –NHC$_6$H$_5$ | phenylhydrazone |
| 2,4-DNP | –NHC$_6$H$_3$(NO$_2$)$_2$ | 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (yellow-orange ppt: test for C=O) |
| semicarbazide | –NHCONH$_2$ | semicarbazone |

## Reduction, oxidation, haloform

- Reduction: NaBH$_4$/LiAlH$_4$/H$_2$ → alcohols; **Clemmensen** (Zn–Hg, conc. HCl) and **Wolff–Kishner** (NH$_2$NH$_2$, then KOH/ethylene glycol, heat) → $\ce{{>}CH2}$.
- **Tollens'** (ammoniacal AgNO$_3$): $\ce{RCHO + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH- -> RCOO- + 2Ag + 2H2O + 4NH3}$ — silver mirror. All aldehydes (aliphatic and aromatic).
- **Fehling's** (A: CuSO$_4$; B: alkaline sodium potassium tartrate): $\ce{RCHO + 2Cu^2+ + 5OH- -> RCOO- + Cu2O + 3H2O}$ — red-brown ppt. Aliphatic aldehydes only (benzaldehyde: negative).
- Ketones need strong oxidants at high $T$ (C–C bonds break → mixture of acids).
- **Haloform / iodoform**: CH$_3$CO– (methyl ketones) and CH$_3$CH(OH)– compounds + NaOI (I$_2$/NaOH) → yellow CHI$_3$. Positive: ethanal, ethanol, propanone, propan-2-ol, butan-2-one, acetophenone. Negative: methanal, methanol, propanal, pentan-3-one, benzaldehyde.

## α-Hydrogen reactions & Cannizzaro

- α-H is acidic: C=O withdraws electrons and the enolate is resonance-stabilised.
- **Aldol**: carbonyls **with** α-H + dil. NaOH → β-hydroxy aldehyde/ketone → heat → α,β-unsaturated product. $\ce{2CH3CHO}$ → 3-hydroxybutanal → but-2-enal. $\ce{2CH3COCH3 ->[Ba(OH)2]}$ 4-hydroxy-4-methylpentan-2-one → 4-methylpent-3-en-2-one.
- **Cross aldol**: two different carbonyls both with α-H → 4 products. One without α-H (benzaldehyde + acetophenone) → mainly benzalacetophenone.
- **Cannizzaro**: aldehydes **without** α-H (HCHO, C$_6$H$_5$CHO) + conc. alkali → alcohol + carboxylate salt. $\ce{2HCHO + conc. KOH -> CH3OH + HCOOK}$; $\ce{2C6H5CHO + conc. NaOH -> C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa}$.
- Ring: –CHO/–CO– deactivating, **meta**: benzaldehyde + HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ (273–283 K) → 3-nitrobenzaldehyde.
- **Uses**: formalin ($\approx$40% HCHO) preserves specimens; HCHO for bakelite, urea–formaldehyde resins; ethanal → ethanoic acid, ethyl ethanoate; propanone, butanone as solvents; benzaldehyde, vanillin etc. in perfumes and flavours.

## Carboxylic acids

:::formula
**Preparation**: 1$^\circ$ alcohol/aldehyde + KMnO$_4$ or K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$; alkylbenzene + alk. KMnO$_4$ → benzoic acid (whole side chain → –COOH; 3$^\circ$ alkyl not oxidised);<br>
nitrile/amide + H$^+$ or OH$^-$ (hydrolysis); $\ce{RMgX + CO2 (dry ice)}$ then H$_3$O$^+$ → RCOOH (**+1 C**); acyl halides, anhydrides, esters + water (esters + NaOH = saponification)
:::

- b.p. higher than alcohols of similar mass: stronger H-bonding; exist as **dimers** in vapour and aprotic solvents. C$_1$–C$_4$ acids mix with water.
- **Acidity**: carboxylate ion has two *equivalent* resonance structures (charge on two O atoms) → RCOOH more acidic than phenol and alcohols. RCOOH + NaHCO$_3$ → CO$_2$ effervescence (phenol does not).
- EWG increase acidity, EDG decrease. Nearer the EWG to –COOH, stronger the acid. Phenyl/vinyl directly on –COOH increases acidity (sp$^2$ C).

**NCERT acidity order:** CF$_3$COOH $>$ CCl$_3$COOH $>$ CHCl$_2$COOH $>$ NO$_2$CH$_2$COOH $>$ NCCH$_2$COOH $>$ FCH$_2$COOH $>$ ClCH$_2$COOH $>$ BrCH$_2$COOH $>$ HCOOH $>$ ClCH$_2$CH$_2$COOH $>$ C$_6$H$_5$COOH $>$ C$_6$H$_5$CH$_2$COOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH.

:::formula
**Reactions**: 2RCOOH $\xrightarrow{\text{H}^+,\ \text{P}_2\text{O}_5,\ \Delta}$ anhydride;   esterification (H$^+$, reversible);<br>
$\ce{RCOOH + SOCl2 -> RCOCl + SO2 + HCl}$ (also PCl$_5$, PCl$_3$);   $\ce{RCOOH + NH3 -> RCOONH4 ->[\Delta] RCONH2}$ (phthalic acid → phthalimide);<br>
LiAlH$_4$ or B$_2$H$_6$ → RCH$_2$OH (NaBH$_4$ does not reduce –COOH; B$_2$H$_6$ leaves ester, nitro, halo groups alone);<br>
**Decarboxylation**: RCOONa + soda lime (NaOH + CaO), heat → R–H (**$-$1 C**);   **Kolbe electrolysis**: aqueous RCOONa → R–R;<br>
**HVZ**: $\ce{RCH2COOH ->[X2 / red P][H2O] RCH(X)COOH}$ (α-halo acid);   ring: –COOH deactivating, meta (benzoic acid → 3-nitro/3-bromobenzoic acid); **no Friedel–Crafts** (AlCl$_3$ binds to –COOH)
:::

- Uses: HCOOH (rubber, textile, dyeing, leather, electroplating); CH$_3$COOH (vinegar, solvent); hexanedioic acid (nylon-6,6); benzoate esters (perfumery); sodium benzoate (food preservative); higher fatty acids (soaps, detergents).

### Orders to know

*write the reason with the order*

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ · S$_\text{N}$1: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X · leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p. (similar mass): carboxylic acid $>$ alcohol $>$ 1$^\circ$ amine $>$ aldehyde/ketone $>$ ether $\approx$ alkane. Amines 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$. Haloalkanes RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl.
- Acidity: RCOOH $>$ phenol $>$ water $>$ alcohol; alcohols 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; picric acid $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ o- $>$ m-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ cresol.
- Carboxylic acids: F– $>$ Cl– $>$ Br–CH$_2$COOH; CCl$_3$ $>$ CHCl$_2$ $>$ CH$_2$ClCOOH; 2-chloro $>$ 3-chloro $>$ 4-chlorobutanoic; HCOOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH; 4-nitrobenzoic $>$ benzoic $>$ 4-methylbenzoic.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$; aliphatic aldehyde $>$ benzaldehyde.
- Basicity (water): (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$; benzylamine $>$ aniline; p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline.
- Reactivity of alcohols with HX / dehydration: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$; HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl.

### Conversions — step chains

*carbon count first*

**Step up one C**: R–X + KCN → RCN (then LiAlH$_4$ → amine, or H$_3$O$^+$ → acid); RMgX + CO$_2$ → RCOOH; RMgX + HCHO → RCH$_2$OH. **Step down one C**: Hoffmann bromamide; decarboxylation. **Double**: Wurtz, Kolbe electrolysis.

- Methanamine → ethanamine: HNO$_2$ → CH$_3$OH → PCl$_5$ → CH$_3$Cl → KCN → CH$_3$CN → LiAlH$_4$.
- Ethanamine → methanamine: HNO$_2$ → C$_2$H$_5$OH → K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$ → CH$_3$COOH → NH$_3$, heat → CH$_3$CONH$_2$ → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Ethanoic acid → methanamine: NH$_3$, heat → acetamide → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Benzene → aniline: conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → Sn/HCl. Aniline → chlorobenzene / benzonitrile / phenol / benzene / fluorobenzene / iodobenzene: diazotise, then CuCl / CuCN / H$_2$O / H$_3$PO$_2$ / HBF$_4$ + heat / KI.
- Aniline → benzoic acid: diazotise → CuCN → H$_3$O$^+$. Aniline → p-bromoaniline: acetylate → Br$_2$ → hydrolyse.
- Propene → propan-1-ol: B$_2$H$_6$, H$_2$O$_2$/OH$^-$. Propene → propan-2-ol: H$_2$O/H$^+$. Propanone → propene: NaBH$_4$ → conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, heat.
- Benzoic acid → benzaldehyde: SOCl$_2$ → Rosenmund. Benzene → acetophenone: CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$. Acetophenone → benzoic acid: NaOI (haloform) → H$^+$.
- Bromomethane → propanone: Mg/ether → CH$_3$MgBr + CH$_3$CN → H$_3$O$^+$.
- Ethanal → but-2-enal: dil. NaOH (aldol), heat. Ethanol → 3-hydroxybutanal: PCC → ethanal → dil. NaOH.
- Phenol → aspirin: Kolbe → salicylic acid → (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O. Phenol → anisole: NaOH → CH$_3$I. Chlorobenzene → phenol: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → H$^+$.
- Ethanol → propanenitrile: PCl$_5$ → KCN. Toluene → benzoic acid: alk. KMnO$_4$ → H$^+$.

### Top "give reason" answers

*one cause, stated in chemistry terms*

- **Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution** — resonance (partial C=X), sp$^2$ C, unstable phenyl cation, electron-rich ring repels Nu$^-$.
- **Grignard reagents are made in dry ether** — they react with water to give alkanes.
- **Chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim** — air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- **Phenol is acidic, ethanol hardly** — phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; ethoxide is not (and is destabilised by +I).
- **o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, p-isomer not** — intramolecular vs intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Alcohols boil higher than ethers of similar mass** — intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Anisole + HI gives phenol + CH$_3$I** — O–C(aryl) has partial double-bond character; S$_\text{N}$2 attack occurs at CH$_3$.
- **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones** — less steric hindrance and less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$.
- **Carboxylic acids do not give typical C=O reactions** — resonance of –COOH reduces the electrophilicity of the C.
- **Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols** — carboxylate has two equivalent resonance forms with charge on O atoms only.
- **Chloroacetic acid stronger than acetic acid** — $-I$ of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion.
- **Aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine/NH$_3$** — lone pair delocalised into the ring.
- **(CH$_3$)$_3$N weaker base than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water** — poorer H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding.
- **Aniline gives meta product on nitration** — in strong acid it forms anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
- **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts** — it forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ deactivates the ring.
- **Acetylation of –NH$_2$ before nitration/bromination** — lowers activation, avoids polysubstitution and oxidation.
- **Gabriel synthesis cannot give aryl amines** — aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide anion.
- **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl ones** — resonance with the ring.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing** — no free anomeric –OH (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are bonded).
- **Amino acids have high m.p. and are water-soluble** — exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
