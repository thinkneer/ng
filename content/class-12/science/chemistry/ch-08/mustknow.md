## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Nature of the carbonyl carbon:** electrophilic ($\delta+$), $sp^2$, planar; nucleophiles attack it.
- **Nucleophilic addition, intermediate:** tetrahedral alkoxide ion, which then takes a proton.
- **Why aldehydes are more reactive than ketones (steric):** only one alkyl group crowds the carbonyl carbon.
- **Why aldehydes are more reactive than ketones (electronic):** one alkyl group releases less electron density than two.
- **Why benzaldehyde is less reactive than ethanal:** resonance with the ring reduces the positive charge on carbon.
- **Aldol condensation:** carbonyl compound with α-hydrogen + dilute alkali gives β-hydroxy carbonyl, then α,β-unsaturated on heating.
- **Cannizzaro reaction:** aldehyde without α-hydrogen + concentrated alkali gives an alcohol and an acid salt.
- **Why α-hydrogens are acidic:** the carbanion (enolate) formed is stabilised by resonance with C=O.
- **Why carboxylic acids are stronger than phenols:** carboxylate ion has two equivalent resonance structures, charge on two oxygens.
- **Effect of electron-withdrawing groups on acids:** increase acidity by stabilising the carboxylate ion.
- **Effect of electron-releasing groups on acids:** decrease acidity.
- **Why carboxylic acids have high boiling points:** they form hydrogen-bonded dimers.
- **Why carboxylic acids do not give carbonyl addition:** resonance with –OH makes the carbonyl carbon less electrophilic.

## Key formulas

:::formula Rosenmund reduction
$\ce{RCOCl + H2 ->[Pd/BaSO4] RCHO + HCl}$<br>
Poisoned catalyst stops at the aldehyde. Slip: leaving out $\ce{BaSO4}$.
:::

:::formula Stephen reaction
$\ce{RCN + SnCl2 + HCl -> RCH=NH ->[H3O+] RCHO}$<br>
Nitrile to aldehyde.
:::

:::formula Etard reaction
$\ce{C6H5CH3 ->[CrO2Cl2][H3O+] C6H5CHO}$<br>
Chromyl chloride oxidises the methyl group only as far as the aldehyde.
:::

:::formula Gatterman–Koch reaction
$\ce{C6H6 + CO + HCl ->[anhyd. AlCl3/CuCl] C6H5CHO}$<br>
Benzene to benzaldehyde.
:::

:::formula Clemmensen reduction
$\ce{{>}C=O ->[Zn-Hg/conc. HCl] {>}CH2}$<br>
Carbonyl to methylene, in acid.
:::

:::formula Wolff–Kishner reduction
$\ce{{>}C=O ->[NH2NH2][KOH/ethylene glycol, heat] {>}CH2}$<br>
Carbonyl to methylene, in base.
:::

:::formula Aldol condensation of ethanal
$\ce{2CH3CHO ->[dil. NaOH] CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO ->[heat] CH3CH=CHCHO}$<br>
Needs α-hydrogen. Slip: forgetting the β-OH, or dehydrating without "heat".
:::

:::formula Cannizzaro reaction
$\ce{2HCHO + KOH(conc.) -> CH3OH + HCOOK}$<br>
No α-hydrogen. Benzaldehyde gives benzyl alcohol + sodium benzoate.
:::

:::formula Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky reaction
$\ce{RCH2COOH ->[X2/red P][H2O] RCH(X)COOH}$<br>
α-Halogenation of an acid that has an α-hydrogen.
:::

:::formula Decarboxylation with soda lime
$\ce{RCOONa + NaOH ->[CaO, heat] RH + Na2CO3}$<br>
The hydrocarbon has one carbon fewer.
:::

:::formula Tests for carbonyl compounds and acids
2,4-DNP: orange precipitate, any C=O · Tollens': silver mirror, all aldehydes<br>
Fehling's: red-brown $\ce{Cu2O}$, aliphatic aldehydes only · $\ce{NaHCO3}$: $\ce{CO2}$, carboxylic acids<br>
Iodoform ($\ce{I2}$/NaOH): yellow $\ce{CHI3}$, for $\ce{CH3CO-}$ and $\ce{CH3CH(OH)-}$
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton Word problem method (3–5 marks)
*e.g. C$_5$H$_{10}$O gives 2,4-DNP derivative and iodoform but not Tollens'.*<br>
(i) 2,4-DNP positive → C=O present. (ii) Tollens' negative → ketone. (iii) Iodoform positive → CH$_3$CO– group. (iv) Fit the formula: **pentan-2-one** CH$_3$COCH$_2$CH$_2$CH$_3$. (v) Write one equation per test.<br>
Mark split: each correct structure/name [1]; supporting equations carry the rest.
:::

:::skeleton Nucleophilic addition of HCN (2 marks)
$\ce{HCN + OH- -> CN- + H2O}$ (base generates $\ce{CN-}$) → $\ce{CN-}$ attacks carbonyl C → tetrahedral alkoxide $\ce{{>}C(O^-)CN}$ (slow) → takes H$^+$ from HCN/H$_2$O → cyanohydrin $\ce{{>}C(OH)CN}$ [1 + 1].
:::

:::skeleton Distinguish (2 marks)
*Propanal vs propanone*: Tollens' — propanal gives silver mirror, propanone does not. Iodoform — propanone gives yellow ppt, propanal does not. One test is enough; give both observations.
:::

:::skeleton Acidity order with reason (2 marks)
FCH$_2$COOH $>$ ClCH$_2$COOH $>$ BrCH$_2$COOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH: $-I$ effect falls F $>$ Cl $>$ Br, so stabilisation of carboxylate falls; –CH$_3$ (+I) destabilises.
:::

:::skeleton Conversions (2–3 marks)
Benzoic acid $\xrightarrow{\text{SOCl}_2}$ benzoyl chloride $\xrightarrow{\text{H}_2,\ \text{Pd/BaSO}_4}$ benzaldehyde.<br>
Propanone $\xrightarrow{\text{NaBH}_4}$ propan-2-ol $\xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\ \Delta}$ propene.<br>
Ethanol $\xrightarrow{\text{PCC}}$ ethanal $\xrightarrow{\text{dil. NaOH}}$ 3-hydroxybutanal.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones.**<br>→ Two alkyl groups in ketones hinder attack and reduce C$^{\delta+}$ by +I effect.
2. **Benzaldehyde less reactive than propanal.**<br>→ Resonance of C=O with the ring lowers the electrophilicity of the carbonyl C.
3. **Carboxylic acids do not give 2,4-DNP/NH$_2$OH reactions.**<br>→ Resonance in –COOH reduces the electrophilic character of the carbonyl C.
4. **Carboxylic acids boil higher than alcohols of similar mass.**<br>→ Stronger intermolecular H-bonding; they exist as dimers.
5. **α-Hydrogens of carbonyl compounds are acidic.**<br>→ Strong electron withdrawal by C=O and resonance stabilisation of the enolate ion.
6. **Only one –NH$_2$ of semicarbazide reacts.**<br>→ The other –NH$_2$ is attached to C=O; its lone pair is delocalised (amide resonance), so it is not nucleophilic.
7. **Chloroacetic acid is stronger than acetic acid.**<br>→ $-I$ effect of Cl stabilises the chloroacetate ion.
8. **Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel–Crafts.**<br>→ –COOH deactivates the ring and the catalyst AlCl$_3$ binds to the carboxyl group.
9. **NH$_2$–Z reactions are done in weakly acidic medium.**<br>→ Acid protonates C=O and makes it more electrophilic; too much acid protonates the nitrogen nucleophile.
10. **Formaldehyde gives Cannizzaro, acetaldehyde gives aldol.**<br>→ HCHO has no α-H; CH$_3$CHO has α-H.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Iodoform test positive: ethanal, ethanol, propanone, propan-2-ol, butan-2-one, acetophenone.
- Iodoform test negative: methanal, methanol, propanal, pentan-3-one, benzaldehyde.
- Benzaldehyde with Fehling's solution: no reaction; with Tollens' reagent: silver mirror.
- Reactivity towards nucleophilic addition: HCHO > $\ce{CH3CHO}$ > $\ce{CH3COCH3}$.
- Acid strength: HCOOH > $\ce{CH3COOH}$ > $\ce{CH3CH2COOH}$.
- Acid strength of chloroethanoic acids: $\ce{Cl3CCOOH}$ > $\ce{Cl2CHCOOH}$ > $\ce{ClCH2COOH}$ > $\ce{CH3COOH}$.
- Acid strength on the ring: 4-nitrobenzoic > benzoic > 4-methylbenzoic acid.
- Acid strength with distance: 2-chlorobutanoic > 3-chlorobutanoic > 4-chlorobutanoic acid.
- Grignard reagent + $\ce{CO2}$ gives: an acid with one carbon more.
- Kolbe electrolysis of a carboxylate salt gives: an alkane with the two alkyl groups joined.
- $\ce{NaBH4}$ does not reduce: carboxylic acids; $\ce{LiAlH4}$ and $\ce{B2H6}$ do.
- Formalin: about 40% aqueous methanal, a preservative.
- –CHO and –COOH on a benzene ring are: deactivating and meta-directing.
- Benzoic acid does not undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction because: the ring is deactivated and $\ce{AlCl3}$ binds to –COOH.
- Product of an aldehyde with HCN: cyanohydrin.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Aldol or Cannizzaro
Using Cannizzaro for compounds *with* α-H (they give aldol).
:::

:::tip Ketones with Tollens' and Fehling's
Saying ketones give Tollens'/Fehling's.
:::

:::tip Benzaldehyde with Fehling's
Benzaldehyde in Fehling's — negative.
:::

:::tip Reagent conditions
Forgetting “anhydrous AlCl$_3$” / “Pd–BaSO$_4$”.
:::

:::tip Writing the aldol product
Aldol product written without the β-OH, or dehydrated product without heating.
:::

:::tip Word problems
In word problems, giving a name without the structure or without equations.
:::

:::tip Reducing an acid
Writing NaBH$_4$ to reduce an acid.
:::
