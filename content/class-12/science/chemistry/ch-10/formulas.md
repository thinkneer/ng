# Ch 10 · Biomolecules — formulas and key facts

## Carbohydrates

- Optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that give these on hydrolysis. Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, ribose); oligosaccharides (2–10 units: sucrose, maltose, lactose); polysaccharides (starch, cellulose, glycogen). Aldoses vs ketoses; glucose = aldohexose, fructose = ketohexose.
- **Reducing sugars** (reduce Tollens'/Fehling's — free –CHO/C=O): all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose. **Non-reducing**: sucrose.
- Glucose from sucrose (dil. HCl/H$_2$SO$_4$, warm) or starch (dil. H$_2$SO$_4$, 393 K, 2–3 atm).

| **Glucose + reagent** | **Result → conclusion** |
|---|---|
| HI, prolonged heating | n-hexane → 6 C in a straight chain |
| NH$_2$OH; HCN | oxime; cyanohydrin → carbonyl group present |
| Bromine water | gluconic acid (C$_6$) → carbonyl is an **aldehyde** |
| Acetic anhydride | glucose pentaacetate → **five –OH** groups on different C |
| Nitric acid | saccharic acid (dicarboxylic) → a **1$^\circ$ alcohol** group present |

- **Open chain fails to explain**: no 2,4-DNP or Schiff's test and no NaHSO$_3$ adduct; pentaacetate does not react with NH$_2$OH; two forms α (m.p. 419 K) and β (423 K).
- **Cyclic**: C5–OH adds to –CHO → six-membered **pyranose** ring (hemiacetal). α and β differ only at C1 — **anomers**; C1 = anomeric carbon. Haworth projection.
- D/L: configuration relative to glyceraldehyde (–OH on right at the lowest chiral C = D); (+)/($-$) = direction of rotation. Glucose: D-(+); fructose: D-($-$), five-membered **furanose** ring (C5–OH + C2 carbonyl).
- **Glycosidic linkage**: oxide link joining two monosaccharide units.

| **Sugar** | **Units & link** | **Reducing?** |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrose | α-D-glucose C1 – β-D-fructose C2 | no |
| Maltose | α-D-glucose C1 – α-D-glucose C4 | yes |
| Lactose (milk) | β-D-galactose C1 – β-D-glucose C4 | yes |
| Starch | α-glucose: amylose (15–20%, soluble, unbranched, C1–C4); amylopectin (80–85%, insoluble, branched, C1–C4 + C1–C6) | – |
| Cellulose | β-D-glucose, straight chains, C1–C4 (plant cell walls; humans cannot digest) | – |
| Glycogen | like amylopectin, more branched; animal starch (liver, muscles, brain) | – |

- **Invert sugar**: sucrose (dextrorotatory) → glucose ($+52.5^\circ$) + fructose ($-92.4^\circ$): mixture is laevorotatory.
- Importance: energy source; storage (starch, glycogen); cell walls (cellulose); wood, cotton; ribose/deoxyribose in nucleic acids.

## Proteins

- α-Amino acids $\ce{H2N-CH(R)-COOH}$. **Essential** (from diet): valine, leucine, isoleucine, arginine, lysine, threonine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, histidine. Neutral/acidic/basic by number of –NH$_2$ and –COOH.
- **Zwitterion** $\ce{H3N+-CH(R)-COO-}$: amphoteric, high m.p., dipolar salt. All except glycine are optically active, mostly L.
- **Peptide bond** –CO–NH–: amide link between –COOH of one and –NH$_2$ of the next, with loss of water. $>$100 residues, mass $>$10 000 u → protein.
- **Fibrous** (parallel chains, H-bonds/disulphide links; insoluble): keratin (hair, wool, silk), myosin (muscle). **Globular** (coiled spheres; soluble): insulin, albumins.
- Structure: **primary** — sequence; **secondary** — α-helix (right-handed, H-bonds between –NH and C=O of adjacent turns) or β-pleated sheet (chains side by side, H-bonds); **tertiary** — further folding (H-bonds, disulphide, van der Waals, electrostatic); **quaternary** — arrangement of two or more subunits.
- **Denaturation**: heat or pH change breaks H-bonds; protein unfolds and loses activity; 2$^\circ$/3$^\circ$ lost, **primary intact**. Egg white coagulates on boiling; milk curdles (lactic acid).
- **Enzymes**: biocatalysts, mostly globular proteins, highly specific (maltase → maltose; sucrase/invertase → sucrose); lower activation energy.

## Vitamins

| **Vitamin** | **Source** | **Deficiency** |
|---|---|---|
| A | fish liver oil, carrots, butter, milk | xerophthalmia (hardening of cornea), night blindness |
| B$_1$ (thiamine) | yeast, milk, green vegetables, cereals | beriberi |
| B$_2$ (riboflavin) | milk, egg white, liver, kidney | cheilosis, digestive disorders, burning skin |
| B$_6$ (pyridoxine) | yeast, milk, egg yolk, cereals, grams | convulsions |
| B$_{12}$ | meat, fish, egg, curd | pernicious anaemia |
| C (ascorbic acid) | citrus fruits, amla, green leafy vegetables | scurvy (bleeding gums) |
| D | sunlight, fish, egg yolk | rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| E | vegetable oils (wheat germ, sunflower) | fragile RBCs, muscular weakness |
| K | green leafy vegetables | longer blood-clotting time |

- Fat-soluble A, D, E, K (stored in liver, adipose tissue). Water-soluble B group and C — excreted in urine, must be taken regularly (except B$_{12}$).

## Nucleic acids & hormones

- Nucleotide = base + pentose sugar + phosphate; nucleoside = base + sugar (base at C1$'$; phosphate at C5$'$). Nucleotides joined by **phosphodiester** links (C5$'$–C3$'$).
- **DNA**: β-D-2-deoxyribose; bases A, G (purines), C, T; double helix (Watson–Crick), A=T (2 H-bonds), G$\equiv$C (3 H-bonds); heredity, replication. **RNA**: β-D-ribose; A, G, C, **U**; usually single strand; mRNA, rRNA, tRNA; protein synthesis.
- **Hormones**: chemical messengers from endocrine glands. Steroids (oestrogens, androgens), polypeptides (insulin, endorphins), amino-acid derivatives (adrenaline, noradrenaline, thyroxine). Insulin + glucagon keep blood glucose steady; low thyroxine → hypothyroidism (lethargy, obesity), excess → hyperthyroidism; low iodine → goitre. Glucocorticoids (carbohydrate metabolism, inflammation), mineralocorticoids (water/salt excretion); testosterone, oestradiol, progesterone.

:::tip
Sucrose is non-reducing because both anomeric carbons are tied up in the glycosidic bond. Cellulose is β-linked (indigestible), starch α-linked. DNA has thymine, RNA uracil. Denaturation keeps the primary structure. Vitamin C cannot be stored.
:::

### Orders to know

*write the reason with the order*

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ · S$_\text{N}$1: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X · leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p. (similar mass): carboxylic acid $>$ alcohol $>$ 1$^\circ$ amine $>$ aldehyde/ketone $>$ ether $\approx$ alkane. Amines 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$. Haloalkanes RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl.
- Acidity: RCOOH $>$ phenol $>$ water $>$ alcohol; alcohols 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; picric acid $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ o- $>$ m-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ cresol.
- Carboxylic acids: F– $>$ Cl– $>$ Br–CH$_2$COOH; CCl$_3$ $>$ CHCl$_2$ $>$ CH$_2$ClCOOH; 2-chloro $>$ 3-chloro $>$ 4-chlorobutanoic; HCOOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH; 4-nitrobenzoic $>$ benzoic $>$ 4-methylbenzoic.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$; aliphatic aldehyde $>$ benzaldehyde.
- Basicity (water): (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$; benzylamine $>$ aniline; p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline.
- Reactivity of alcohols with HX / dehydration: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$; HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl.

### Conversions — step chains

*carbon count first*

**Step up one C**: R–X + KCN → RCN (then LiAlH$_4$ → amine, or H$_3$O$^+$ → acid); RMgX + CO$_2$ → RCOOH; RMgX + HCHO → RCH$_2$OH. **Step down one C**: Hoffmann bromamide; decarboxylation. **Double**: Wurtz, Kolbe electrolysis.

- Methanamine → ethanamine: HNO$_2$ → CH$_3$OH → PCl$_5$ → CH$_3$Cl → KCN → CH$_3$CN → LiAlH$_4$.
- Ethanamine → methanamine: HNO$_2$ → C$_2$H$_5$OH → K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$ → CH$_3$COOH → NH$_3$, heat → CH$_3$CONH$_2$ → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Ethanoic acid → methanamine: NH$_3$, heat → acetamide → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Benzene → aniline: conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → Sn/HCl. Aniline → chlorobenzene / benzonitrile / phenol / benzene / fluorobenzene / iodobenzene: diazotise, then CuCl / CuCN / H$_2$O / H$_3$PO$_2$ / HBF$_4$ + heat / KI.
- Aniline → benzoic acid: diazotise → CuCN → H$_3$O$^+$. Aniline → p-bromoaniline: acetylate → Br$_2$ → hydrolyse.
- Propene → propan-1-ol: B$_2$H$_6$, H$_2$O$_2$/OH$^-$. Propene → propan-2-ol: H$_2$O/H$^+$. Propanone → propene: NaBH$_4$ → conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, heat.
- Benzoic acid → benzaldehyde: SOCl$_2$ → Rosenmund. Benzene → acetophenone: CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$. Acetophenone → benzoic acid: NaOI (haloform) → H$^+$.
- Bromomethane → propanone: Mg/ether → CH$_3$MgBr + CH$_3$CN → H$_3$O$^+$.
- Ethanal → but-2-enal: dil. NaOH (aldol), heat. Ethanol → 3-hydroxybutanal: PCC → ethanal → dil. NaOH.
- Phenol → aspirin: Kolbe → salicylic acid → (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O. Phenol → anisole: NaOH → CH$_3$I. Chlorobenzene → phenol: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → H$^+$.
- Ethanol → propanenitrile: PCl$_5$ → KCN. Toluene → benzoic acid: alk. KMnO$_4$ → H$^+$.

### Top "give reason" answers

*one cause, stated in chemistry terms*

- **Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution** — resonance (partial C=X), sp$^2$ C, unstable phenyl cation, electron-rich ring repels Nu$^-$.
- **Grignard reagents are made in dry ether** — they react with water to give alkanes.
- **Chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim** — air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- **Phenol is acidic, ethanol hardly** — phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; ethoxide is not (and is destabilised by +I).
- **o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, p-isomer not** — intramolecular vs intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Alcohols boil higher than ethers of similar mass** — intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Anisole + HI gives phenol + CH$_3$I** — O–C(aryl) has partial double-bond character; S$_\text{N}$2 attack occurs at CH$_3$.
- **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones** — less steric hindrance and less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$.
- **Carboxylic acids do not give typical C=O reactions** — resonance of –COOH reduces the electrophilicity of the C.
- **Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols** — carboxylate has two equivalent resonance forms with charge on O atoms only.
- **Chloroacetic acid stronger than acetic acid** — $-I$ of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion.
- **Aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine/NH$_3$** — lone pair delocalised into the ring.
- **(CH$_3$)$_3$N weaker base than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water** — poorer H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding.
- **Aniline gives meta product on nitration** — in strong acid it forms anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
- **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts** — it forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ deactivates the ring.
- **Acetylation of –NH$_2$ before nitration/bromination** — lowers activation, avoids polysubstitution and oxidation.
- **Gabriel synthesis cannot give aryl amines** — aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide anion.
- **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl ones** — resonance with the ring.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing** — no free anomeric –OH (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are bonded).
- **Amino acids have high m.p. and are water-soluble** — exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
