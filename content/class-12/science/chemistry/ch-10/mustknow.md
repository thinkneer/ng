## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Carbohydrates**: optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that produce such units on hydrolysis.
- **Reducing sugars**: reduce Fehling's/Tollens' (free aldehydic or ketonic group) — all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose. Sucrose is non-reducing.
- **Anomers**: α and β forms of glucose differing only in configuration at C1 (the anomeric carbon).
- **Glycosidic linkage**: oxide linkage joining two monosaccharide units through an oxygen atom.
- **Peptide bond**: amide link (–CO–NH–) formed between –COOH of one amino acid and –NH$_2$ of another with loss of water.
- **Denaturation**: loss of biological activity when a protein is subjected to change in temperature or pH; H-bonds break, the protein unfolds; secondary and tertiary structures are destroyed but the primary structure remains.
- **Essential amino acids**: cannot be synthesised in the body; must be taken in the diet.
- **Vitamins**: organic compounds needed in small amounts in the diet to perform specific biological functions for normal health, growth and maintenance.
- **Enzymes**: biocatalysts, mostly globular proteins, very specific in action.
- **Hormones**: molecules that act as intercellular messengers, produced by endocrine glands.

## Key formulas

### Key facts

:::formula
Glucose: aldohexose, D-(+), pyranose ring. Fructose: ketohexose, D-($-$), furanose ring<br>
Sucrose = α-glucose (C1) + β-fructose (C2) · Maltose = 2 α-glucose (C1–C4) · Lactose = β-galactose + β-glucose (C1–C4)<br>
Starch α-linked (amylose 15–20%, amylopectin 80–85%); cellulose β-linked; glycogen = animal starch<br>
DNA: deoxyribose, A G C T, A=T (2 H-bonds), G$\equiv$C (3) · RNA: ribose, A G C U<br>
Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K; water-soluble B and C
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Sucrose is non-reducing.**<br>→ Both reducing groups (C1 of glucose, C2 of fructose) are used in the glycosidic bond.
2. **Glucose pentaacetate does not react with NH$_2$OH.**<br>→ No free –CHO group: glucose exists in the cyclic hemiacetal form.
3. **Glucose does not give Schiff's test or NaHSO$_3$ adduct.**<br>→ Aldehyde group is not free in the cyclic structure.
4. **Cellulose cannot be digested by humans.**<br>→ Humans lack the enzyme to break β-glycosidic links.
5. **Amino acids have high m.p. and dissolve in water.**<br>→ They exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
6. **Vitamin C must be taken regularly.**<br>→ Water-soluble; excreted in urine, not stored.
7. **Sucrose hydrolysis gives ``invert sugar''.**<br>→ Sign of rotation changes from + (sucrose) to $-$ (mixture), because fructose ($-92.4^\circ$) outweighs glucose ($+52.5^\circ$).
8. **Boiling an egg hardens the white.**<br>→ Heat denatures albumin; the protein coagulates.
9. **DNA strands are complementary.**<br>→ A pairs only with T (2 H-bonds) and G with C (3 H-bonds).

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Deficiencies: A — night blindness, xerophthalmia; B$_1$ — beriberi; B$_2$ — cheilosis; B$_6$ — convulsions; B$_{12}$ — pernicious anaemia; C — scurvy; D — rickets/osteomalacia; E — fragile RBCs; K — slow clotting.
- All naturally occurring α-amino acids except glycine are optically active, mostly L.
- Keratin (hair, wool, silk) and myosin — fibrous; insulin and albumins — globular.
- Hydrolysis: sucrose → glucose + fructose; maltose → 2 glucose; lactose → glucose + galactose; starch → glucose.
- Glucose α (m.p. 419 K) and β (423 K) forms. D/L = configuration (not rotation); (+)/($-$) = rotation.
- Nucleoside = base + sugar; nucleotide = nucleoside + phosphate. Purines A, G; pyrimidines C, T, U.
- Hormones: insulin/glucagon (blood sugar), thyroxine (deficiency: hypothyroidism; iodine lack: goitre), adrenaline (emergency response), steroids (sex hormones, glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids).

## Case-based angles

- Diet/nutrition passage → vitamins, deficiency diseases, storage.
- Lab tests on sugars (Fehling's, Tollens', iodine for starch) → reducing/non-reducing, polysaccharide type.
- Cooking and protein (boiling eggs, curd) → denaturation.
- DNA fingerprinting / genetics context → base pairing, DNA vs RNA.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip
(1) Calling sucrose reducing. (2) D means ``dextrorotatory'' — wrong; D is configuration. (3) Denaturation ``destroys the primary structure'' — wrong. (4) Thymine in RNA / uracil in DNA. (5) Cellulose α-linked — wrong (β). (6) Mixing up nucleoside and nucleotide. (7) Writing vitamin B$_{12}$ deficiency as beriberi (it's pernicious anaemia; beriberi is B$_1$).
:::
