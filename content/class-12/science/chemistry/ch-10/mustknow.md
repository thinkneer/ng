## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Carbohydrates:** optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds giving them on hydrolysis.
- **Monosaccharide:** carbohydrate that cannot be hydrolysed to simpler units.
- **Reducing sugar:** reduces Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent; has a free anomeric carbon.
- **Anomers:** α and β forms of a sugar differing only in configuration at the anomeric carbon.
- **Glycosidic linkage:** oxygen bridge joining two monosaccharide units, formed with loss of water.
- **Invert sugar:** equimolar glucose + fructose from sucrose; rotation changes from dextro to laevo.
- **Amino acid zwitterion:** dipolar ion $\ce{H3N+-CHR-COO-}$; makes amino acids amphoteric and salt-like.
- **Essential amino acids:** cannot be made in the body; must come from the diet.
- **Peptide bond:** amide link –CO–NH– between two amino acids, formed with loss of water.
- **Primary structure of a protein:** the sequence of amino acids in the chain.
- **Secondary structure of a protein:** α-helix or β-pleated sheet, held by hydrogen bonds.
- **Denaturation:** loss of biological activity when heat or pH change unfolds the protein.
- **What survives denaturation:** the primary structure; secondary and tertiary structures are destroyed.
- **Enzymes:** biocatalysts, mostly globular proteins, highly specific in action.
- **Vitamins:** organic compounds needed in small amounts in the diet for specific biological functions.
- **Nucleotide:** pentose sugar + nitrogenous base + phosphate.
- **Nucleoside:** pentose sugar + nitrogenous base only.
- **Hormones:** chemical messengers produced by endocrine glands and carried in the blood.

## Key formulas

:::formula Glucose and fructose at a glance
Glucose: aldohexose, D-(+), six-membered pyranose ring, anomeric carbon C1<br>
Fructose: ketohexose, D-(−), five-membered furanose ring, anomeric carbon C2
:::

:::formula Evidence for the structure of glucose
HI, heat → n-hexane (straight chain of six C) · $\ce{NH2OH}$, HCN → oxime, cyanohydrin (carbonyl group)<br>
$\ce{Br2}$ water → gluconic acid (–CHO) · acetic anhydride → pentaacetate (five –OH) · $\ce{HNO3}$ → saccharic acid (primary –OH)
:::

:::formula Why glucose must be cyclic
No Schiff's test, no $\ce{NaHSO3}$ addition; the pentaacetate does not react with $\ce{NH2OH}$; α and β forms exist<br>
Explained by a hemiacetal ring between C1 and the –OH on C5.
:::

:::formula Disaccharides and their linkages
Sucrose = α-D-glucose (C1) + β-D-fructose (C2): non-reducing<br>
Maltose = two α-D-glucose (C1–C4): reducing · Lactose = β-D-galactose (C1) + β-D-glucose (C4): reducing
:::

:::formula Polysaccharides
Starch: α-glucose; amylose (15–20%, unbranched, C1–C4) + amylopectin (80–85%, branched at C1–C6)<br>
Cellulose: β-glucose, straight chain, C1–C4 · Glycogen: animal starch, like amylopectin but more branched
:::

:::formula DNA and RNA
DNA: β-D-2-deoxyribose · A, G, C, T · double helix · A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds)<br>
RNA: β-D-ribose · A, G, C, U · single strand · types m-RNA, r-RNA, t-RNA
:::

:::formula Vitamins and deficiency diseases
A: night blindness, xerophthalmia · $\ce{B1}$: beriberi · $\ce{B2}$: cheilosis · $\ce{B6}$: convulsions · $\ce{B12}$: pernicious anaemia<br>
C: scurvy · D: rickets, osteomalacia · E: fragile red blood cells · K: slow blood clotting<br>
Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K. Water-soluble: B group and C.
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton Reactions proving glucose structure (3 marks)
HI, heat → n-hexane: six C in a straight chain [½]<br>
NH$_2$OH → oxime; HCN → cyanohydrin: C=O present [½]<br>
Br$_2$ water → gluconic acid: C=O is –CHO [½]<br>
(CH$_3$CO)$_2$O → pentaacetate: five –OH [½]<br>
HNO$_3$ → saccharic acid: one 1$^\circ$ –OH (–CH$_2$OH) also oxidised [1]
:::

:::skeleton Denaturation (2 marks)
Definition (change in $T$ or pH → loss of biological activity) [1] + what changes (H-bonds break, 2$^\circ$/3$^\circ$ structure lost, 1$^\circ$ intact) + example (egg white coagulates on boiling; milk curdles) [1].
:::

:::skeleton DNA vs RNA (3 marks)
Sugar: 2-deoxyribose vs ribose · Base: thymine vs uracil · Structure: double helix vs usually single strand · Function: heredity/replication vs protein synthesis (any three, 1 each).
:::

:::skeleton Levels of protein structure (3–4 marks)
Primary: amino acid sequence · Secondary: α-helix (intramolecular H-bonds) or β-pleated sheet (H-bonds between chains) · Tertiary: overall folding — H-bonds, disulphide, van der Waals, electrostatic → fibrous/globular shape · Quaternary: arrangement of subunits.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Sucrose is non-reducing.**<br>→ Both reducing groups (C1 of glucose, C2 of fructose) are used in the glycosidic bond.
2. **Glucose pentaacetate does not react with NH$_2$OH.**<br>→ No free –CHO group: glucose exists in the cyclic hemiacetal form.
3. **Glucose does not give Schiff's test or NaHSO$_3$ adduct.**<br>→ Aldehyde group is not free in the cyclic structure.
4. **Cellulose cannot be digested by humans.**<br>→ Humans lack the enzyme to break β-glycosidic links.
5. **Amino acids have high m.p. and dissolve in water.**<br>→ They exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
6. **Vitamin C must be taken regularly.**<br>→ Water-soluble; excreted in urine, not stored.
7. **Sucrose hydrolysis gives “invert sugar”.**<br>→ Sign of rotation changes from + (sucrose) to $-$ (mixture), because fructose ($-92.4^\circ$) outweighs glucose ($+52.5^\circ$).
8. **Boiling an egg hardens the white.**<br>→ Heat denatures albumin; the protein coagulates.
9. **DNA strands are complementary.**<br>→ A pairs only with T (2 H-bonds) and G with C (3 H-bonds).

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- The only α-amino acid that is not optically active: glycine.
- Configuration of most natural amino acids: L.
- Fibrous proteins: keratin (hair, wool), myosin (muscle); insoluble in water.
- Globular proteins: insulin, albumin; soluble in water.
- Hydrolysis of sucrose gives: glucose + fructose.
- Hydrolysis of maltose gives: two glucose units.
- Hydrolysis of lactose gives: glucose + galactose.
- D and L refer to: configuration, not the direction of rotation.
- Melting points of α- and β-glucose: 419 K and 423 K.
- Purine bases: adenine and guanine.
- Pyrimidine bases: cytosine, thymine and uracil.
- Linkage between nucleotides: phosphodiester, between C5′ and C3′ of the sugars.
- The water-soluble vitamin that is stored in the body: $\ce{B12}$.
- Hormones controlling blood glucose: insulin lowers it, glucagon raises it.
- Hormone containing iodine: thyroxine; its deficiency causes hypothyroidism.
- Chemical nature of hormones: steroids, polypeptides (insulin), amino acid derivatives (adrenaline, thyroxine).
- Everyday examples of denaturation: boiling an egg, curdling of milk.
- Humans cannot digest cellulose because: we lack the enzyme that breaks the β-glycosidic linkage.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Is sucrose reducing
Calling sucrose reducing.
:::

:::tip Meaning of D
D means “dextrorotatory” — wrong; D is configuration.
:::

:::tip Denaturation and primary structure
Denaturation “destroys the primary structure” — wrong.
:::

:::tip Thymine or uracil
Thymine in RNA / uracil in DNA.
:::

:::tip Linkage in cellulose
Cellulose α-linked — wrong (β).
:::

:::tip Nucleoside or nucleotide
Mixing up nucleoside and nucleotide.
:::

:::tip Vitamin B12 or B1
Writing vitamin B$_{12}$ deficiency as beriberi (it's pernicious anaemia; beriberi is B$_1$).
:::
