## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Werner**: metals show primary (ionisable, = OS, satisfied by negative ions) and secondary (non-ionisable, = CN, fixed, satisfied by neutral or negative ligands, directional) valencies.
- **Coordination entity**: central metal atom/ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules (e.g. $\ce{[CoCl3(NH3)3]}$).
- **Ligands**: ions or molecules bound to the central atom/ion in the coordination entity (electron-pair donors).
- **Coordination number**: number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central atom/ion.
- **Ambidentate ligand**: can ligate through two different atoms ($\ce{NO2-}$/$\ce{ONO-}$, $\ce{SCN-}$/$\ce{NCS-}$).
- **Chelate**: a di- or polydentate ligand using two or more donor atoms to bind a single metal ion, forming a ring; more stable than similar unidentate complexes.
- **Homoleptic**: metal bound to only one kind of donor group; **heteroleptic**: more than one kind.
- **Spectrochemical series**: ligands arranged in order of increasing field strength (increasing $\Delta_o$).
- **Crystal field splitting**: splitting of degenerate d orbitals in the field of ligands; $\Delta_o$ for octahedral.
- **Synergic bonding**: M–C $\sigma$ bond (C lone pair → vacant metal orbital) plus $\pi$ back-bond (filled metal d → empty $\pi^*$ of CO); each strengthens the other.

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **$\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$ paramagnetic, $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$ diamagnetic.**<br>→ Cl$^-$ weak: no pairing, $sp^3$, 2 unpaired. CN$^-$ strong: pairing, $dsp^2$ square planar, 0 unpaired.
2. **$\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ diamagnetic though tetrahedral.**<br>→ Ni(0) $3d^84s^2$; strong-field CO pushes 4s electrons into 3d → $3d^{10}$; $sp^3$; no unpaired electron.
3. **$\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$ strongly, $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$ weakly paramagnetic.**<br>→ H$_2$O weak field: $d^5$ high spin, 5 unpaired; CN$^-$ strong: low spin, 1 unpaired.
4. **$\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$ inner-orbital, $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ outer-orbital.**<br>→ NH$_3$ pairs the $3d^6$ electrons → $d^2sp^3$, diamagnetic; F$^-$ weak → $sp^3d^2$, 4 unpaired.
5. **Low-spin tetrahedral complexes are rare.**<br>→ $\Delta_t=\frac49\Delta_o$ is usually smaller than pairing energy, so electrons do not pair.
6. **Anhydrous CuSO$_4$ white, CuSO$_4$·5H$_2$O blue.**<br>→ Without water ligands there is no crystal-field splitting, so no d–d transition.
7. **Green $\ce{[Ni(H2O)6]^2+}$ turns violet with en.**<br>→ en is a stronger-field ligand → larger $\Delta_o$ → absorbs shorter wavelength → complementary colour shifts.
8. **Chelates are more stable.**<br>→ Ring formation (chelate effect): two or more donor atoms of one ligand bind the same metal.
9. **trans-$\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ is optically inactive.**<br>→ It has a plane of symmetry; its mirror image is superimposable.
10. **Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism.**<br>→ All four positions are adjacent to each other.
11. **M–C bond in carbonyls has double-bond character.**<br>→ Synergic bonding: $\sigma$ donation + $\pi$ back-donation into CO $\pi^*$.
12. **CFT limitation.**<br>→ Treats ligands as point charges, so anionic ligands should split most, yet they lie at the weak end of the series; covalent character ignored.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- CN: $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$ 6; $\ce{[Fe(C2O4)3]^3-}$ 6; $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ 4; EDTA$^{4-}$ hexadentate.
- OS: Ni in $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ 0; Fe in $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ 0; Co in $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ +3; Pt in $\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ +2; Fe in $\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$ +3.
- Shapes: $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$ square planar ($dsp^2$); $\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$ tetrahedral ($sp^3$); $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ trigonal bipyramidal; $\ce{[Cr(CO)6]}$ octahedral.
- $\mu$: $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$ 1.73; $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ 4.90; $\ce{[MnCl4]^2-}$ 5.92; $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$ 0 BM.
- Spectrochemical series: weakest I$^-$, strongest CO; NH$_3$ $<$ en $<$ CN$^-$ $<$ CO; H$_2$O $<$ NH$_3$.
- $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]Cl3}$ → 4 ions, 3 AgCl; $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]Cl2}$ → 3 ions, 2 AgCl; $\ce{[CoCl2(NH3)4]Cl}$ → 2 ions, 1 AgCl.
- Isomer counts: $\ce{[MA2B2]}$ sq. planar 2; $\ce{[MABCD]}$ 3; $\ce{[MA4B2]}$ 2; $\ce{[MA3B3]}$ 2 (fac/mer); $\ce{[M(AA)3]}$ 2 optical; $\ce{[M(AA)2B2]}$ 3.
- Ionisation isomers $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$ / $\ce{[Co(NH3)5SO4]Br}$: BaCl$_2$ gives white ppt with the first; AgNO$_3$ gives pale yellow ppt with the second.
- Linkage isomerism needs an ambidentate ligand. cis-platin = cis-$\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$ (anticancer). Wilkinson's catalyst $\ce{[RhCl(PPh3)3]}$.
- Haemoglobin Fe; chlorophyll Mg; vitamin B$_{12}$ Co. Ni$^{2+}$ + DMG → red ppt. EDTA: water hardness, lead poisoning.

## Case-based angles

- Absorption/colour data for one metal with different ligands → order of $\Delta_o$, spectrochemical series.
- Measured $\mu$ values → number of unpaired electrons → geometry/hybridisation.
- Werner's AgNO$_3$ / conductivity experiments → structures of CoCl$_3$·$x$NH$_3$.
- Medicine and biology (cis-platin, EDTA chelation, haemoglobin) → naming, geometry, isomerism.
- Gemstones (ruby, emerald) or coloured solutions → CFT and d–d transitions.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip
(1) Counting CN as the number of ligands when en/ox/EDTA are present. (2) Naming errors: amine for ammine, chloro for chlorido, missing ``-ate'', wrong alphabetical order (judge by ligand name, not prefix), missing Roman numeral. (3) Assuming every NH$_3$ complex is low spin. (4) Saying tetrahedral complexes show geometrical isomerism. (5) Drawing trans-$\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ as chiral. (6) Inner orbital = $(n-1)d$; outer = $nd$ — don't swap. (7) Answering in CFT language when VBT is asked (and vice versa). (8) Ni in $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ is 0, not +2.
:::
