## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Primary valency (Werner):** ionisable; equals the oxidation state; satisfied by negative ions.
- **Secondary valency (Werner):** non-ionisable; equals the coordination number; directional, so it fixes the shape.
- **Coordination entity:** central metal atom or ion bonded to a fixed number of ligands.
- **Ligand:** ion or molecule that donates an electron pair to the central metal.
- **Coordination number:** number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal.
- **Denticity:** number of donor atoms through which one ligand binds the metal.
- **Ambidentate ligand:** can bind through two different atoms, as $\ce{NO2-}$/$\ce{ONO-}$ and $\ce{SCN-}$/$\ce{NCS-}$.
- **Chelate:** ring formed when a polydentate ligand binds one metal ion through two or more atoms.
- **Chelate effect:** chelate complexes are more stable than similar complexes of unidentate ligands.
- **Homoleptic complex:** metal bound to only one kind of donor group.
- **Heteroleptic complex:** metal bound to more than one kind of donor group.
- **Double salt:** dissociates completely into simple ions in water, as Mohr's salt does.
- **Crystal field splitting:** splitting of the degenerate d orbitals in the field of the ligands.
- **Spectrochemical series:** ligands arranged in order of increasing field strength, that is increasing $\Delta_o$.
- **Low-spin complex:** $\Delta_o>P$; electrons pair in $t_{2g}$ (strong-field ligand).
- **High-spin complex:** $\Delta_o<P$; electrons enter $e_g$ before pairing (weak-field ligand).
- **Synergic bonding:** σ donation from CO to metal and π back-donation from metal to CO strengthen each other.

## Key formulas

:::formula Oxidation state of the metal
$x+\sum(\text{ligand charges})=\text{charge on the complex}$<br>
Neutral ligands ($\ce{NH3}$, $\ce{H2O}$, CO, en) count zero. Slip: Ni in $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ is 0.
:::

:::formula Coordination number
CN = number of donor atoms bonded to the metal<br>
en and oxalate count 2 each, EDTA counts 6. Slip: counting ligands instead of donor atoms.
:::

:::formula Tetrahedral and octahedral splitting
$\Delta_t=\tfrac49\Delta_o$<br>
$\Delta_t$ is small, so tetrahedral complexes are almost always high spin.
:::

:::formula Crystal field stabilisation energy (octahedral)
CFSE $=(-0.4\,n_{t_{2g}}+0.6\,n_{e_g})\Delta_o$<br>
$n$ = number of electrons in each set. Add pairing energy $P$ for each extra pair formed.
:::

:::formula Spin-only magnetic moment of a complex
$\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM<br>
$n$ = unpaired electrons after allowing for the ligand field. $n=0$ means diamagnetic.
:::

:::formula Ions and AgCl from a complex
Moles of AgCl = number of $\ce{Cl-}$ outside the bracket; ions in solution = counter ions + 1<br>
$\ce{[Co(NH3)6]Cl3}$: 4 ions, 3 AgCl. $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]Cl2}$: 3 ions, 2 AgCl. $\ce{[CoCl2(NH3)4]Cl}$: 2 ions, 1 AgCl.
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton VBT for a complex (3 marks)
*e.g. $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$.*<br>
Fe$^{3+}$: [Ar]$3d^5$; CN$^-$ is a strong-field ligand → electrons pair up [½]<br>
`[↑↓][↑↓][↑ ][××][××]` `[××]` `[××][××][××]` (3d | 4s | 4p; ×× = CN$^-$ pairs) → **$d^2sp^3$** [1]<br>
Shape: **octahedral**; inner-orbital, low-spin complex [½]<br>
One unpaired electron → **paramagnetic**, $\mu\approx1.73$ BM [1]
:::

:::skeleton CFT for $d^4$ (2 marks)
Sketch: d orbitals split into lower $t_{2g}$ ($-0.4\Delta_o$) and upper $e_g$ ($+0.6\Delta_o$) [1]<br>
$\Delta_o<P$ (weak field): $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$, high spin, 4 unpaired; $\Delta_o>P$ (strong field): $t_{2g}^4e_g^0$, low spin, 2 unpaired [½ + ½]
:::

:::skeleton IUPAC name (1 mark each)
(i) Cation before anion. (ii) Inside [ ]: ligands alphabetically (ignore di/tri/bis) → metal → OS in Roman numerals. (iii) Anionic complex → metal + “-ate” (ferrate, cuprate, argentate, aurate). (iv) Check: $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]Cl}$ = dichloridobis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) chloride.
:::

:::skeleton Isomers of $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ (3 marks)
Draw the octahedron: **trans** (Cl at 180$^\circ$) — has a plane of symmetry, optically inactive; **cis** (Cl at 90$^\circ$) — non-superimposable mirror images d and l. Total 3 stereoisomers [1 each drawing].
:::

:::skeleton Werner / AgCl (2 marks)
*CoCl$_3$·5NH$_3$ gives 2 mol AgCl per mol.* → 2 Cl$^-$ outside → $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]Cl2}$; ions = 3; CN = 6; OS = +3.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **$\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$ paramagnetic, $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$ diamagnetic.**<br>→ Cl$^-$ weak: no pairing, $sp^3$, 2 unpaired. CN$^-$ strong: pairing, $dsp^2$ square planar, 0 unpaired.
2. **$\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ diamagnetic though tetrahedral.**<br>→ Ni(0) $3d^84s^2$; strong-field CO pushes 4s electrons into 3d → $3d^{10}$; $sp^3$; no unpaired electron.
3. **$\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^3+}$ strongly, $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$ weakly paramagnetic.**<br>→ H$_2$O weak field: $d^5$ high spin, 5 unpaired; CN$^-$ strong: low spin, 1 unpaired.
4. **$\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$ inner-orbital, $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$ outer-orbital.**<br>→ NH$_3$ pairs the $3d^6$ electrons → $d^2sp^3$, diamagnetic; F$^-$ weak → $sp^3d^2$, 4 unpaired.
5. **Low-spin tetrahedral complexes are rare.**<br>→ $\Delta_t=\frac49\Delta_o$ is usually smaller than pairing energy, so electrons do not pair.
6. **Anhydrous CuSO$_4$ white, CuSO$_4$·5H$_2$O blue.**<br>→ Without water ligands there is no crystal-field splitting, so no d–d transition.
7. **Green $\ce{[Ni(H2O)6]^2+}$ turns violet with en.**<br>→ en is a stronger-field ligand → larger $\Delta_o$ → absorbs shorter wavelength → complementary colour shifts.
8. **Chelates are more stable.**<br>→ Ring formation (chelate effect): two or more donor atoms of one ligand bind the same metal.
9. **trans-$\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ is optically inactive.**<br>→ It has a plane of symmetry; its mirror image is superimposable.
10. **Tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism.**<br>→ All four positions are adjacent to each other.
11. **M–C bond in carbonyls has double-bond character.**<br>→ Synergic bonding: $\sigma$ donation + $\pi$ back-donation into CO $\pi^*$.
12. **CFT limitation.**<br>→ Treats ligands as point charges, so anionic ligands should split most, yet they lie at the weak end of the series; covalent character ignored.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Coordination number of Co in $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$: 6.
- Denticity of EDTA$^{4-}$: six (hexadentate).
- Oxidation state of Ni in $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ and Fe in $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$: zero.
- Oxidation state of Co in $\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$: +3.
- Shape and hybridisation of $\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}$: square planar, $dsp^2$, diamagnetic.
- Shape and hybridisation of $\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}$: tetrahedral, $sp^3$, paramagnetic.
- Shape of $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$: trigonal bipyramidal.
- Magnetic moment of $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$: 1.73 BM (one unpaired electron).
- Magnetic moment of $\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$: 4.90 BM (four unpaired electrons).
- Magnetic moment of $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$: zero; it is diamagnetic.
- Spectrochemical order of common ligands: $\ce{I-}$ < $\ce{Cl-}$ < $\ce{F-}$ < $\ce{H2O}$ < $\ce{NH3}$ < en < $\ce{CN-}$ < CO.
- Geometrical isomers of square planar $\ce{[MA2B2]}$: two, cis and trans.
- Geometrical isomers of octahedral $\ce{[MA3B3]}$: two, fac and mer.
- Isomers of $\ce{[M(AA)2B2]}$: three; trans, and a cis pair of optical isomers.
- Test for ionisation isomers $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$ and $\ce{[Co(NH3)5SO4]Br}$: $\ce{BaCl2}$ gives white precipitate with the first.
- Linkage isomerism needs: an ambidentate ligand.
- cis-Platin: cis-$\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$, used against cancer.
- Wilkinson's catalyst: $\ce{[RhCl(PPh3)3]}$, for hydrogenation of alkenes.
- Metals in haemoglobin, chlorophyll, vitamin $\ce{B12}$: Fe, Mg, Co.
- Test for $\ce{Ni^2+}$: red precipitate with dimethylglyoxime (DMG).
- Uses of EDTA: estimating hardness of water; treating lead poisoning.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Coordination number with chelating ligands
Counting CN as the number of ligands when en/ox/EDTA are present.
:::

:::tip Naming slips
Naming errors: amine for ammine, chloro for chlorido, missing “-ate”, wrong alphabetical order (judge by ligand name, not prefix), missing Roman numeral.
:::

:::tip Ammonia is not always strong field
Assuming every NH$_3$ complex is low spin.
:::

:::tip Geometrical isomerism in tetrahedral complexes
Saying tetrahedral complexes show geometrical isomerism.
:::

:::tip Chirality of the trans isomer
Drawing trans-$\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ as chiral.
:::

:::tip Inner and outer orbital complexes
Inner orbital = $(n-1)d$; outer = $nd$ — don't swap.
:::

:::tip VBT or CFT language
Answering in CFT language when VBT is asked (and vice versa).
:::

:::tip Oxidation state of Ni in nickel carbonyl
Ni in $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ is 0, not +2.
:::
