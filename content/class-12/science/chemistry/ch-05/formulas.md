# Ch 5 · Coordination Compounds — formulas and key facts

## Werner's theory

- **Primary valency**: ionisable, = oxidation state, satisfied by negative ions.
- **Secondary valency**: non-ionisable, = coordination number, fixed for a metal, satisfied by neutral or negative ligands; directed in space → definite geometry.

| **Formula** | **Werner formula** | **AgCl (mol)** | **Ions** | **Colour** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CoCl$_3$·6NH$_3$ | $\ce{[Co(NH3)6]Cl3}$ | 3 | 4 | yellow |
| CoCl$_3$·5NH$_3$ | $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]Cl2}$ | 2 | 3 | purple |
| CoCl$_3$·4NH$_3$ | $\ce{[CoCl2(NH3)4]Cl}$ | 1 | 2 | green (trans), violet (cis) |

- **Double salt** (Mohr's salt $\ce{FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O}$, carnallite $\ce{KCl.MgCl2.6H2O}$, potash alum) dissociates fully into simple ions. **Complex** keeps its complex ion: $\ce{K4[Fe(CN)6]}$ gives no Fe$^{2+}$/CN$^-$ test.

## Key terms

- **Ligand**: ion/molecule donating an electron pair to the central atom. Unidentate (Cl$^-$, H$_2$O, NH$_3$); didentate (en = H$_2$NCH$_2$CH$_2$NH$_2$, ox$^{2-}$ = C$_2$O$_4^{2-}$); hexadentate EDTA$^{4-}$.
- **Ambidentate**: can bind through two different atoms — $\ce{NO2-}$ (N) / $\ce{ONO-}$ (O); $\ce{SCN-}$ (S) / $\ce{NCS-}$ (N).
- **Chelate**: di- or polydentate ligand binding through two or more atoms to form a ring; more stable than similar unidentate complexes (chelate effect).
- **Denticity**: number of donor atoms in a ligand. **Coordination number**: number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the metal (by $\sigma$ bonds) — e.g. $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$ CN 6.
- **Coordination sphere**: metal + ligands in [ ]; ions outside are **counter ions**. **Coordination polyhedron**: spatial arrangement of donor atoms (octahedral, square planar, tetrahedral).
- **Homoleptic**: one kind of ligand ($\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$); **heteroleptic**: more than one ($\ce{[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+}$).

:::formula
Oxidation number: $x+\sum(\text{ligand charges})=\text{charge on complex}$<br>
$\ce{K3[Fe(CN)6]}$: $x-6=-3\Rightarrow x=+3$ · $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$: $x=0$ · $\ce{[Co(en)2Cl2]+}$: $x-2=+1\Rightarrow +3$
:::

- **Naming**: cation first, then anion. Inside the complex: ligands in *alphabetical* order (ignore di/tri) → metal → oxidation state in Roman numerals.
- Prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- for simple ligands; bis-, tris-, tetrakis- when the ligand name already has a prefix or is complex (put it in brackets): bis(ethane-1,2-diamine).
- Anionic complex: metal ends in **-ate**; use Latin for Fe (ferrate), Cu (cuprate), Ag (argentate), Au (aurate), Pb (plumbate), Sn (stannate). Others: cobaltate, nickelate, chromate, zincate, platinate, manganate.
- **Writing formulae**: central atom first, ligands alphabetical by formula/abbreviation, whole entity in [ ], charge outside.

## Isomerism

### Structural isomerism

- **Ionisation**: ion inside/outside swaps — $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(SO4)]Br}$ and $\ce{[Co(NH3)5Br]SO4}$ (test: BaCl$_2$ vs AgNO$_3$).
- **Linkage**: ambidentate ligand — $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2}$ (yellow) and $\ce{[Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2}$ (red).
- **Coordination**: ligands swap between cationic and anionic complexes — $\ce{[Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6]}$ and $\ce{[Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]}$.
- **Solvate (hydrate)**: water inside vs outside — $\ce{[Cr(H2O)6]Cl3}$ (violet), $\ce{[CrCl(H2O)5]Cl2.H2O}$ (grey-green), $\ce{[CrCl2(H2O)4]Cl.2H2O}$ (dark green).

- **Geometrical**: square planar $\ce{[MX2L2]}$ cis/trans ($\ce{[Pt(NH3)2Cl2]}$; cis-platin is the anticancer drug). Tetrahedral complexes show *no* geometrical isomerism.
- Octahedral $\ce{[MX2L4]}$ cis/trans ($\ce{[Co(NH3)4Cl2]+}$); $\ce{[MX3L3]}$ **fac/mer** ($\ce{[Co(NH3)3(NO2)3]}$).

- **Optical**: non-superimposable mirror images (d/l, dextro/laevo). Common in octahedral complexes with didentate ligands: $\ce{[Co(en)3]^3+}$, *cis*-$\ce{[CoCl2(en)2]+}$ (the trans form has a plane of symmetry — optically inactive).

- $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$: Ni(0) $3d^84s^2$ → $3d^{10}$, $sp^3$, tetrahedral, diamagnetic. $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$: $d^5$ low spin, $d^2sp^3$, 1 unpaired (1.73 BM). $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^4-}$: $d^6$, diamagnetic. $\ce{[Cr(NH3)6]^3+}$: $d^3$, $d^2sp^3$, 3 unpaired. $\ce{[Mn(CN)6]^3-}$: $d^4$, $d^2sp^3$, 2 unpaired. $\ce{[MnCl4]^2-}$: $sp^3$, 5 unpaired.
- **Limitations of VBT**: many assumptions; no quantitative magnetic data; does not explain colour; no distinction of weak/strong ligands; cannot predict tetrahedral vs square planar for CN 4; no quantitative stability.

- Ligands treated as point charges; d orbitals split.
- Octahedral: $e_g$ ($d_{x^2-y^2}$, $d_{z^2}$) up; $t_{2g}$ ($d_{xy}$, $d_{yz}$, $d_{xz}$) down.
- Tetrahedral: inverted; $\Delta_t=\frac49\Delta_o$ → always high spin.

:::formula
**Spectrochemical series** (increasing $\Delta_o$):<br>
$\ce{I-}$ $<$ $\ce{Br-}$ $<$ $\ce{SCN-}$ $<$ $\ce{Cl-}$ $<$ $\ce{S^2-}$ $<$ $\ce{F-}$ $<$ $\ce{OH-}$ $<$ $\ce{C2O4^2-}$ $<$ $\ce{H2O}$ $<$ $\ce{NCS-}$ $<$ edta$^{4-}$ $<$ $\ce{NH3}$ $<$ en $<$ $\ce{CN-}$ $<$ CO<br>
$d^4$–$d^7$: $\Delta_o<P$ (weak field) → high spin, e.g. $d^4$ = $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$;   $\Delta_o>P$ (strong field) → low spin, $d^4$ = $t_{2g}^4e_g^0$<br>
CFSE (octahedral) $=(-0.4\,n_{t_{2g}}+0.6\,n_{e_g})\,\Delta_o$ (+ $P$ for each extra pairing)
:::

| | **Weak field (high spin)** | **Strong field (low spin)** |
|---|---|---|
| $d^4$ | $t_{2g}^3e_g^1$, 4 unp. | $t_{2g}^4$, 2 unp. |
| $d^5$ | $t_{2g}^3e_g^2$, 5 unp. ($\ce{[FeF6]^3-}$) | $t_{2g}^5$, 1 unp. ($\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}$) |
| $d^6$ | $t_{2g}^4e_g^2$, 4 unp. ($\ce{[CoF6]^3-}$, $\ce{[Fe(H2O)6]^2+}$) | $t_{2g}^6$, 0 ($\ce{[Co(NH3)6]^3+}$, $\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^4-}$) |
| $d^7$ | $t_{2g}^5e_g^2$, 3 unp. | $t_{2g}^6e_g^1$, 1 unp. |

- **Colour**: electron jumps $t_{2g}$ → $e_g$ absorbing light of energy $\Delta_o$; complementary colour seen. $\ce{[Ti(H2O)6]^3+}$ ($d^1$) violet. Anhydrous CuSO$_4$ is white, CuSO$_4$·5H$_2$O blue — no ligands, no splitting. Adding en to green $\ce{[Ni(H2O)6]^2+}$ gives blue → violet $\ce{[Ni(en)3]^2+}$ (larger $\Delta_o$, absorbs shorter $\lambda$). Ruby: Cr$^{3+}$ in Al$_2$O$_3$; emerald: Cr$^{3+}$ in beryl.
- Stronger ligand → larger $\Delta_o$ → absorbs higher energy (shorter $\lambda$).
- **Limitations of CFT**: treats ligands as point charges, so anionic ligands should split most — yet they are at the weak end; ignores covalent character of M–L bonds.

## Metal carbonyls

- $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ tetrahedral; $\ce{[Fe(CO)5]}$ trigonal bipyramidal; $\ce{[Cr(CO)6]}$ octahedral; $\ce{[Mn2(CO)10]}$ two square pyramids joined by Mn–Mn; $\ce{[Co2(CO)8]}$ Co–Co bond bridged by two CO.
- **Synergic bonding**: $\sigma$ bond — lone pair of C → vacant metal orbital; $\pi$ back-bond — filled metal d → vacant antibonding $\pi^*$ of CO. Each strengthens the other: M–C bond strengthens, C–O bond weakens.

## Importance & applications

| Qualitative analysis | Ni$^{2+}$ + dimethylglyoxime → red precipitate; colour reactions of Cu$^{2+}$, Fe$^{3+}$, etc. |
|---|---|
| Hardness of water | titration with Na$_2$EDTA (stable Ca$^{2+}$/Mg$^{2+}$ complexes) |
| Metallurgy | Ag, Au leached as $\ce{[Ag(CN)2]-}$, $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$; Ni purified via $\ce{[Ni(CO)4]}$ (Mond process) |
| Biology | chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe), vitamin B$_{12}$ cyanocobalamin (Co) |
| Catalysis | Wilkinson's catalyst $\ce{[(Ph3P)3RhCl]}$ — hydrogenation of alkenes |
| Electroplating | smooth Ag/Au coats from $\ce{[Ag(CN)2]-}$, $\ce{[Au(CN)2]-}$ |
| Photography | hypo dissolves AgBr as $\ce{[Ag(S2O3)2]^3-}$ |
| Medicine | cis-platin (anticancer); EDTA (lead poisoning); D-penicillamine, desferrioxamine B (excess Cu, Fe) |

:::skeleton
STANDARD QUESTION RECIPES **(1) Hybridisation, shape, magnetism from a formula.** OS of metal → $d^n$ → ligand strong or weak (series) → pair up if strong (CN$^-$, CO, en, NH$_3$ with Co$^{3+}$) → count empty orbitals → hybridisation → shape → unpaired $n$ → $\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}$.<br>
**(2) $\mu$ given, find $n$.** 1.73→1, 2.83→2, 3.87→3, 4.90→4, 5.92→5. E.g. Fe$^{2+}$ complex with 4.90 BM → high spin, outer orbital.<br>
**(3) Name ↔ formula.** Charges first, then alphabetical ligands, then OS; check ``-ate'' for anions.<br>
**(4) Count ions / AgCl.** Only ions outside [ ] ionise. 1 mol $\ce{[CoCl(NH3)5]Cl2}$ + excess AgNO$_3$ → 2 mol AgCl.<br>
**(5) Draw isomers.** Octahedral: put identical ligands at 90$^\circ$ (cis) or 180$^\circ$ (trans); check mirror image for en complexes.<br>
**(6) $3d^n$ in $t_{2g}$/$e_g$** and CFSE for weak vs strong field; which absorbs at longer $\lambda$ (weaker ligand).
:::

:::tip
Ni$^{2+}$ with CN$^-$ is square planar ($dsp^2$), with Cl$^-$ tetrahedral ($sp^3$). NH$_3$ behaves as strong field with Co$^{3+}$ but $\ce{[Ni(NH3)6]^2+}$ is $sp^3d^2$ (only 2 unpaired either way). $d^3$ (Cr$^{3+}$) is always inner orbital $d^2sp^3$. Tetrahedral complexes: no geometrical isomers, almost always high spin.
:::
