# Ch 6 · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates, not exact counts: ★★★ asked in most years · ★★ often · ★ sometimes. Marks in square brackets.

1. ★★★ Write the reaction: Finkelstein, Swarts, Wurtz, Wurtz–Fittig, Fittig, Sandmeyer (often two in one question). [1–2]
2. ★★★ Which undergoes S$_\text{N}$2 (or S$_\text{N}$1) faster in a pair, and why; inversion vs racemisation. [2–3]
3. ★★★ Why are haloarenes less reactive towards nucleophilic substitution? Why does –NO$_2$ at o/p help? [2–3]
4. ★★ Arrange: b.p., S$_\text{N}$1/S$_\text{N}$2 reactivity, dipole moment, density. [1–2]
5. ★★ Chirality, enantiomers, racemic mixture; spot the chiral molecule. [1–2]
6. ★★ Products: KCN vs AgCN; KNO$_2$ vs AgNO$_2$; aq. vs alc. KOH; major alkene (Zaitsev); HBr with/without peroxide. [1–2]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **S$_\text{N}$2 mechanism:** one step; nucleophile attacks from the back as the halide leaves.
- **S$_\text{N}$2 rate law:** rate $=k[\ce{RX}][\ce{Nu-}]$; second order overall.
- **S$_\text{N}$2 stereochemistry:** inversion of configuration.
- **S$_\text{N}$1 mechanism:** two steps; slow ionisation to a carbocation, then fast attack by the nucleophile.
- **S$_\text{N}$1 rate law:** rate $=k[\ce{RX}]$; first order, independent of the nucleophile.
- **S$_\text{N}$1 stereochemistry:** racemisation, because the planar carbocation is attacked from both faces.
- **Solvent favouring S$_\text{N}$1:** polar protic (water, alcohols), which stabilises the ions.
- **Chiral molecule:** not superimposable on its mirror image.
- **Asymmetric carbon:** carbon bonded to four different groups.
- **Enantiomers:** non-superimposable mirror-image isomers that rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions.
- **Racemic mixture:** equal amounts of two enantiomers; zero optical rotation.
- **Zaitsev's rule:** the preferred alkene has more alkyl groups on the doubly bonded carbons.
- **Ambident nucleophile:** has two attacking sites, as $\ce{CN-}$ and $\ce{NO2-}$ do.
- **Grignard reagent:** R–MgX; destroyed by any proton source (water, alcohols) to give RH.
- **Why haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution:** resonance gives C–X partial double-bond character; carbon is $sp^2$.
- **Halogen on a benzene ring:** deactivating but ortho- and para-directing.

## Key formulas

:::formula Alcohol to chloroalkane with thionyl chloride
$\ce{R-OH + SOCl2 -> R-Cl + SO2 + HCl}$<br>
Preferred method: both by-products are gases, so the product is pure.
:::

:::formula Finkelstein reaction
$\ce{R-X + NaI ->[dry acetone] R-I + NaX}$ (X = Cl, Br)<br>
Works because NaCl or NaBr precipitates in dry acetone. Slip: leaving out "dry acetone".
:::

:::formula Swarts reaction
$\ce{R-Br + AgF -> R-F + AgBr}$<br>
Makes alkyl fluorides; $\ce{Hg2F2}$, $\ce{CoF2}$ or $\ce{SbF3}$ also work.
:::

:::formula Ambident nucleophiles with haloalkanes
$\ce{R-X + KCN -> R-CN}$ · $\ce{R-X + AgCN -> R-NC}$<br>
$\ce{R-X + KNO2 -> R-O-N=O}$ · $\ce{R-X + AgNO2 -> R-NO2}$<br>
Ionic K salts attack through C (or O); covalent Ag salts through N.
:::

:::formula Wurtz reaction
$\ce{2R-X + 2Na ->[dry ether] R-R + 2NaX}$<br>
Doubles the carbon chain. Slip: leaving out "dry ether".
:::

:::formula Wurtz–Fittig and Fittig reactions
$\ce{Ar-X + R-X + 2Na ->[dry ether] Ar-R + 2NaX}$ (Wurtz–Fittig)<br>
$\ce{2Ar-X + 2Na ->[dry ether] Ar-Ar + 2NaX}$ (Fittig)
:::

:::formula Sandmeyer reaction
$\ce{ArN2+Cl- ->[CuCl/HCl] ArCl + N2}$ (CuBr/HBr gives ArBr)<br>
Route to aryl halides from aniline through the diazonium salt.
:::

:::formula Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination)
$\ce{CH3CH2CHBrCH3 ->[alc. KOH] CH3CH=CHCH3}$ (major) $+\ \ce{CH3CH2CH=CH2}$<br>
Alcoholic KOH eliminates; aqueous KOH substitutes. Major product by Zaitsev's rule.
:::

:::formula Grignard reagent: formation and hydrolysis
$\ce{R-X + Mg ->[dry ether] R-MgX}$   $\ce{RMgX + H2O -> RH + Mg(OH)X}$<br>
Everything must be dry.
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton S$_\text{N}$2 faster in a pair (2 marks)
*1-bromobutane or 2-bromobutane?*<br>
1-Bromobutane reacts faster by S$_\text{N}$2 [½] — it is primary; less steric hindrance to the backside attack of the nucleophile at the carbon bearing Br [1]; transition state forms more easily [½].
:::

:::skeleton Racemisation in S$_\text{N}$1 (2 marks)
Slow step: C–X ionises → **planar** carbocation (sp$^2$) [1]. Nucleophile attacks from either face equally → both enantiomers → racemic mixture (optically inactive) [1].
:::

:::skeleton Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution (3 marks)
(i) Resonance: lone pair of X delocalised into ring → C–X partial double bond, harder to break. (ii) C is sp$^2$, more electronegative → shorter, stronger C–X bond. (iii) Phenyl cation not resonance-stabilised → no S$_\text{N}$1; electron-rich ring repels nucleophile, backside attack blocked [1 each].
:::

:::skeleton Named reaction (1 mark each)
Reactant(s) → product with reagent and condition over the arrow; name the reaction if not given. E.g. Swarts: $\ce{CH3Br + AgF -> CH3F + AgBr}$.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Grignard reagent prepared in dry ether.**<br>→ It reacts with water to form an alkane; ether also stabilises it.
2. **Chloroform stored in dark, full bottles.**<br>→ Air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene ($\ce{COCl2}$).
3. **Allylic/benzylic halides react fast by S$_\text{N}$1.**<br>→ The carbocation is resonance-stabilised.
4. **Chlorine is deactivating yet o/p-directing.**<br>→ $-I$ effect withdraws electron density (deactivates); $+R$ effect raises density at o/p.
5. **p-Dichlorobenzene has higher m.p. than o-/m-isomers.**<br>→ Symmetrical; fits better in the crystal lattice.
6. **Haloalkanes barely dissolve in water.**<br>→ Energy to break water's H-bonds is not repaid by weaker haloalkane–water attractions.
7. **KCN gives nitrile, AgCN isocyanide.**<br>→ KCN ionic: C (more nucleophilic site) attacks; AgCN covalent: only N lone pair free.
8. **Thionyl chloride is preferred for R–Cl.**<br>→ By-products SO$_2$ and HCl are gases and escape, leaving pure R–Cl.
9. **–NO$_2$ at o/p speeds up substitution of chlorobenzene.**<br>→ It withdraws electrons and stabilises the carbanion intermediate by resonance; at meta it cannot.
10. **Racemic mixture is optically inactive.**<br>→ Rotation by one enantiomer cancels the other (external compensation).

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- S$_\text{N}$2 reactivity order: $\ce{CH3X}$ > 1° > 2° > 3°.
- S$_\text{N}$1 reactivity order: 3° > 2° > 1° > $\ce{CH3X}$.
- Leaving-group order: I > Br > Cl > F.
- Boiling points for the same alkyl group: RI > RBr > RCl > RF.
- Effect of branching on boiling point: lowers it.
- Dipole moment order of methyl halides: $\ce{CH3Cl}$ > $\ce{CH3F}$ > $\ce{CH3Br}$ > $\ce{CH3I}$.
- The peroxide effect is shown only by: HBr, not HCl or HI.
- Propene + HBr with peroxide gives: 1-bromopropane (anti-Markovnikov).
- Chiral molecules to recognise: butan-2-ol, 2-bromobutane, 2-chlorobutane.
- para-Dichlorobenzene melts higher than ortho and meta because: its symmetry packs better in the lattice.
- Conditions for chlorobenzene + NaOH: 623 K and 300 atm.
- Nitro groups at ortho and para make chlorobenzene: more reactive towards nucleophiles.
- Allylic and benzylic halides are fast in S$_\text{N}$1 because: their carbocations are resonance-stabilised.
- Chloroform is stored in dark, full bottles because: light and air oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- Freons and $\ce{CCl4}$ harm the environment by: depleting the ozone layer.
- DDT is a problem because: it is non-biodegradable and accumulates in fatty tissue.
- Use of iodoform: antiseptic.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Dry ether in Wurtz–Fittig
Writing Wurtz–Fittig without “dry ether”.
:::

:::tip Aqueous or alcoholic KOH
Using aq. KOH for elimination (alcoholic is needed).
:::

:::tip Peroxide effect
Applying the peroxide effect to HCl/HI.
:::

:::tip Stereochemistry of SN1
Saying S$_\text{N}$1 gives inversion.
:::

:::tip Products from chlorobenzene
Missing the minor product in EAS of chlorobenzene (write both o and p; p major).
:::

:::tip Aryl or vinylic halide
Calling an aryl halide a “vinylic” halide.
:::

:::tip Why Finkelstein works
Forgetting that Finkelstein works because NaCl/NaBr precipitates in dry acetone.
:::
