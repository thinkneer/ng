## Must-know lines and definitions

- **S$_\text{N}$2**: rate depends on both haloalkane and nucleophile; one step; nucleophile attacks from the side opposite the halogen → **inversion of configuration**.
- **S$_\text{N}$1**: rate depends only on haloalkane; two steps via carbocation; favoured in polar protic solvents; optically active substrate gives **racemisation**.
- **Chiral**: object/molecule not superimposable on its mirror image; carbon with four different groups is an asymmetric (chiral) centre.
- **Enantiomers**: non-superimposable mirror-image stereoisomers. **Racemic mixture**: equal proportions of enantiomers; zero rotation.
- **Zaitsev rule**: in dehydrohalogenation the preferred alkene has more alkyl groups on the doubly bonded carbons.
- **Ambident nucleophile**: has two nucleophilic sites ($\ce{CN-}$, $\ce{NO2-}$).
- **Grignard reagents** react with any source of protons (water, alcohols, amines) to give hydrocarbons.

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton Mc · S$_\text{N}$2 faster in a pair (2 marks)
*1-bromobutane or 2-bromobutane?*<br>
1-Bromobutane reacts faster by S$_\text{N}$2 [½] — it is primary; less steric hindrance to the backside attack of the nucleophile at the carbon bearing Br [1]; transition state forms more easily [½].
:::

:::skeleton Mc · Racemisation in S$_\text{N}$1 (2 marks)
Slow step: C–X ionises → **planar** carbocation (sp$^2$) [1]. Nucleophile attacks from either face equally → both enantiomers → racemic mixture (optically inactive) [1].
:::

:::skeleton R · Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution (3 marks)
(i) Resonance: lone pair of X delocalised into ring → C–X partial double bond, harder to break. (ii) C is sp$^2$, more electronegative → shorter, stronger C–X bond. (iii) Phenyl cation not resonance-stabilised → no S$_\text{N}$1; electron-rich ring repels nucleophile, backside attack blocked [1 each].
:::

:::skeleton Eq · Named reaction (1 mark each)
Reactant(s) → product with reagent and condition over the arrow; name the reaction if not given. E.g. Swarts: $\ce{CH3Br + AgF -> CH3F + AgBr}$.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Grignard reagent prepared in dry ether.**<br>→ It reacts with water to form an alkane; ether also stabilises it.
2. **Chloroform stored in dark, full bottles.**<br>→ Air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene ($\ce{COCl2}$).
3. **Allylic/benzylic halides react fast by S$_\text{N}$1.**<br>→ The carbocation is resonance-stabilised.
4. **Chlorine is deactivating yet o/p-directing.**<br>→ $-I$ effect withdraws electron density (deactivates); $+R$ effect raises density at o/p.
5. **p-Dichlorobenzene has higher m.p. than o-/m-isomers.**<br>→ Symmetrical; fits better in the crystal lattice.
6. **Haloalkanes barely dissolve in water.**<br>→ Energy to break water's H-bonds is not repaid by weaker haloalkane–water attractions.
7. **KCN gives nitrile, AgCN isocyanide.**<br>→ KCN ionic: C (more nucleophilic site) attacks; AgCN covalent: only N lone pair free.
8. **Thionyl chloride is preferred for R–Cl.**<br>→ By-products SO$_2$ and HCl are gases and escape, leaving pure R–Cl.
9. **–NO$_2$ at o/p speeds up substitution of chlorobenzene.**<br>→ It withdraws electrons and stabilises the carbanion intermediate by resonance; at meta it cannot.
10. **Racemic mixture is optically inactive.**<br>→ Rotation by one enantiomer cancels the other (external compensation).

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; S$_\text{N}$1: reverse. Leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p.: RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl $>$ RF; branching lowers b.p. Dipole: CH$_3$Cl $>$ CH$_3$F $>$ CH$_3$Br $>$ CH$_3$I.
- Peroxide effect only with HBr. Propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane; with peroxide → 1-bromopropane.
- Chiral: butan-2-ol, 2-bromobutane, 2-chlorobutane. Not chiral: 1-bromobutane, 2-bromopropane.
- Chlorobenzene + NaOH needs 623 K, 300 atm; 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene reacts with warm water.
- Freons and CCl$_4$ deplete ozone; DDT is non-biodegradable, accumulates in fatty tissue; iodoform is an antiseptic.
- Aryl and vinyl halides do not undergo S$_\text{N}$1/S$_\text{N}$2 easily.

## Case-based angles

- Kinetic data: doubling [Nu$^-$] doubles rate → S$_\text{N}$2; no change → S$_\text{N}$1. Predict substrate type.
- Optical rotation of a product after substitution → inversion or racemisation.
- Ozone hole / insecticide bans → freons, CCl$_4$, DDT.
- Synthesis scheme with Grignard reagent → identify products, explain dry conditions.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip
(1) Writing Wurtz–Fittig without ``dry ether''. (2) Using aq. KOH for elimination (alcoholic is needed). (3) Applying the peroxide effect to HCl/HI. (4) Saying S$_\text{N}$1 gives inversion. (5) Missing the minor product in EAS of chlorobenzene (write both o and p; p major). (6) Calling an aryl halide a ``vinylic'' halide. (7) Forgetting that Finkelstein works because NaCl/NaBr precipitates in dry acetone.
:::
