# Ch 6 · Haloalkanes and Haloarenes — formulas and key facts

## Classification, C–X bond, physical properties

- sp$^3$ C–X: alkyl (1$^\circ$, 2$^\circ$, 3$^\circ$), **allylic** (X on C next to C=C), **benzylic** (X on C next to ring). sp$^2$ C–X: **vinylic** (X on C=C), **aryl** (X on ring). Geminal (same C) vs vicinal (adjacent C) dihalides.
- C–X polar (C$^{\delta+}$). Bond length C–F $<$ C–Cl $<$ C–Br $<$ C–I; bond enthalpy reverse. Dipole moment: CH$_3$Cl $>$ CH$_3$F $>$ CH$_3$Br $>$ CH$_3$I.
- b.p.: RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl $>$ RF; rises with size; isomers: more branching → lower b.p. (1-bromobutane $>$ 2-bromobutane $>$ 2-bromo-2-methylpropane). p-Dichlorobenzene has highest m.p. of the three isomers (symmetrical, packs well).
- Only slightly soluble in water: energy to break H-bonds in water is not repaid by new C–X/water attractions. Density rises with mass and number of halogen atoms.

## Preparation

:::formula
$\ce{R-OH + HCl ->[ZnCl2] R-Cl + H2O}$ (3$^\circ$ needs no ZnCl$_2$)   $\ce{3R-OH + PCl3 -> 3R-Cl + H3PO3}$<br>
$\begin{gathered}\ce{R-OH + PCl5}\\ \ce{-> R-Cl + POCl3 + HCl}\end{gathered}$   $\begin{gathered}\ce{R-OH + SOCl2}\\ \ce{-> R-Cl + SO2 ^ + HCl ^}\end{gathered}$ (best: by-products escape)<br>
Alkene + HX: Markovnikov (H to the C with more H): propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane<br>
With peroxide (**HBr only**): anti-Markovnikov → 1-bromopropane<br>
Arenes: $\ce{C6H6 + Cl2 ->[Fe / FeCl3][dark] C6H5Cl + HCl}$ (o/p for substituted rings)
:::

- Halogen exchange: **Finkelstein** (NaI, dry acetone), **Swarts** (AgF, Hg$_2$F$_2$, CoF$_2$, SbF$_3$). Aryl halides from diazonium salts: **Sandmeyer**, **Gattermann**, KI. (Equations in the Named-reaction table.)
- Direct iodination is reversible — needs an oxidant (HNO$_3$, HIO$_4$) to remove HI. Fluorination is too violent.

## Nucleophilic substitution

| **Reagent** | **Product** | **Reagent** | **Product** |
|---|---|---|---|
| aq. KOH/NaOH | alcohol R–OH | KCN (ionic) | nitrile R–C$\equiv$N |
| NaOR$'$ | ether R–O–R$'$ | AgCN (covalent) | isocyanide R–N$\equiv$C |
| NH$_3$ | amines (mixture) | KNO$_2$ | alkyl nitrite R–O–N=O |
| LiAlH$_4$ | alkane R–H | AgNO$_2$ | nitroalkane R–NO$_2$ |
| R$'$COOAg | ester R$'$COOR | NaI | R–I |

| | **S$_\text{N}$2** | **S$_\text{N}$1** |
|---|---|---|
| Steps | one step; backside attack via 5-bonded transition state | two: slow ionisation to carbocation, fast attack |
| Rate law | $k$[RX][Nu$^-$] (2nd order) | $k$[RX] (1st order) |
| Reactivity | CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ (steric hindrance) | 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X (carbocation stability); allylic, benzylic very fast (resonance) |
| Stereochemistry | **inversion** (Walden) | **racemisation** (planar carbocation; attack from both faces) |
| Favoured by | strong nucleophile, aprotic solvent | polar protic solvent (water, alcohol) |

- Leaving-group ability (both): R–I $>$ R–Br $>$ R–Cl $>$ R–F.
- **Ambident nucleophiles**: $\ce{CN-}$ (C or N) and $\ce{NO2-}$ (O or N).
- **Optical activity**: rotates plane-polarised light — dextrorotatory (+, $d$) or laevorotatory ($-$, $l$). **Chiral** carbon: four different groups (butan-2-ol, 2-bromobutane). Enantiomers = non-superimposable mirror images. **Racemic mixture** (±): equal amounts of both, optically inactive (external compensation).
- **Elimination** (β-elimination) with alc. KOH: **Zaitsev rule** — the more substituted alkene is the major product (2-bromobutane → but-2-ene major). 3$^\circ$ halide + strong/bulky base → elimination; 1$^\circ$ + good nucleophile → substitution.
- **Metals**: Grignard reagent $\ce{R-X + Mg ->[dry ether] RMgX}$; reacts with any proton source ($\ce{RMgX + H2O -> RH + Mg(OH)X}$), so it must be kept dry. **Wurtz**: $\ce{2RX + 2Na ->[dry ether] R-R + 2NaX}$.

## Haloarenes

- **Less reactive to nucleophilic substitution**: (i) resonance gives C–X partial double-bond character; (ii) sp$^2$ C holds the bond tighter (shorter, stronger); (iii) phenyl cation not resonance-stabilised (no S$_\text{N}$1); (iv) electron-rich ring repels nucleophile; backside attack impossible.
- Chlorobenzene + NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → phenol (after H$^+$). **–NO$_2$ at o/p speeds it up** (stabilises the carbanion by resonance): 4-nitrochlorobenzene 443 K; 2,4-dinitro 368 K; 2,4,6-trinitro warm water. –NO$_2$ at meta: no such effect.
- **Electrophilic substitution**: halogen is deactivating ($-I$) but **o/p-directing** ($+R$); para product major. Cl$_2$/FeCl$_3$ → 1,2- + 1,4-dichlorobenzene; conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → 1-chloro-2- + 1-chloro-4-nitrobenzene; conc. H$_2$SO$_4$ → chlorobenzenesulphonic acids; CH$_3$Cl/AlCl$_3$ → 2- + 4-chlorotoluene; CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$ → 2- + 4-chloroacetophenone.
- **Wurtz–Fittig**: $\ce{ArX + RX + 2Na -> Ar-R + 2NaX}$; **Fittig**: $\ce{2ArX + 2Na -> Ar-Ar + 2NaX}$ (biphenyl).

## Polyhalogen compounds — uses & effects

| CH$_2$Cl$_2$ | paint remover, propellant, metal cleaning; harms central nervous system, burns skin, blurs vision |
|---|---|
| CHCl$_3$ | solvent for fats, alkaloids, iodine; was an anaesthetic. Light + air → **phosgene**: $\ce{2CHCl3 + O2 ->[light] 2COCl2 + 2HCl}$ → stored in closed dark bottles filled to the brim |
| CHI$_3$ | antiseptic (releases free iodine); replaced because of its smell |
| CCl$_4$ | refrigerants, propellants, solvent; in the stratosphere it depletes the ozone layer |
| Freons (CFCs, CCl$_2$F$_2$ = freon-12) | refrigeration, air conditioning, aerosol propellants; very stable, reach the stratosphere and destroy ozone |
| DDT | first chlorinated insecticide (malaria, typhus); not biodegradable, stored in fatty tissue → banned in many countries |

:::tip
Markovnikov's exception (peroxide effect) works with HBr only. KCN → nitrile but AgCN → isocyanide; KNO$_2$ → nitrite but AgNO$_2$ → nitro. Allyl/benzyl halides react fastest by S$_\text{N}$1 even though primary. Aryl and vinyl halides do not undergo S$_\text{N}$1/S$_\text{N}$2 under normal conditions.
:::

### Orders to know

*write the reason with the order*

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ · S$_\text{N}$1: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X · leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p. (similar mass): carboxylic acid $>$ alcohol $>$ 1$^\circ$ amine $>$ aldehyde/ketone $>$ ether $\approx$ alkane. Amines 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$. Haloalkanes RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl.
- Acidity: RCOOH $>$ phenol $>$ water $>$ alcohol; alcohols 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; picric acid $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ o- $>$ m-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ cresol.
- Carboxylic acids: F– $>$ Cl– $>$ Br–CH$_2$COOH; CCl$_3$ $>$ CHCl$_2$ $>$ CH$_2$ClCOOH; 2-chloro $>$ 3-chloro $>$ 4-chlorobutanoic; HCOOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH; 4-nitrobenzoic $>$ benzoic $>$ 4-methylbenzoic.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$; aliphatic aldehyde $>$ benzaldehyde.
- Basicity (water): (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$; benzylamine $>$ aniline; p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline.
- Reactivity of alcohols with HX / dehydration: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$; HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl.

### Conversions — step chains

*carbon count first*

**Step up one C**: R–X + KCN → RCN (then LiAlH$_4$ → amine, or H$_3$O$^+$ → acid); RMgX + CO$_2$ → RCOOH; RMgX + HCHO → RCH$_2$OH. **Step down one C**: Hoffmann bromamide; decarboxylation. **Double**: Wurtz, Kolbe electrolysis.

- Methanamine → ethanamine: HNO$_2$ → CH$_3$OH → PCl$_5$ → CH$_3$Cl → KCN → CH$_3$CN → LiAlH$_4$.
- Ethanamine → methanamine: HNO$_2$ → C$_2$H$_5$OH → K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$ → CH$_3$COOH → NH$_3$, heat → CH$_3$CONH$_2$ → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Ethanoic acid → methanamine: NH$_3$, heat → acetamide → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Benzene → aniline: conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → Sn/HCl. Aniline → chlorobenzene / benzonitrile / phenol / benzene / fluorobenzene / iodobenzene: diazotise, then CuCl / CuCN / H$_2$O / H$_3$PO$_2$ / HBF$_4$ + heat / KI.
- Aniline → benzoic acid: diazotise → CuCN → H$_3$O$^+$. Aniline → p-bromoaniline: acetylate → Br$_2$ → hydrolyse.
- Propene → propan-1-ol: B$_2$H$_6$, H$_2$O$_2$/OH$^-$. Propene → propan-2-ol: H$_2$O/H$^+$. Propanone → propene: NaBH$_4$ → conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, heat.
- Benzoic acid → benzaldehyde: SOCl$_2$ → Rosenmund. Benzene → acetophenone: CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$. Acetophenone → benzoic acid: NaOI (haloform) → H$^+$.
- Bromomethane → propanone: Mg/ether → CH$_3$MgBr + CH$_3$CN → H$_3$O$^+$.
- Ethanal → but-2-enal: dil. NaOH (aldol), heat. Ethanol → 3-hydroxybutanal: PCC → ethanal → dil. NaOH.
- Phenol → aspirin: Kolbe → salicylic acid → (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O. Phenol → anisole: NaOH → CH$_3$I. Chlorobenzene → phenol: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → H$^+$.
- Ethanol → propanenitrile: PCl$_5$ → KCN. Toluene → benzoic acid: alk. KMnO$_4$ → H$^+$.

### Top "give reason" answers

*one cause, stated in chemistry terms*

- **Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution** — resonance (partial C=X), sp$^2$ C, unstable phenyl cation, electron-rich ring repels Nu$^-$.
- **Grignard reagents are made in dry ether** — they react with water to give alkanes.
- **Chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim** — air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- **Phenol is acidic, ethanol hardly** — phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; ethoxide is not (and is destabilised by +I).
- **o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, p-isomer not** — intramolecular vs intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Alcohols boil higher than ethers of similar mass** — intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Anisole + HI gives phenol + CH$_3$I** — O–C(aryl) has partial double-bond character; S$_\text{N}$2 attack occurs at CH$_3$.
- **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones** — less steric hindrance and less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$.
- **Carboxylic acids do not give typical C=O reactions** — resonance of –COOH reduces the electrophilicity of the C.
- **Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols** — carboxylate has two equivalent resonance forms with charge on O atoms only.
- **Chloroacetic acid stronger than acetic acid** — $-I$ of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion.
- **Aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine/NH$_3$** — lone pair delocalised into the ring.
- **(CH$_3$)$_3$N weaker base than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water** — poorer H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding.
- **Aniline gives meta product on nitration** — in strong acid it forms anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
- **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts** — it forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ deactivates the ring.
- **Acetylation of –NH$_2$ before nitration/bromination** — lowers activation, avoids polysubstitution and oxidation.
- **Gabriel synthesis cannot give aryl amines** — aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide anion.
- **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl ones** — resonance with the ring.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing** — no free anomeric –OH (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are bonded).
- **Amino acids have high m.p. and are water-soluble** — exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
