# Ch 4 · The d- and f-Block Elements — formulas and key facts

## Position & electronic configuration

- d-block: groups 3–12. Series: 3d (Sc–Zn), 4d (Y–Cd), 5d (La, Hf–Hg), 6d (Ac, Rf–Cn). General configuration $(n-1)d^{1-10}\,ns^{1-2}$ (Pd: $4d^{10}5s^0$).
- **Transition element**: has an incompletely filled d subshell in the atom *or* in any of its common oxidation states.
- Zn, Cd, Hg ($d^{10}$ in atom and ions) are *not* regarded as transition metals. Cu (Cu$^{2+}$ $3d^9$), Ag, Au (Au$^{3+}$ $5d^8$) *are*. Sc is ($3d^1$).
- Exceptions: Cr $3d^54s^1$, Cu $3d^{10}4s^1$ (extra stability of half-filled and full d).
- Ions: remove 4s electrons first. Fe$^{2+}$ $3d^6$, Fe$^{3+}$ $3d^5$, Mn$^{2+}$ $3d^5$, Cu$^+$ $3d^{10}$.

## General properties & trends (3d series)

- **Metallic character**: hard, high m.p./b.p., high enthalpy of atomisation — strong metallic bonding from $(n-1)d$ + $ns$ electrons. Maximum near the middle (most unpaired d electrons). Mn and Zn lower; Hg is liquid. 4d/5d metals have higher $\Delta_aH$ than 3d (frequent metal–metal bonding in heavier metals).
- **Radii**: decrease across the series, then stay nearly constant (added d electrons shield poorly). 4d and 5d radii almost equal (Zr 160 pm, Hf 159 pm) — lanthanoid contraction. Density rises Ti → Cu.
- **Ionisation enthalpy**: rises gradually and irregularly. IE$_2$ high for Cr and Cu (would break $d^5$/$d^{10}$); IE$_3$ high for Mn and Zn (from $d^5$/$d^{10}$).
- **Oxidation states**: variable because $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ energies are close; differ by one unit (Fe$^{2+}$/Fe$^{3+}$). Max OS rises to Mn (+7) and then falls. Mn shows the most OS. Highest OS appear in oxides/oxoanions ($\ce{MnO4-}$, $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$) and fluorides; low/zero OS with $\pi$-acceptor ligands ($\ce{Ni(CO)4}$, $\ce{Fe(CO)5}$). Heavier members: higher OS more stable (Mo(VI), W(VI) vs oxidising Cr(VI)).
- Highest Mn fluoride is MnF$_4$ but highest oxide Mn$_2$O$_7$ (+7): O forms multiple bonds with the metal.
- **Oxides**: acidic character rises with OS. MnO basic, MnO$_2$ amphoteric, Mn$_2$O$_7$ acidic; CrO basic, Cr$_2$O$_3$ amphoteric, CrO$_3$ acidic; V$_2$O$_5$ amphoteric (mainly acidic).

### Standard electrode potentials (V)

| | Ti | V | Cr | Mn | Fe | Co | Ni | Cu | Zn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $E^\circ_{\ce{M^2+/M}}$ | $-1.63$ | $-1.18$ | $-0.90$ | $-1.18$ | $-0.44$ | $-0.28$ | $-0.25$ | $+0.34$ | $-0.76$ |
| $E^\circ_{\ce{M^3+/M^2+}}$ | $-0.37$ | $-0.26$ | $-0.41$ | $+1.57$ | $+0.77$ | $+1.97$ | – | – | – |

- Cu alone has positive $E^\circ_{\ce{M^2+/M}}$: high $\Delta_aH$ + IE not balanced by $\Delta_\text{hyd}H$ → Cu does not liberate H$_2$ from acids.
- Mn, Zn more negative than the trend: stable Mn$^{2+}$ ($d^5$), Zn$^{2+}$ ($d^{10}$). Ni: very negative $\Delta_\text{hyd}H$.
- $E^\circ_{\ce{Mn^3+/Mn^2+}}$ high → Mn$^{3+}$ strong oxidant ($d^5$ Mn$^{2+}$ favoured). $E^\circ_{\ce{Cr^3+/Cr^2+}}$ negative → Cr$^{2+}$ strong reductant (Cr$^{3+}$ $t_{2g}^3$ stable). Fe$^{3+}$/Fe$^{2+}$ low → Fe$^{3+}$ ($d^5$) stable. Co$^{3+}$ unstable in water.

### Magnetic, colour, complexes, catalysis, interstitials, alloys

:::formula
Spin-only moment: $\mu=\sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM   ($n$ = unpaired electrons)<br>
$n=1$: 1.73 · 2: 2.83 · 3: 3.87 · 4: 4.90 · 5: 5.92 BM
:::

- Paramagnetic: unpaired electrons; diamagnetic: all paired ($d^0$, $d^{10}$).
- **Colour**: d–d transition; ion absorbs part of visible light, we see the complementary colour. $d^0$ (Sc$^{3+}$, Ti$^{4+}$) and $d^{10}$ (Zn$^{2+}$, Cu$^+$) are colourless. (Purple $\ce{MnO4-}$ and orange $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ are $d^0$: colour from charge transfer.)
- **Complexes**: small size, high ionic charge, vacant d orbitals of suitable energy.
- **Catalysis**: variable OS and ability to form complexes / adsorb reactants on the surface. V$_2$O$_5$ (Contact process), finely divided Fe (Haber), Ni (hydrogenation). Fe$^{3+}$ catalyses $\ce{S2O8^2-}$/$\ce{I-}$: $\ce{2Fe^3+ + 2I- -> 2Fe^2+ + I2}$; $\ce{2Fe^2+ + S2O8^2- -> 2Fe^3+ + 2SO4^2-}$.
- **Interstitial compounds**: small H, C, N, B trapped in metal lattice (TiC, Mn$_4$N, Fe$_3$H, TiH$_{1.7}$) — non-stoichiometric, very hard, high m.p., keep metallic conductivity, chemically inert.
- **Alloys**: similar atomic radii → atoms substitute in the lattice (brass Cu–Zn, bronze Cu–Sn, steels).

## Potassium dichromate, K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$

:::formula
From chromite: $\ce{4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3 + 7O2 -> 8Na2CrO4 + 2Fe2O3 + 8CO2}$<br>
$\ce{2Na2CrO4 + 2H+ -> Na2Cr2O7 + 2Na+ + H2O}$;   $\ce{Na2Cr2O7 + 2KCl -> K2Cr2O7 + 2NaCl}$<br>
pH switch: $\ce{2CrO4^2- + 2H+ <=> Cr2O7^2- + H2O}$ (yellow $\rightleftharpoons$ orange; base reverses it)<br>
Oxidant in acid: $\ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6e- -> 2Cr^3+ + 7H2O}$ ($E^\circ=1.33$ V)
:::

- $\ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6I- -> 2Cr^3+ + 3I2 + 7H2O}$
- $\ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6Fe^2+ -> 2Cr^3+ + 6Fe^3+ + 7H2O}$
- $\ce{Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 3Sn^2+ -> 2Cr^3+ + 3Sn^4+ + 7H2O}$
- $\ce{Cr2O7^2- + 8H+ + 3H2S -> 2Cr^3+ + 3S + 7H2O}$
- Structure: chromate tetrahedral; dichromate = two tetrahedra sharing one O (Cr–O–Cr $\approx126^\circ$). Na$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$ is more soluble; K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$ is used as a primary standard in volumetric analysis.

## Potassium permanganate, KMnO$_4$

:::formula
$\ce{2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2 -> 2K2MnO4 + 2H2O}$ (fusion);   $\ce{3MnO4^2- + 4H+ -> 2MnO4- + MnO2 + 2H2O}$<br>
Commercial: alkaline manganate oxidised electrolytically $\ce{MnO4^2- -> MnO4- + e-}$<br>
Lab: $\ce{2Mn^2+ + 5S2O8^2- + 8H2O -> 2MnO4- + 10SO4^2- + 16H+}$<br>
Acid: $\ce{MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- -> Mn^2+ + 4H2O}$ ($E^\circ=1.52$ V)<br>
Neutral/faintly alkaline: $\ce{MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- -> MnO2 + 4OH-}$
:::

**Acidic:** $\ce{10I- + 2MnO4- + 16H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 5I2 + 8H2O}$ · $\ce{5Fe^2+ + MnO4- + 8H+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O}$ · $\ce{5C2O4^2- + 2MnO4- + 16H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O}$ · $\ce{5S^2- + 2MnO4- + 16H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 5S + 8H2O}$ · $\ce{5SO3^2- + 2MnO4- + 6H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 5SO4^2- + 3H2O}$ · $\ce{5NO2- + 2MnO4- + 6H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 5NO3- + 3H2O}$<br>
**Neutral/faintly alkaline:** $\ce{2MnO4- + H2O + I- -> 2MnO2 + 2OH- + IO3-}$ · $\ce{8MnO4- + 3S2O3^2- + H2O -> 8MnO2 + 6SO4^2- + 2OH-}$ · $\ce{2MnO4- + 3Mn^2+ + 2H2O -> 5MnO2 + 4H+}$

- Manganate $\ce{MnO4^2-}$: green, paramagnetic (1 unpaired). Permanganate $\ce{MnO4-}$: purple, diamagnetic. Both tetrahedral with $\pi$-bonding.
- Uses: titrations (self-indicator — pale pink end point), bleaching wool, cotton, silk; decolourising oils. In titrations use dilute H$_2$SO$_4$, not HCl (HCl is oxidised to Cl$_2$).

:::tip
Balance redox quickly: $\ce{MnO4-}$ takes 5 e$^-$ (acid), 3 e$^-$ (neutral); $\ce{Cr2O7^2-}$ takes 6 e$^-$. Electrons lost by the reductant: $\ce{Fe^2+}$ 1, $\ce{C2O4^2-}$ 2, $\ce{I-}$ 1, $\ce{S^2-}$ 2, $\ce{Sn^2+}$ 2, $\ce{SO3^2-}$ 2, $\ce{NO2-}$ 2. Cross-multiply, then add H$^+$/H$_2$O.
:::

## Lanthanoids (Ce 58 – Lu 71) & actinoids (Th 90 – Lr 103)

- Lanthanoids $[\text{Xe}]4f^{1-14}5d^{0-1}6s^2$. Common OS **+3**. Ce$^{4+}$ ($4f^0$, strong oxidant, $E^\circ=+1.74$ V), Tb$^{4+}$ ($4f^7$) oxidant; Eu$^{2+}$ ($4f^7$), Yb$^{2+}$ ($4f^{14}$) reductants.
- **Lanthanoid contraction**: steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii from La to Lu, due to imperfect shielding of one 4f electron by another as nuclear charge rises.
- Consequences: (i) 4d and 5d radii nearly equal — Zr/Hf, Nb/Ta, Mo/W very similar, occur together, hard to separate; (ii) basicity of Ln(OH)$_3$ decreases La → Lu; (iii) lanthanoids themselves are hard to separate.
- Reactions: burn in O$_2$ → Ln$_2$O$_3$; with C → carbides; with H$_2$ on heating; liberate H$_2$ from dilute acids; Ln(OH)$_3$ basic. Colour from f–f transitions (La$^{3+}$, Lu$^{3+}$ colourless). **Mischmetall**: $\approx$95% lanthanoid + $\approx$5% Fe + traces S, C, Ca, Al — Mg alloy for bullets, shells, lighter flint.
- Actinoids $[\text{Rn}]5f^{1-14}6d^{0-1}7s^2$: all radioactive; wider range of OS (U up to +6, Np/Pu up to +7) because 5f, 6d, 7s are close in energy; actinoid contraction greater (5f shields even more poorly).

| | **Lanthanoids** | **Actinoids** |
|---|---|---|
| Orbital filled | 4f | 5f (penetrate less, more exposed — take part in bonding) |
| Oxidation states | mostly +3 (few +2, +4) | +3 plus many higher (+4 to +7) |
| Radioactivity | only Pm | all |
| Contraction | smaller | larger, element to element |
| Complexes, oxocations | weaker tendency; no oxocations | greater tendency; $\ce{UO2^2+}$, $\ce{PuO2^2+}$ |
| Magnetism | easier to explain | complex |

### Applications (NCERT list)

Fe & steels (construction); TiO$_2$ pigment; MnO$_2$ in dry cells; Zn and Ni/Cd in batteries; Cu, Ag, Au coinage metals; V$_2$O$_5$ in Contact process; TiCl$_4$ + Al(CH$_3$)$_3$ (Ziegler catalyst, polythene); Fe catalyst (Haber); Ni (hydrogenation of oils); PdCl$_2$ (ethene → ethanal, Wacker); AgBr in photography.

## Exceptions & ``give reason'' — one-line answers

| **Fact** | **Reason** |
|---|---|
| Zn, Cd, Hg not transition metals | full $d^{10}$ in atom and common ions |
| Cu, Ag, Au are transition metals | Cu$^{2+}$ $3d^9$, Au$^{3+}$ $5d^8$ (incomplete d in an OS) |
| Variable oxidation states | $(n-1)d$ and $ns$ electrons of similar energy both used |
| Mn shows the most OS | $3d^54s^2$: all 7 electrons can be used |
| Zn lowest m.p./$\Delta_aH$ in series | no unpaired d electrons for metallic bonding |
| Mn has lower m.p. than Cr | stable $d^5$ holds its d electrons; weaker metallic bonding |
| Higher $\Delta_aH$ in 4d/5d series | larger d orbitals, stronger metal–metal bonds |
| Zr and Hf have almost the same radius | lanthanoid contraction |
| Cu$^+$ disproportionates in water | $\ce{2Cu+ -> Cu^2+ + Cu}$; much larger $\Delta_\text{hyd}H$ of Cu$^{2+}$ outweighs IE$_2$ |
| Cr$^{2+}$ reducing, Mn$^{3+}$ oxidising (both $d^4$) | Cr$^{2+}$ → Cr$^{3+}$ ($t_{2g}^3$, stable); Mn$^{3+}$ → Mn$^{2+}$ ($d^5$, stable) |
| Fe$^{3+}$ more stable than Fe$^{2+}$ | Fe$^{3+}$ is $d^5$ (half-filled) |
| Sc$^{3+}$, Ti$^{4+}$, Zn$^{2+}$ colourless | $d^0$ / $d^{10}$: no d–d transition |
| Transition-metal compounds mostly coloured | d–d transitions in visible region |
| Most transition-metal ions paramagnetic | unpaired d electrons |
| Highest OS in oxides/fluorides (Mn$_2$O$_7$) | O and F are small, highly electronegative; O forms multiple bonds |
| Co(II) oxidised to Co(III) easily in complexes | large CFSE of $d^6$ Co(III) with strong-field ligands |
| Ce$^{4+}$ oxidant; Eu$^{2+}$, Yb$^{2+}$ reductants | tend to reach common +3 state (Ce$^{4+}$ is $4f^0$; Eu$^{2+}$ $4f^7$) |
| Actinoids show more OS than lanthanoids | 5f, 6d, 7s have comparable energies |
| Actinoid contraction $>$ lanthanoid | 5f electrons shield more poorly than 4f |
| Transition metals and compounds are good catalysts | variable OS; form complexes; large surface for adsorption |
