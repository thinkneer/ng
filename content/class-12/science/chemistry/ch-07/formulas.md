# Ch 7 · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers — formulas and key facts

## Structure & preparation of alcohols

- C–O–H angle in methanol 108.9$^\circ$ (lone-pair repulsion). Phenol C–O shorter (136 pm): partial double bond from resonance; sp$^2$ C. Ether C–O–C 111.7$^\circ$ (bulky R groups).

:::formula
**Acid-catalysed hydration** (Markovnikov): $\begin{gathered}\ce{CH3CH=CH2 + H2O}\\ \ce{->[H+] CH3CH(OH)CH3}\end{gathered}$<br>
**Hydroboration–oxidation** (anti-Markovnikov, high yield): propene $\xrightarrow{(\text{BH}_3)_2}\;\xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O}_2/\text{OH}^-}$ propan-1-ol<br>
**Reduction**: aldehyde → 1$^\circ$, ketone → 2$^\circ$ (H$_2$/Pd, NaBH$_4$, LiAlH$_4$); acid → 1$^\circ$ (LiAlH$_4$); commercially via esters (H$_2$/catalyst)<br>
**Grignard**: HCHO → 1$^\circ$; other aldehydes → 2$^\circ$; ketones → 3$^\circ$ (adduct + H$_2$O)
:::

- Hydration mechanism: (1) alkene protonated by H$_3$O$^+$ → carbocation; (2) water attacks carbocation; (3) loss of H$^+$ → alcohol.

## Preparation of phenols

- Chlorobenzene + NaOH (623 K, 300 atm) → sodium phenoxide → H$^+$ → phenol.
- Benzenesulphonic acid + fused NaOH → sodium phenoxide → H$^+$ → phenol.
- Diazonium salt + warm water: $\begin{gathered}\ce{C6H5N2+Cl- + H2O}\\ \ce{-> C6H5OH + N2 + HCl}\end{gathered}$.
- **Cumene** (isopropylbenzene) + O$_2$ → cumene hydroperoxide → H$^+$/H$_2$O → phenol + **acetone** (by-product).

## Physical properties & acidity

- b.p.: alcohols/phenols $\gg$ hydrocarbons, ethers, haloalkanes of similar mass (intermolecular H-bonding). b.p. rises with C atoms, falls with branching. Solubility in water (H-bonds with water) falls as the alkyl group grows.
- Alcohols: $\ce{2R-OH + 2Na -> 2R-ONa + H2}$. Acidity 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ (+I of alkyl groups). Alcohols are *weaker* acids than water: $\ce{RO- + H2O -> ROH + OH-}$.
- **Phenol more acidic than alcohols**: phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised (negative charge spread over ring); phenol's O on sp$^2$ C. Phenol reacts with NaOH; neither phenol nor alcohol reacts with NaHCO$_3$.
- –NO$_2$ (EWG) at o/p raises acidity; –CH$_3$ (EDG) lowers it. pK$_a$: 2,4,6-trinitrophenol 0.5 $<$ p-nitrophenol 7.1 $<$ o-nitrophenol 7.2 $<$ m-nitrophenol 8.3 $<$ phenol 10.0 $<$ cresols $\approx$10.2 $<$ ethanol 15.9.

## Reactions of alcohols

:::formula
**Esterification**: $\ce{ROH + R{'}COOH <=>[H+] R{'}COOR + H2O}$; with R$'$COCl + pyridine (removes HCl)<br>
**Aspirin**: salicylic acid + (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O → acetylsalicylic acid<br>
**Dehydration**: ethanol $\xrightarrow{\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\;443\text{ K}}$ ethene; 2$^\circ$: 85% H$_3$PO$_4$, 440 K; 3$^\circ$: 20% H$_3$PO$_4$, 358 K. Ease 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$<br>
**Oxidation**: 1$^\circ$ → aldehyde → acid (acidified KMnO$_4$/K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$); stop at aldehyde with **PCC** or CrO$_3$ (anhydrous); 2$^\circ$ → ketone (CrO$_3$); 3$^\circ$ resist<br>
**Cu, 573 K**: 1$^\circ$ → aldehyde, 2$^\circ$ → ketone, 3$^\circ$ → alkene (dehydration)
:::

- With HX/PCl$_3$/PCl$_5$/SOCl$_2$ → R–X. **Lucas test** (conc. HCl + ZnCl$_2$): 3$^\circ$ turbid at once; 2$^\circ$ within minutes; 1$^\circ$ no turbidity at room temperature.
- **Dehydration mechanism**: (1) protonation of –OH (fast); (2) loss of H$_2$O → carbocation (slow, rate-determining); (3) loss of H$^+$ from β-C → alkene.

## Reactions of phenol (–OH activates, o/p)

- **Nitration**: dilute HNO$_3$, 298 K → o- + p-nitrophenol; separated by steam distillation (o-isomer steam-volatile: intramolecular H-bond; p-isomer: intermolecular H-bond). Conc. HNO$_3$ → 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid).
- **Bromination**: Br$_2$ in CS$_2$/CHCl$_3$, low $T$ → o- + p-bromophenol; **bromine water** → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt).
- **Kolbe's reaction** → salicylic acid; **Reimer–Tiemann** → salicylaldehyde (table). Zn dust → benzene. Chromic acid → benzoquinone.

## Commercially important alcohols

- **Methanol** (wood spirit): $\begin{gathered}\ce{CO + 2H2}\\ \ce{->[ZnO-Cr2O3] CH3OH}\\ \text{(200–300 atm, 573–673 K)}\end{gathered}$. Very poisonous: small amounts cause blindness, larger amounts death. Solvent for paints, varnishes; making HCHO.
- **Ethanol**: fermentation of molasses/sugarcane/fruit sugars by yeast enzymes — invertase (sucrose → glucose + fructose), zymase (→ ethanol + CO$_2$), without air. Commercial alcohol is *denatured* with CuSO$_4$ (colour) and pyridine (smell).

## Ethers

- **From alcohols**: conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, 413 K (excess alcohol) → symmetrical ether. S$_\text{N}$2 mechanism: protonated alcohol attacked by second alcohol molecule. Good only for 1$^\circ$ alcohols (2$^\circ$/3$^\circ$ give alkenes).
- **Williamson synthesis**: $\ce{R-X + R{'}-ONa -> R-O-R{'} + NaX}$ (S$_\text{N}$2). Use a **1$^\circ$ halide** with the bulky alkoxide: $\ce{(CH3)3C-ONa + CH3Br}$ → tert-butyl methyl ether; but $\ce{(CH3)3C-Br + CH3ONa}$ → 2-methylpropene (elimination). Phenoxide + RX → aryl alkyl ether.
- b.p. of ethers $\approx$ alkanes (no H-bonding between molecules); miscibility with water $\approx$ alcohols (H-bond with water).
- **Cleavage by HX** (HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl): $\ce{R-O-R + HX -> RX + ROH}$. Mixed 1$^\circ$/2$^\circ$: X goes to the smaller group (S$_\text{N}$2). With a 3$^\circ$ group: X goes to the 3$^\circ$ C (S$_\text{N}$1): $\begin{gathered}\ce{CH3-O-C(CH3)3 + HI}\\ \ce{-> CH3OH + (CH3)3C-I}\end{gathered}$. **Anisole** + HI → phenol + CH$_3$I (O–aryl bond stronger; no phenyl cation).
- Anisole (–OCH$_3$ activating, o/p): Br$_2$ in ethanoic acid → p-bromoanisole (major); CH$_3$Cl/AlCl$_3$ → 2- + 4-methoxytoluene; CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$ → 2- + 4-methoxyacetophenone; HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → 2- + 4-nitroanisole.

:::tip
Phenol + bromine water gives the *tribromo* product even without a catalyst; in CS$_2$ at low temperature it stops at mono. Never use a 3$^\circ$ alkyl halide in Williamson synthesis. ``Anisole + HI'' never gives iodobenzene.
:::

### Orders to know

*write the reason with the order*

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ · S$_\text{N}$1: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X · leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p. (similar mass): carboxylic acid $>$ alcohol $>$ 1$^\circ$ amine $>$ aldehyde/ketone $>$ ether $\approx$ alkane. Amines 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$. Haloalkanes RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl.
- Acidity: RCOOH $>$ phenol $>$ water $>$ alcohol; alcohols 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; picric acid $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ o- $>$ m-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ cresol.
- Carboxylic acids: F– $>$ Cl– $>$ Br–CH$_2$COOH; CCl$_3$ $>$ CHCl$_2$ $>$ CH$_2$ClCOOH; 2-chloro $>$ 3-chloro $>$ 4-chlorobutanoic; HCOOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH; 4-nitrobenzoic $>$ benzoic $>$ 4-methylbenzoic.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$; aliphatic aldehyde $>$ benzaldehyde.
- Basicity (water): (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$; benzylamine $>$ aniline; p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline.
- Reactivity of alcohols with HX / dehydration: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$; HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl.

### Conversions — step chains

*carbon count first*

**Step up one C**: R–X + KCN → RCN (then LiAlH$_4$ → amine, or H$_3$O$^+$ → acid); RMgX + CO$_2$ → RCOOH; RMgX + HCHO → RCH$_2$OH. **Step down one C**: Hoffmann bromamide; decarboxylation. **Double**: Wurtz, Kolbe electrolysis.

- Methanamine → ethanamine: HNO$_2$ → CH$_3$OH → PCl$_5$ → CH$_3$Cl → KCN → CH$_3$CN → LiAlH$_4$.
- Ethanamine → methanamine: HNO$_2$ → C$_2$H$_5$OH → K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$ → CH$_3$COOH → NH$_3$, heat → CH$_3$CONH$_2$ → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Ethanoic acid → methanamine: NH$_3$, heat → acetamide → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Benzene → aniline: conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → Sn/HCl. Aniline → chlorobenzene / benzonitrile / phenol / benzene / fluorobenzene / iodobenzene: diazotise, then CuCl / CuCN / H$_2$O / H$_3$PO$_2$ / HBF$_4$ + heat / KI.
- Aniline → benzoic acid: diazotise → CuCN → H$_3$O$^+$. Aniline → p-bromoaniline: acetylate → Br$_2$ → hydrolyse.
- Propene → propan-1-ol: B$_2$H$_6$, H$_2$O$_2$/OH$^-$. Propene → propan-2-ol: H$_2$O/H$^+$. Propanone → propene: NaBH$_4$ → conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, heat.
- Benzoic acid → benzaldehyde: SOCl$_2$ → Rosenmund. Benzene → acetophenone: CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$. Acetophenone → benzoic acid: NaOI (haloform) → H$^+$.
- Bromomethane → propanone: Mg/ether → CH$_3$MgBr + CH$_3$CN → H$_3$O$^+$.
- Ethanal → but-2-enal: dil. NaOH (aldol), heat. Ethanol → 3-hydroxybutanal: PCC → ethanal → dil. NaOH.
- Phenol → aspirin: Kolbe → salicylic acid → (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O. Phenol → anisole: NaOH → CH$_3$I. Chlorobenzene → phenol: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → H$^+$.
- Ethanol → propanenitrile: PCl$_5$ → KCN. Toluene → benzoic acid: alk. KMnO$_4$ → H$^+$.

### Top "give reason" answers

*one cause, stated in chemistry terms*

- **Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution** — resonance (partial C=X), sp$^2$ C, unstable phenyl cation, electron-rich ring repels Nu$^-$.
- **Grignard reagents are made in dry ether** — they react with water to give alkanes.
- **Chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim** — air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- **Phenol is acidic, ethanol hardly** — phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; ethoxide is not (and is destabilised by +I).
- **o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, p-isomer not** — intramolecular vs intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Alcohols boil higher than ethers of similar mass** — intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Anisole + HI gives phenol + CH$_3$I** — O–C(aryl) has partial double-bond character; S$_\text{N}$2 attack occurs at CH$_3$.
- **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones** — less steric hindrance and less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$.
- **Carboxylic acids do not give typical C=O reactions** — resonance of –COOH reduces the electrophilicity of the C.
- **Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols** — carboxylate has two equivalent resonance forms with charge on O atoms only.
- **Chloroacetic acid stronger than acetic acid** — $-I$ of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion.
- **Aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine/NH$_3$** — lone pair delocalised into the ring.
- **(CH$_3$)$_3$N weaker base than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water** — poorer H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding.
- **Aniline gives meta product on nitration** — in strong acid it forms anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
- **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts** — it forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ deactivates the ring.
- **Acetylation of –NH$_2$ before nitration/bromination** — lowers activation, avoids polysubstitution and oxidation.
- **Gabriel synthesis cannot give aryl amines** — aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide anion.
- **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl ones** — resonance with the ring.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing** — no free anomeric –OH (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are bonded).
- **Amino acids have high m.p. and are water-soluble** — exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
