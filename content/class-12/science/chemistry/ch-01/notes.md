## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Raoult's law** (volatile liquids): the partial vapour pressure of each component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the solution.
- For a non-volatile solute: the **relative lowering of vapour pressure** equals the mole fraction of the solute.
- **Henry's law**: the partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase is proportional to its mole fraction in the solution, $p=K_Hx$.
- **Ideal solution**: obeys Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration; $\Delta_\text{mix}H=0$, $\Delta_\text{mix}V=0$.
- **Azeotropes**: binary mixtures with the same composition in liquid and vapour phase that boil at a constant temperature.
- **Colligative properties**: depend on the number of solute particles, not on their nature (RLVP, $\Delta T_b$, $\Delta T_f$, $\pi$).
- **Osmotic pressure**: the extra pressure that must be applied to the solution to stop osmosis (flow of solvent into it through a semipermeable membrane).
- **Reverse osmosis**: applying pressure larger than $\pi$ makes pure solvent flow out of the solution through the membrane.
- **$K_b$ / $K_f$** (molal elevation / depression constant): elevation in b.p. / depression in f.p. for a 1 molal solution.
- **Abnormal molar mass**: experimental molar mass higher or lower than the expected value, due to association or dissociation of solute.

## Key formulas

:::formula
$\dfrac{p_1^\circ-p_1}{p_1^\circ}=i\,x_2$   $\Delta T_b=iK_bm$   $\Delta T_f=iK_fm$   $\pi=iCRT$<br>
$M_2=\dfrac{1000\,K\,w_2}{\Delta T\,w_1}$   $M_2=\dfrac{w_2RT}{\pi V}$<br>
$\alpha_\text{diss}=\dfrac{i-1}{n-1}$   $\alpha_\text{assoc}=\dfrac{1-i}{1-1/n}$<br>
$p_\text{total}=x_1p_1^\circ+x_2p_2^\circ$   $y_1=\dfrac{x_1p_1^\circ}{p_\text{total}}$   $p=K_Hx$
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton N · New freezing point of an electrolyte solution (3 marks)
*e.g. 2 g NaCl (58.5) in 100 g water, $K_f=1.86$.*<br>
Given: $w_2=2$ g, $w_1=100$ g, $M_2=58.5$, $K_f=1.86$ K kg mol$^{-1}$.<br>
NaCl → Na$^+$ + Cl$^-$; assuming complete dissociation, $i=2$. [½]<br>
$m=\dfrac{2\times1000}{58.5\times100}=0.342$ mol kg$^{-1}$ [½]<br>
$\Delta T_f=iK_fm=2\times1.86\times0.342=1.27$ K [1]<br>
$T_f=273.15-1.27=\boxed{271.88\text{ K}}$ (or $-1.27^\circ$C) [1]
:::

:::skeleton N · Degree of dissociation from observed data
$i=\dfrac{\Delta T_\text{observed}}{\Delta T_\text{calculated (no dissociation)}}$ or $\dfrac{M_\text{calculated}}{M_\text{observed}}$ → $\alpha=\dfrac{i-1}{n-1}$ → state as %.<br>
Check: dissociation gives $i>1$; association gives $i<1$.
:::

:::skeleton N · Osmotic pressure
Moles $n_2=w_2/M_2$; $V$ in L; $T$ in K; $R=0.083$ L bar K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$ (answer in bar) or $0.0821$ (atm).<br>
$\pi=\dfrac{n_2RT}{V}$ → box with unit. For $M_2$: $M_2=\dfrac{w_2RT}{\pi V}$.
:::

:::skeleton Df+Cmp · Deviation question (3 marks)
Definition (Raoult) → positive: A–B weaker, $p$ higher, $\Delta_\text{mix}H>0$, $\Delta_\text{mix}V>0$, e.g. ethanol + acetone, min-boiling azeotrope → negative: A–B stronger, $p$ lower, both $<0$, e.g. chloroform + acetone, max-boiling azeotrope. A small $p$–$x$ sketch earns credit if asked.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Aquatic animals are more comfortable in cold water.**<br>→ $K_H$ of O$_2$ increases with $T$, so solubility of O$_2$ falls with rise in $T$; cold water holds more dissolved O$_2$.
2. **Osmotic pressure is preferred for macromolecules.**<br>→ Measured at room $T$ (proteins denature on heating); uses molarity; $\pi$ is large enough to measure even for very dilute solutions, while $\Delta T_f$, $\Delta T_b$ would be too small.
3. **Chloroform + acetone shows negative deviation.**<br>→ H-bond forms between H of CHCl$_3$ and O of acetone; A–B attraction is stronger, escaping tendency and $p$ fall; $\Delta_\text{mix}H<0$.
4. **Ethanol + acetone shows positive deviation.**<br>→ Acetone molecules come between ethanol molecules and break some of their H-bonds; weaker A–B forces; $p$ rises.
5. **Ethanoic acid in benzene: molar mass $\approx$ 120.**<br>→ It dimerises through H-bonding, halving the number of particles ($i\approx0.5$); colligative property halves, so calculated molar mass doubles.
6. **Molality is preferred to molarity.**<br>→ Molality uses mass of solvent, which does not change with $T$; molarity uses volume, which does.
7. **0.1 m NaCl boils higher than 0.1 m glucose.**<br>→ NaCl gives two ions ($i=2$); glucose does not dissociate ($i=1$); $\Delta T_b\propto i$.
8. **Salt is spread on icy roads.**<br>→ It lowers the freezing point of water, so ice melts at lower temperature.
9. **Blood cells swell in water / shrink in 1.5% NaCl.**<br>→ Water is hypotonic: water enters cells by osmosis. 1.5% NaCl is hypertonic: water leaves cells.
10. **Azeotropes cannot be separated by fractional distillation.**<br>→ Liquid and vapour have the same composition at the azeotropic point, so distillation cannot enrich either component.
11. **Vapour pressure falls when a non-volatile solute is added.**<br>→ Solute particles occupy part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape per unit time.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Larger $K_H$ → lower solubility. Gas solubility falls as $T$ rises.
- $T$-independent: molality, mole fraction, mass %, ppm. $T$-dependent: molarity.
- Positive deviation ↔ **minimum**-boiling azeotrope; negative ↔ **maximum**-boiling.
- Ideal: $\Delta_\text{mix}H=0$, $\Delta_\text{mix}V=0$ (but $\Delta_\text{mix}S>0$, $\Delta_\text{mix}G<0$).
- Same molality: lowest f.p./highest b.p./highest $\pi$ ↔ largest $i$. Al$_2$(SO$_4$)$_3$ (5) > CaCl$_2$ (3) > NaCl (2) > glucose (1).
- $K_b$, $K_f$ depend only on the solvent; unit K kg mol$^{-1}$.
- Normal saline: 0.9% (m/V) NaCl, isotonic with blood. RO membrane: cellulose acetate.
- Raoult's law is a special case of Henry's law ($K_H=p_1^\circ$).
- Vapour above an ideal mixture is richer in the more volatile component.

## Case-based angles

- Scuba diving / mountaineering → Henry's law, bends, anoxia, He in tanks.
- Desalination plant → reverse osmosis, membrane, pressure $>\pi$.
- Hospital IV drip / saline → isotonic, what happens if hypertonic.
- Car radiator antifreeze (ethylene glycol), salting roads → $\Delta T_f$ numerical.
- Data table of $\Delta T_f$ for electrolytes → compute $i$, compare.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip
(1) Using mass of *solution* instead of solvent in molality. (2) Forgetting $i$ for NaCl, CaCl$_2$, K$_2$SO$_4$. (3) Giving $\Delta T_f$ when the question asks for the freezing point. (4) $V$ in mL or $T$ in $^\circ$C in $\pi=CRT$. (5) Swapping min-/max-boiling azeotropes. (6) Writing $p=x_2p^\circ$ for a non-volatile solute (correct: $p=x_1p_1^\circ$). (7) Saying ``higher $K_H$ means more soluble''.
:::
