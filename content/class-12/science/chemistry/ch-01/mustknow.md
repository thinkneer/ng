# Ch 1 · Solutions — must know

1. ★★★ Find molar mass of solute (or new f.p./b.p.) from $\Delta T_f$ or $\Delta T_b$; often an electrolyte with $i$. [3]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Raoult's law (volatile liquids):** partial vapour pressure of each component is proportional to its mole fraction.
- **Raoult's law (non-volatile solute):** relative lowering of vapour pressure equals the mole fraction of solute.
- **Henry's law:** partial pressure of a gas above a solution is proportional to its mole fraction, $p=K_Hx$.
- **Ideal solution:** obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations; $\Delta_\text{mix}H=0$ and $\Delta_\text{mix}V=0$.
- **Positive deviation:** A–B attractions weaker than A–A and B–B; vapour pressure above Raoult's value.
- **Negative deviation:** A–B attractions stronger than A–A and B–B; vapour pressure below Raoult's value.
- **Azeotrope:** binary mixture with the same composition in liquid and vapour; boils at constant temperature.
- **Colligative property:** depends on the number of solute particles, not on their nature.
- **Four colligative properties:** relative lowering of vapour pressure, $\Delta T_b$, $\Delta T_f$, osmotic pressure.
- **Osmotic pressure:** extra pressure applied on the solution that just stops osmosis.
- **Reverse osmosis:** pressure greater than $\pi$ pushes pure solvent out of the solution through the membrane.
- **Isotonic solutions:** solutions with the same osmotic pressure at the same temperature.
- **$K_b$ (molal elevation constant):** elevation in boiling point of a 1 molal solution.
- **$K_f$ (molal depression constant):** depression in freezing point of a 1 molal solution.
- **Abnormal molar mass:** measured molar mass differs from the expected value because solute associates or dissociates.
- **van't Hoff factor $i$:** observed colligative property divided by the calculated value.

## Key formulas

:::formula Relative lowering of vapour pressure
$\dfrac{p_1^\circ-p_1}{p_1^\circ}=i\,x_2$<br>
Use for a non-volatile solute. A ratio, so no unit. Slip: $x_2$ is the mole fraction of the *solute*.
:::

:::formula Elevation of boiling point
$\Delta T_b=iK_bm$<br>
$m$ = molality (mol per kg of solvent); $K_b$ in K kg mol$^{-1}$. New boiling point $=T_b^\circ+\Delta T_b$.
:::

:::formula Depression of freezing point
$\Delta T_f=iK_fm$<br>
$K_f$ in K kg mol$^{-1}$. Slip: the freezing point asked for is $T_f^\circ-\Delta T_f$, not $\Delta T_f$.
:::

:::formula Osmotic pressure
$\pi=iCRT$<br>
$C$ in mol L$^{-1}$, $T$ in K, $R=0.0821$ L atm K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$ (0.083 with bar). Best method for polymers and proteins.
:::

:::formula Molar mass from boiling or freezing point
$M_2=\dfrac{1000\,K\,w_2}{\Delta T\,w_1}$<br>
$w_2$ solute and $w_1$ solvent, both in g; $K=K_b$ or $K_f$. Slip: $w_1$ is the solvent, not the solution.
:::

:::formula Molar mass from osmotic pressure
$M_2=\dfrac{w_2RT}{\pi V}$<br>
$V$ in litres, $T$ in kelvin; match the unit of $R$ to the unit of $\pi$.
:::

:::formula Degree of dissociation and association
$\alpha_\text{diss}=\dfrac{i-1}{n-1}$   $\alpha_\text{assoc}=\dfrac{1-i}{1-1/n}$<br>
$n$ = ions formed from one formula unit, or molecules that join. $i>1$ dissociation, $i<1$ association.
:::

:::formula Total vapour pressure of two volatile liquids
$p_\text{total}=x_1p_1^\circ+x_2p_2^\circ$   $y_1=\dfrac{x_1p_1^\circ}{p_\text{total}}$<br>
$x$ = mole fraction in the liquid, $y$ = mole fraction in the vapour (Dalton's law).
:::

:::formula Henry's law
$p=K_Hx$<br>
$K_H$ in bar (or atm) at a given temperature. Slip: larger $K_H$ means *lower* solubility.
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton New freezing point of an electrolyte solution (3 marks)
*e.g. 2 g NaCl (58.5) in 100 g water, $K_f=1.86$.*<br>
Given: $w_2=2$ g, $w_1=100$ g, $M_2=58.5$, $K_f=1.86$ K kg mol$^{-1}$.<br>
NaCl → Na$^+$ + Cl$^-$; assuming complete dissociation, $i=2$. [½]<br>
$m=\dfrac{2\times1000}{58.5\times100}=0.342$ mol kg$^{-1}$ [½]<br>
$\Delta T_f=iK_fm=2\times1.86\times0.342=1.27$ K [1]<br>
$T_f=273.15-1.27=\boxed{271.88\text{ K}}$ (or $-1.27^\circ$C) [1]
:::

:::skeleton Degree of dissociation from observed data
$i=\dfrac{\Delta T_\text{observed}}{\Delta T_\text{calculated (no dissociation)}}$ or $\dfrac{M_\text{calculated}}{M_\text{observed}}$ → $\alpha=\dfrac{i-1}{n-1}$ → state as %.<br>
Check: dissociation gives $i>1$; association gives $i<1$.
:::

:::skeleton Osmotic pressure
Moles $n_2=w_2/M_2$; $V$ in L; $T$ in K; $R=0.083$ L bar K$^{-1}$mol$^{-1}$ (answer in bar) or $0.0821$ (atm).<br>
$\pi=\dfrac{n_2RT}{V}$ → box with unit. For $M_2$: $M_2=\dfrac{w_2RT}{\pi V}$.
:::

:::skeleton Deviation question (3 marks)
Definition (Raoult) → positive: A–B weaker, $p$ higher, $\Delta_\text{mix}H>0$, $\Delta_\text{mix}V>0$, e.g. ethanol + acetone, min-boiling azeotrope → negative: A–B stronger, $p$ lower, both $<0$, e.g. chloroform + acetone, max-boiling azeotrope. A small $p$–$x$ sketch earns credit if asked.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Aquatic animals are more comfortable in cold water.**<br>→ $K_H$ of O$_2$ increases with $T$, so solubility of O$_2$ falls with rise in $T$; cold water holds more dissolved O$_2$.
2. **Osmotic pressure is preferred for macromolecules.**<br>→ Measured at room $T$ (proteins denature on heating); uses molarity; $\pi$ is large enough to measure even for very dilute solutions, while $\Delta T_f$, $\Delta T_b$ would be too small.
3. **Chloroform + acetone shows negative deviation.**<br>→ H-bond forms between H of CHCl$_3$ and O of acetone; A–B attraction is stronger, escaping tendency and $p$ fall; $\Delta_\text{mix}H<0$.
4. **Ethanol + acetone shows positive deviation.**<br>→ Acetone molecules come between ethanol molecules and break some of their H-bonds; weaker A–B forces; $p$ rises.
5. **Ethanoic acid in benzene: molar mass $\approx$ 120.**<br>→ It dimerises through H-bonding, halving the number of particles ($i\approx0.5$); colligative property halves, so calculated molar mass doubles.
6. **Molality is preferred to molarity.**<br>→ Molality uses mass of solvent, which does not change with $T$; molarity uses volume, which does.
7. **0.1 m NaCl boils higher than 0.1 m glucose.**<br>→ NaCl gives two ions ($i=2$); glucose does not dissociate ($i=1$); $\Delta T_b\propto i$.
8. **Salt is spread on icy roads.**<br>→ It lowers the freezing point of water, so ice melts at lower temperature.
9. **Blood cells swell in water / shrink in 1.5% NaCl.**<br>→ Water is hypotonic: water enters cells by osmosis. 1.5% NaCl is hypertonic: water leaves cells.
10. **Azeotropes cannot be separated by fractional distillation.**<br>→ Liquid and vapour have the same composition at the azeotropic point, so distillation cannot enrich either component.
11. **Vapour pressure falls when a non-volatile solute is added.**<br>→ Solute particles occupy part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape per unit time.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- A larger $K_H$ means: lower solubility of the gas at the same pressure.
- Gas solubility as temperature rises: decreases, because dissolving a gas is exothermic.
- Temperature-independent concentration terms: molality, mole fraction, mass %, ppm.
- Temperature-dependent concentration term: molarity, because volume changes with temperature.
- Positive deviation forms: a minimum-boiling azeotrope (ethanol–water).
- Negative deviation forms: a maximum-boiling azeotrope (nitric acid–water).
- Mixing of an ideal solution, signs: $\Delta_\text{mix}S>0$ and $\Delta_\text{mix}G<0$.
- At the same molality, the largest $i$ gives: lowest freezing point, highest boiling point, highest $\pi$.
- Values of $i$ on complete dissociation: $\ce{Al2(SO4)3}$ 5, $\ce{CaCl2}$ 3, NaCl 2, glucose 1.
- $K_b$ and $K_f$ depend on: the solvent only; unit K kg mol$^{-1}$.
- Normal saline: 0.9% (mass/volume) NaCl, isotonic with blood.
- Membrane used in reverse osmosis: cellulose acetate.
- Raoult's law as a special case of Henry's law: when $K_H=p_1^\circ$.
- Vapour above an ideal liquid mixture is richer in: the more volatile component.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Molality denominator
Using mass of *solution* instead of solvent in molality.
:::

:::tip Forgetting the van't Hoff factor
Forgetting $i$ for NaCl, CaCl$_2$, K$_2$SO$_4$.
:::

:::tip Freezing point or the depression
Giving $\Delta T_f$ when the question asks for the freezing point.
:::

:::tip Units in the osmotic pressure formula
$V$ in mL or $T$ in $^\circ$C in $\pi=CRT$.
:::

:::tip Azeotrope type
Swapping min-/max-boiling azeotropes.
:::

:::tip Vapour pressure with a non-volatile solute
Writing $p=x_2p^\circ$ for a non-volatile solute (correct: $p=x_1p_1^\circ$).
:::

:::tip Meaning of a high Henry's constant
Saying “higher $K_H$ means more soluble”.
:::
