# Ch 9 · Amines — formulas and key facts

## Structure, naming, preparation

- N is sp$^3$, pyramidal (lone pair); C–N–C in trimethylamine 108$^\circ$. 1$^\circ$ RNH$_2$, 2$^\circ$ R$_2$NH, 3$^\circ$ R$_3$N, quaternary R$_4$N$^+$X$^-$.
- IUPAC: ethanamine; N-methylmethanamine; N,N-dimethylmethanamine; benzenamine (aniline); prop-2-en-1-amine; hexane-1,6-diamine.

:::formula
Nitro → amine: H$_2$/Ni, Pd or Pt; Sn/HCl or **Fe/HCl** (preferred: FeCl$_2$ hydrolyses and regenerates HCl, so little acid needed)<br>
Ammonolysis: $\ce{RX + NH3 ->[ethanol][373 K, sealed tube]}$ $\ce{RNH3+X-}$ → 1$^\circ$, 2$^\circ$, 3$^\circ$ amines + quaternary salt (**mixture**; excess NH$_3$ favours 1$^\circ$). RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl<br>
$\begin{gathered}\ce{RC#N}\\ \ce{->[LiAlH4 / H2-Ni / Na(Hg)-C2H5OH]}\\ \ce{RCH2NH2}\end{gathered}$ (**+1 C**);   $\ce{RCONH2 ->[LiAlH4][H2O] RCH2NH2}$<br>
**Gabriel phthalimide**: phthalimide + alc. KOH → K-phthalimide + RX → N-alkylphthalimide → NaOH(aq) → RNH$_2$ (1$^\circ$ aliphatic only)<br>
**Hoffmann bromamide**: $\begin{gathered}\ce{RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH}\\ \ce{-> RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr}\\ \text{+ }\ce{2H2O}\end{gathered}$ (**$-$1 C**)
:::

- Gabriel cannot make aniline: aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.
- b.p.: 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ (3$^\circ$ has no N–H for H-bonding); amines $<$ alcohols of similar mass (N less electronegative than O). Lower amines: fishy smell. Aniline darkens on storage (air oxidation).

## Basicity

- Lone pair on N accepts H$^+$; larger $K_b$ / smaller p$K_b$ = stronger base. Aliphatic amines $>$ NH$_3$ (+I effect). **Aniline $<$ NH$_3$**: lone pair delocalised into the ring; anilinium ion has fewer resonance structures than aniline.
- Gas phase: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ NH$_3$ (+I only).
- Water (+I, solvation by H-bonding, steric): **(CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$**; **(C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$ $>$ NH$_3$**.
- p$K_b$: (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH 3.00, (CH$_3$)$_2$NH 3.27, C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$ 3.29, CH$_3$NH$_2$ 3.38, NH$_3$ 4.75, benzylamine 4.70, aniline 9.38.
- Substituted anilines: EDG (–CH$_3$, –OCH$_3$) raise basicity; EWG (–NO$_2$, –COOH, –SO$_3$H, –X) lower it. p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline. Benzylamine $>$ aniline (N not attached to ring).

## Reactions of amines

- **Acylation** (RCOCl, anhydride; pyridine removes HCl) → amides. **Benzoylation** with C$_6$H$_5$COCl: $\ce{CH3NH2}$ → N-methylbenzamide.
- **Carbylamine**: $\begin{gathered}\ce{R-NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH}\\ \ce{->[\Delta] R-NC + 3KCl}\\ \text{+ }\ce{3H2O}\end{gathered}$ — foul-smelling isocyanide; 1$^\circ$ amines only (aliphatic and aromatic).
- **Nitrous acid** (NaNO$_2$ + HCl): 1$^\circ$ aliphatic → alcohol + **N$_2$** (quantitative; estimation of amino acids/proteins). Aniline at 273–278 K → diazonium salt.
- **Hinsberg test** (benzenesulphonyl chloride): 1$^\circ$ → sulphonamide **soluble in alkali** (acidic N–H); 2$^\circ$ → sulphonamide **insoluble in alkali** (no N–H); 3$^\circ$ → no reaction.
- **Aniline + bromine water** → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (white ppt). For mono: acetylate (acetanilide, less activating: N lone pair also delocalised onto C=O) → brominate → 4-bromoacetanilide → hydrolyse → 4-bromoaniline.
- **Nitration**: direct gives tarry oxidation products and 47% meta (anilinium ion is meta-directing); 51% p, 2% o. Protect by acetylation → p-nitroaniline.
- **Sulphonation**: conc. H$_2$SO$_4$ → anilinium hydrogensulphate → 453–473 K → sulphanilic acid (zwitterion).
- **No Friedel–Crafts**: aniline forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ strongly deactivates the ring.

## Diazonium salts

:::formula
Diazotisation: $\begin{gathered}\ce{C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl}\\ \ce{->[273-278 K] C6H5N2+Cl-}\\ \text{+ }\ce{NaCl + 2H2O}\end{gathered}$<br>
Aryl diazonium salts are stable at low $T$ (resonance); used at once. Alkyl diazonium salts decompose immediately. $\ce{C6H5N2+BF4-}$ is stable at room temperature.
:::

| **Reagent (N$_2$ lost)** | **Product** |
|---|---|
| CuCl/HCl; CuBr/HBr (**Sandmeyer**) | ArCl; ArBr |
| Cu powder + HCl/HBr (**Gattermann**) | ArCl; ArBr |
| CuCN/KCN | ArCN (benzonitrile) |
| KI | ArI |
| HBF$_4$, then heat | ArF (+ BF$_3$ + N$_2$) |
| H$_3$PO$_2$ or CH$_3$CH$_2$OH | ArH (benzene) |
| H$_2$O, warm | ArOH (phenol) |
| HBF$_4$, then NaNO$_2$/Cu, heat | ArNO$_2$ |
| **Coupling (N retained)** | |
| phenol, alkaline (pH 9–10) | p-hydroxyazobenzene (**orange** dye) |
| aniline, mild acid | p-aminoazobenzene (**yellow** dye) |

- **Importance**: F, I, CN, OH can be put on the ring this way, which direct halogenation/substitution cannot do (ArF, ArI, ArCN, 1,3,5-tribromobenzene via aniline).

:::tip
Aniline is *o/p*-directing, but anilinium (in acid) is *meta*-directing. Carbylamine = test for 1$^\circ$ amines only. Hoffmann bromamide removes a C; nitrile reduction adds a C. Diazonium salts: 0–5 $^\circ$C (273–278 K).
:::

### Orders to know

*write the reason with the order*

- S$_\text{N}$2: CH$_3$X $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$ · S$_\text{N}$1: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ CH$_3$X · leaving group I $>$ Br $>$ Cl $>$ F.
- b.p. (similar mass): carboxylic acid $>$ alcohol $>$ 1$^\circ$ amine $>$ aldehyde/ketone $>$ ether $\approx$ alkane. Amines 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$. Haloalkanes RI $>$ RBr $>$ RCl.
- Acidity: RCOOH $>$ phenol $>$ water $>$ alcohol; alcohols 1$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 3$^\circ$; picric acid $>$ p-nitrophenol $>$ o- $>$ m-nitrophenol $>$ phenol $>$ cresol.
- Carboxylic acids: F– $>$ Cl– $>$ Br–CH$_2$COOH; CCl$_3$ $>$ CHCl$_2$ $>$ CH$_2$ClCOOH; 2-chloro $>$ 3-chloro $>$ 4-chlorobutanoic; HCOOH $>$ CH$_3$COOH $>$ CH$_3$CH$_2$COOH; 4-nitrobenzoic $>$ benzoic $>$ 4-methylbenzoic.
- Nucleophilic addition: HCHO $>$ CH$_3$CHO $>$ CH$_3$COCH$_3$; aliphatic aldehyde $>$ benzaldehyde.
- Basicity (water): (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$; benzylamine $>$ aniline; p-toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline.
- Reactivity of alcohols with HX / dehydration: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$; HI $>$ HBr $>$ HCl.

### Conversions — step chains

*carbon count first*

**Step up one C**: R–X + KCN → RCN (then LiAlH$_4$ → amine, or H$_3$O$^+$ → acid); RMgX + CO$_2$ → RCOOH; RMgX + HCHO → RCH$_2$OH. **Step down one C**: Hoffmann bromamide; decarboxylation. **Double**: Wurtz, Kolbe electrolysis.

- Methanamine → ethanamine: HNO$_2$ → CH$_3$OH → PCl$_5$ → CH$_3$Cl → KCN → CH$_3$CN → LiAlH$_4$.
- Ethanamine → methanamine: HNO$_2$ → C$_2$H$_5$OH → K$_2$Cr$_2$O$_7$/H$^+$ → CH$_3$COOH → NH$_3$, heat → CH$_3$CONH$_2$ → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Ethanoic acid → methanamine: NH$_3$, heat → acetamide → Br$_2$/NaOH.
- Benzene → aniline: conc. HNO$_3$/H$_2$SO$_4$ → Sn/HCl. Aniline → chlorobenzene / benzonitrile / phenol / benzene / fluorobenzene / iodobenzene: diazotise, then CuCl / CuCN / H$_2$O / H$_3$PO$_2$ / HBF$_4$ + heat / KI.
- Aniline → benzoic acid: diazotise → CuCN → H$_3$O$^+$. Aniline → p-bromoaniline: acetylate → Br$_2$ → hydrolyse.
- Propene → propan-1-ol: B$_2$H$_6$, H$_2$O$_2$/OH$^-$. Propene → propan-2-ol: H$_2$O/H$^+$. Propanone → propene: NaBH$_4$ → conc. H$_2$SO$_4$, heat.
- Benzoic acid → benzaldehyde: SOCl$_2$ → Rosenmund. Benzene → acetophenone: CH$_3$COCl/AlCl$_3$. Acetophenone → benzoic acid: NaOI (haloform) → H$^+$.
- Bromomethane → propanone: Mg/ether → CH$_3$MgBr + CH$_3$CN → H$_3$O$^+$.
- Ethanal → but-2-enal: dil. NaOH (aldol), heat. Ethanol → 3-hydroxybutanal: PCC → ethanal → dil. NaOH.
- Phenol → aspirin: Kolbe → salicylic acid → (CH$_3$CO)$_2$O. Phenol → anisole: NaOH → CH$_3$I. Chlorobenzene → phenol: NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm → H$^+$.
- Ethanol → propanenitrile: PCl$_5$ → KCN. Toluene → benzoic acid: alk. KMnO$_4$ → H$^+$.

### Top "give reason" answers

*one cause, stated in chemistry terms*

- **Haloarenes resist nucleophilic substitution** — resonance (partial C=X), sp$^2$ C, unstable phenyl cation, electron-rich ring repels Nu$^-$.
- **Grignard reagents are made in dry ether** — they react with water to give alkanes.
- **Chloroform stored in dark bottles filled to the brim** — air + light oxidise it to poisonous phosgene.
- **Phenol is acidic, ethanol hardly** — phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; ethoxide is not (and is destabilised by +I).
- **o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, p-isomer not** — intramolecular vs intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Alcohols boil higher than ethers of similar mass** — intermolecular H-bonding.
- **Anisole + HI gives phenol + CH$_3$I** — O–C(aryl) has partial double-bond character; S$_\text{N}$2 attack occurs at CH$_3$.
- **Aldehydes more reactive than ketones** — less steric hindrance and less +I reduction of C$^{\delta+}$.
- **Carboxylic acids do not give typical C=O reactions** — resonance of –COOH reduces the electrophilicity of the C.
- **Carboxylic acids are more acidic than phenols** — carboxylate has two equivalent resonance forms with charge on O atoms only.
- **Chloroacetic acid stronger than acetic acid** — $-I$ of Cl stabilises the carboxylate ion.
- **Aniline is a weaker base than ethylamine/NH$_3$** — lone pair delocalised into the ring.
- **(CH$_3$)$_3$N weaker base than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water** — poorer H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding.
- **Aniline gives meta product on nitration** — in strong acid it forms anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
- **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts** — it forms a salt with AlCl$_3$; N$^+$ deactivates the ring.
- **Acetylation of –NH$_2$ before nitration/bromination** — lowers activation, avoids polysubstitution and oxidation.
- **Gabriel synthesis cannot give aryl amines** — aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with phthalimide anion.
- **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl ones** — resonance with the ring.
- **Sucrose is non-reducing** — no free anomeric –OH (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose are bonded).
- **Amino acids have high m.p. and are water-soluble** — exist as dipolar zwitterions (salt-like).
