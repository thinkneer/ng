## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Why amines are basic:** the lone pair on nitrogen accepts a proton.
- **p$K_b$ and base strength:** smaller p$K_b$ (larger $K_b$) means a stronger base.
- **Why alkylamines are stronger bases than ammonia:** alkyl groups release electrons and stabilise the ammonium ion.
- **Why aniline is a weaker base than ammonia:** its lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring.
- **Factors deciding basicity in water:** inductive effect, solvation of the cation by hydrogen bonding, steric hindrance.
- **Classification of amines:** by the number of carbon groups on nitrogen (1°, 2°, 3°).
- **Gabriel phthalimide synthesis:** gives pure primary aliphatic amines; cannot make aniline.
- **Hoffmann bromamide degradation:** amide + $\ce{Br2}$ + NaOH gives a primary amine with one carbon fewer.
- **Carbylamine reaction:** primary amine + $\ce{CHCl3}$ + alcoholic KOH gives a foul-smelling isocyanide.
- **Hinsberg's reagent:** benzenesulphonyl chloride, $\ce{C6H5SO2Cl}$.
- **Hinsberg's test result:** 1° product soluble in alkali, 2° product insoluble, 3° no reaction.
- **Diazotisation:** primary aromatic amine + $\ce{NaNO2}$ + HCl at 273–278 K gives a diazonium salt.
- **Coupling reaction:** diazonium ion attacks phenol or aniline at the para position to give an azo dye.
- **Why aniline is acetylated before nitration or bromination:** to reduce the activating effect of $\ce{-NH2}$ and get the para product.
- **Why aniline gives no Friedel–Crafts reaction:** it forms a salt with $\ce{AlCl3}$, which deactivates the ring.

## Key formulas

:::formula Hoffmann bromamide degradation
$\ce{RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH -> RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O}$<br>
One carbon fewer than the amide. Slip: unbalanced equation; remember 4 NaOH.
:::

:::formula Carbylamine reaction
$\ce{RNH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH ->[heat] RNC + 3KCl + 3H2O}$<br>
Test for primary amines (aliphatic and aromatic). KOH is alcoholic.
:::

:::formula Gabriel phthalimide synthesis
Phthalimide $\xrightarrow{\text{KOH}}$ potassium phthalimide $\xrightarrow{\ce{R-X}}$ N-alkylphthalimide $\xrightarrow{\text{NaOH(aq)}}$ $\ce{R-NH2}$<br>
Primary aliphatic amines only.
:::

:::formula Reduction routes to amines
$\ce{ArNO2 ->[Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl] ArNH2}$   $\ce{RCN ->[LiAlH4] RCH2NH2}$   $\ce{RCONH2 ->[LiAlH4] RCH2NH2}$<br>
Halide to nitrile to amine adds one carbon.
:::

:::formula Diazotisation
$\ce{C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2HCl ->[273-278 K] C6H5N2+Cl- + NaCl + 2H2O}$<br>
Slip: above 278 K the salt reacts with water and gives phenol.
:::

:::formula Sandmeyer and Gattermann reactions
$\ce{ArN2+Cl- ->[CuCl/HCl] ArCl}$ · $\ce{->[CuBr/HBr] ArBr}$ · $\ce{->[CuCN/KCN] ArCN}$ (Sandmeyer)<br>
Gattermann: copper powder with HCl or HBr gives ArCl or ArBr.
:::

:::formula Other replacements of the diazonium group
KI → ArI · $\ce{HBF4}$ then heat → ArF · warm $\ce{H2O}$ → ArOH · $\ce{H3PO2}$ (or ethanol) → ArH<br>
Nitrogen gas is lost in every case.
:::

:::formula Coupling reactions
$\ce{C6H5N2+Cl-}$ + phenol (alkaline) → p-hydroxyazobenzene, orange<br>
$\ce{C6H5N2+Cl-}$ + aniline (weakly acidic) → p-aminoazobenzene, yellow
:::

:::formula Basicity orders
Water, methyl: $\ce{(CH3)2NH}$ > $\ce{CH3NH2}$ > $\ce{(CH3)3N}$ > $\ce{NH3}$<br>
Water, ethyl: $\ce{(C2H5)2NH}$ > $\ce{(C2H5)3N}$ > $\ce{C2H5NH2}$ > $\ce{NH3}$<br>
Gas phase: 3° > 2° > 1° > $\ce{NH3}$. Slip: giving the gas-phase order for an aqueous question.
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton Basicity in water (3 marks)
*Arrange CH$_3$NH$_2$, (CH$_3$)$_2$NH, (CH$_3$)$_3$N, NH$_3$, C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$ in decreasing basicity.*<br>
(CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$ [1]<br>
Alkyl amines $>$ NH$_3$: +I raises electron density on N. 3$^\circ$ below 2$^\circ$/1$^\circ$: fewer N–H for H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding [1]. Aniline weakest: lone pair in resonance with ring [1].
:::

:::skeleton Hinsberg test (3 marks)
Shake each amine with benzenesulphonyl chloride + KOH: 1$^\circ$ → N-alkylbenzenesulphonamide dissolves in alkali (acidic N–H) [1]; 2$^\circ$ → N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide, insoluble [1]; 3$^\circ$ → no reaction [1].
:::

:::skeleton Ascent/descent (2–3 marks)
**Up**: CH$_3$NH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2}$ CH$_3$OH $\xrightarrow{\text{PCl}_5}$ CH$_3$Cl $\xrightarrow{\text{KCN}}$ CH$_3$CN $\xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4}$ CH$_3$CH$_2$NH$_2$.<br>
**Down**: C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2}$ C$_2$H$_5$OH $\xrightarrow{\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7/\text{H}^+}$ CH$_3$COOH $\xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3,\ \Delta}$ CH$_3$CONH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{NaOH}}$ CH$_3$NH$_2$.
:::

:::skeleton Aniline → p-bromoaniline (3 marks)
Aniline $\xrightarrow{(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O},\ \text{pyridine}}$ acetanilide $\xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}}$ 4-bromoacetanilide $\xrightarrow{\text{H}^+\text{ or OH}^-,\ \text{H}_2\text{O}}$ 4-bromoaniline. Reason: acetylation reduces activation, avoids tribromination.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Aniline is a weaker base than NH$_3$/methylamine.**<br>→ Lone pair on N is delocalised into the ring; anilinium ion is less resonance-stabilised than aniline.
2. **(CH$_3$)$_3$N is weaker than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water.**<br>→ Poorer H-bond solvation of (CH$_3$)$_3$NH$^+$ and steric hindrance.
3. **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction.**<br>→ It forms a salt with AlCl$_3$ (Lewis acid); positive N strongly deactivates the ring.
4. **Nitration of aniline gives a large amount of meta product.**<br>→ In strong acid aniline is protonated to anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
5. **Gabriel synthesis cannot make aniline.**<br>→ Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.
6. **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl.**<br>→ Positive charge is delocalised over the ring by resonance.
7. **Fe/HCl preferred for reducing nitrobenzene.**<br>→ FeCl$_2$ formed is hydrolysed, releasing HCl; only a little acid is needed.
8. **1$^\circ$ amines boil higher than 3$^\circ$ amines.**<br>→ 1$^\circ$ amines have two N–H bonds for intermolecular H-bonding; 3$^\circ$ have none.
9. **Amines boil lower than alcohols of similar mass.**<br>→ N is less electronegative than O, so N–H$\cdots$N bonds are weaker.
10. **Aniline darkens on storage.**<br>→ Atmospheric oxidation forms coloured impurities.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- p$K_b$ of aniline: 9.38; of ammonia: 4.75.
- Basicity on the ring: p-toluidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline.
- Boiling points of isomeric amines: 1° > 2° > 3°.
- Amines boil lower than alcohols of similar mass because: N–H···N hydrogen bonds are weaker than O–H···O.
- Primary aliphatic amine with nitrous acid gives: an alcohol, with nitrogen gas evolved.
- Aniline with bromine water gives: 2,4,6-tribromoaniline, a white precipitate.
- Direct nitration of aniline gives much meta product because: it forms the meta-directing anilinium ion in acid.
- Sulphanilic acid exists as: a zwitterion.
- Temperature of diazotisation: 273–278 K (0–5 °C).
- Benzenediazonium ion is fairly stable because: its positive charge is delocalised into the ring by resonance.
- Medium for coupling with phenol: weakly alkaline.
- Medium for coupling with aniline: weakly acidic.
- Tertiary amines are not acylated because: they have no hydrogen on nitrogen.
- Reagent preferred for reducing nitrobenzene in industry: iron scrap and HCl.
- Test for a primary aromatic amine: azo dye (orange-red) with alkaline 2-naphthol after diazotisation.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Aqueous or gas-phase order
Giving gas-phase order when “aqueous” is asked (and vice versa).
:::

:::tip Carbylamine test
Writing carbylamine for 2$^\circ$ amines.
:::

:::tip Hinsberg solubility
Hinsberg solubility swapped (1$^\circ$ soluble in alkali).
:::

:::tip Temperature of diazotisation
Diazotisation at room temperature — phenol forms instead.
:::

:::tip Hoffmann equation
Hoffmann equation unbalanced (4NaOH, Na$_2$CO$_3$, 2NaBr, 2H$_2$O).
:::

:::tip Aniline in strong acid
Saying aniline is o/p-directing even in strong acid.
:::
