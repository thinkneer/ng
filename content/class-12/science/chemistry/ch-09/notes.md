## Must-know lines and definitions

- Amines are bases because of the unshared electron pair on nitrogen. Larger $K_b$ (smaller pK$_b$) → stronger base.
- Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia (+I effect of alkyl groups); aromatic amines are weaker (lone pair delocalised into the ring).
- In water, basicity depends on +I effect, **solvation** of the ammonium ion by H-bonding, and **steric** hindrance.
- **Carbylamine reaction**: 1$^\circ$ aliphatic and aromatic amines heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH give foul-smelling isocyanides; 2$^\circ$ and 3$^\circ$ amines do not.
- **Hinsberg reagent** (benzenesulphonyl chloride): sulphonamide of 1$^\circ$ amine is soluble in alkali; of 2$^\circ$ amine insoluble; 3$^\circ$ amines do not react.
- Diazonium salts of aromatic amines are stable only at low temperature (273–278 K) and are used immediately.

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton Ord+R · Basicity in water (3 marks)
*Arrange CH$_3$NH$_2$, (CH$_3$)$_2$NH, (CH$_3$)$_3$N, NH$_3$, C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$ in decreasing basicity.*<br>
(CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$ $>$ C$_6$H$_5$NH$_2$ [1]<br>
Alkyl amines $>$ NH$_3$: +I raises electron density on N. 3$^\circ$ below 2$^\circ$/1$^\circ$: fewer N–H for H-bond solvation of R$_3$NH$^+$ and steric crowding [1]. Aniline weakest: lone pair in resonance with ring [1].
:::

:::skeleton Ds · Hinsberg test (3 marks)
Shake each amine with benzenesulphonyl chloride + KOH: 1$^\circ$ → N-alkylbenzenesulphonamide dissolves in alkali (acidic N–H) [1]; 2$^\circ$ → N,N-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide, insoluble [1]; 3$^\circ$ → no reaction [1].
:::

:::skeleton Cv · Ascent/descent (2–3 marks)
**Up**: CH$_3$NH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2}$ CH$_3$OH $\xrightarrow{\text{PCl}_5}$ CH$_3$Cl $\xrightarrow{\text{KCN}}$ CH$_3$CN $\xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4}$ CH$_3$CH$_2$NH$_2$.<br>
**Down**: C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{HNO}_2}$ C$_2$H$_5$OH $\xrightarrow{\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7/\text{H}^+}$ CH$_3$COOH $\xrightarrow{\text{NH}_3,\ \Delta}$ CH$_3$CONH$_2$ $\xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{NaOH}}$ CH$_3$NH$_2$.
:::

:::skeleton Cv · Aniline → p-bromoaniline (3 marks)
Aniline $\xrightarrow{(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O},\ \text{pyridine}}$ acetanilide $\xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}}$ 4-bromoacetanilide $\xrightarrow{\text{H}^+\text{ or OH}^-,\ \text{H}_2\text{O}}$ 4-bromoaniline. Reason: acetylation reduces activation, avoids tribromination.
:::

### Why/reason answers — write them like this

1. **Aniline is a weaker base than NH$_3$/methylamine.**<br>→ Lone pair on N is delocalised into the ring; anilinium ion is less resonance-stabilised than aniline.
2. **(CH$_3$)$_3$N is weaker than (CH$_3$)$_2$NH in water.**<br>→ Poorer H-bond solvation of (CH$_3$)$_3$NH$^+$ and steric hindrance.
3. **Aniline does not undergo Friedel–Crafts reaction.**<br>→ It forms a salt with AlCl$_3$ (Lewis acid); positive N strongly deactivates the ring.
4. **Nitration of aniline gives a large amount of meta product.**<br>→ In strong acid aniline is protonated to anilinium ion, which is meta-directing.
5. **Gabriel synthesis cannot make aniline.**<br>→ Aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution with the phthalimide anion.
6. **Aryl diazonium salts are more stable than alkyl.**<br>→ Positive charge is delocalised over the ring by resonance.
7. **Fe/HCl preferred for reducing nitrobenzene.**<br>→ FeCl$_2$ formed is hydrolysed, releasing HCl; only a little acid is needed.
8. **1$^\circ$ amines boil higher than 3$^\circ$ amines.**<br>→ 1$^\circ$ amines have two N–H bonds for intermolecular H-bonding; 3$^\circ$ have none.
9. **Amines boil lower than alcohols of similar mass.**<br>→ N is less electronegative than O, so N–H$\cdots$N bonds are weaker.
10. **Aniline darkens on storage.**<br>→ Atmospheric oxidation forms coloured impurities.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Water: (CH$_3$)$_2$NH $>$ CH$_3$NH$_2$ $>$ (CH$_3$)$_3$N $>$ NH$_3$; (C$_2$H$_5$)$_2$NH $>$ (C$_2$H$_5$)$_3$N $>$ C$_2$H$_5$NH$_2$ $>$ NH$_3$. Gas phase: 3$^\circ$ $>$ 2$^\circ$ $>$ 1$^\circ$ $>$ NH$_3$.
- pK$_b$: aniline 9.38 (weakest), NH$_3$ 4.75, benzylamine 4.70, diethylamine 3.00 (strongest listed).
- p-Toluidine $>$ aniline $>$ p-nitroaniline (basicity).
- Hoffmann bromamide: product has one C less. Nitrile reduction: one C more.
- Carbylamine test only for 1$^\circ$ amines; HNO$_2$ with 1$^\circ$ aliphatic amine → N$_2$ gas (estimation).
- Diazotisation at 273–278 K (0–5 $^\circ$C). Sulphanilic acid exists as a zwitterion.
- Coupling with phenol in alkaline medium (orange dye); with aniline in mildly acidic medium (yellow dye).

- pK$_b$ table → arrange, explain solvation/steric/+I.
- Dye industry passage → diazotisation and coupling equations, temperature control.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip
(1) Giving gas-phase order when ``aqueous'' is asked (and vice versa). (2) Writing carbylamine for 2$^\circ$ amines. (3) Hinsberg solubility swapped (1$^\circ$ soluble in alkali). (4) Diazotisation at room temperature — phenol forms instead. (5) Hoffmann equation unbalanced (4NaOH, Na$_2$CO$_3$, 2NaBr, 2H$_2$O). (6) Saying aniline is o/p-directing even in strong acid.
:::
