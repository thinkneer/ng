# Practical · Project, record and exam day

Nine of the 30 practical marks do not come from the experiment you perform on the day: **5 for the investigatory project** (file + viva) and **4 for the practical record + viva**. Both are finished before the exam, so they are the easiest marks to secure. The practical exams run in the window of **1 January to 13 February 2027**; your school sets the exact day.

## Choosing a project

An investigatory project asks **one clear question** and answers it with your own observations. A good topic is small enough to finish in 3–4 weeks, uses school-lab or household materials, and connects to the syllabus so you can explain it in the viva.

Ideas that fit the 2026–27 syllabus (pick one and make it your own):
- **Pollen germination** in sucrose solutions of different strengths (5%, 10%, 15%, 20%): which works best?
- **Mitotic index** of onion root tips cut at different times of day.
- **Curd setting** at different temperatures: how long does milk take to set?
- **Seed germination** under different salt concentrations or pH.
- **Plant biodiversity** of your school campus by the quadrat method: density and frequency of common herbs.
- **DNA yield** from different fruits (banana, papaya, strawberry) by the kitchen method.
- **Mosquito breeding sites** around your home or school and how to remove them.
- **Inheritance of a harmless trait** (tongue rolling, ear lobes, widow's peak) as a pedigree of your own family, with their permission.

:::tip Safety and ethics first
Do not grow disease-causing microbes at home, do not harm animals, and never write anyone's name in a survey. Projects that culture bacteria are done only in the school lab with your teacher.
:::

## Running the project

```ng-steps
id: project-steps
title: From idea to viva
step: **Question**: write your aim as one sentence ("To study the effect of sucrose concentration on pollen germination").
step: **Read**: note the background from NCERT and your teacher; write it in your own words.
step: **Plan**: decide what you change (one factor), what you measure, and what you keep the same; get the plan approved by your teacher.
step: **Do and repeat**: carry out the experiment or survey, at least three times or with enough samples; record everything the same day.
step: **Analyse**: put the readings in tables, draw a graph, work out averages or percentages.
step: **Conclude**: answer your question in two or three lines and list the sources of error.
step: **Write and rehearse**: complete the file and practise explaining it in two minutes for the viva.
```

```ng-compare
id: project-fair-test
title: What changes, what you measure, what stays the same
cols: Example: pollen germination | Example: curd setting
row: You change | sucrose concentration | temperature
row: You measure | % germination after 30 minutes | time for milk to set
row: You keep the same | flower species, time, slide, temperature | milk volume, amount of starter, vessel
```

**In one breath:** Aim → background → plan with one changing factor → repeat and record → tables and graph → conclusion and errors → file and a two-minute explanation.

## The project file and the practical record

The **project file** shows your own work in a clear order:

```ng-fill
id: project-file-order
title: Sections of a project file, in order
cols: Section | What goes in it
row: Title page and certificate | [[topic, your name and class, school; teacher's signature]]
row: Acknowledgement and index | [[thanks; page numbers of each section]]
row: Aim and introduction | [[the question; background biology in your own words]]
row: Materials and procedure | [[what you used; the steps you followed]]
row: Observations | [[tables and graphs of your readings]]
row: Result and conclusion | [[what you found; the answer to your question]]
row: Precautions, errors, bibliography | [[care taken; what could go wrong; books and sites used]]
```

The **practical record** holds every experiment you did in the year. For each one write: aim, principle, materials, procedure, **labelled diagram** (pencil, on the plain left page), observation table, result and precautions. Spotting items go in with a sketch and two or three identification points. Get each experiment signed by your teacher as you go, not in the last week.

**In one breath:** Project file: title page to bibliography, in a fixed order, all in your own words. Practical record: every experiment as aim, principle, materials, procedure, labelled pencil diagram, observations, result, precautions; signed as you go.

## The viva

The viva checks that **you did the work and understand it**. Typical questions: What was your aim? Why did you choose this method? What did you keep constant? What would you change if you did it again? How is this linked to the syllabus? For experiments: the role of each reagent (HCl, aceto-carmine, sucrose, boric acid, detergent, chilled ethanol) and the formula you used.

:::tip Don't bluff
If you do not know an answer, say so and say what you would check. Examiners mark honesty and understanding; a confident wrong answer costs more.
:::

**In one breath:** Know your aim, method, constants, result and one improvement; know each reagent's role and each formula. Answer briefly and honestly.

## Practical exam day

```ng-steps
id: exam-day-steps
title: The 3-hour practical exam, in order
step: **Carry**: practical record and project file (signed), pen, pencil, eraser, sharpener, ruler; arrive 15 minutes early.
step: **Read** the question slip fully; note which experiment is the major one (5) and which the minor one (4).
step: **Start** the step that needs waiting time first (e.g. fix or hydrolyse root tips, soak material), then set up the other experiment.
step: **Write as you go**: aim, a short procedure, and the observation table before you take readings.
step: **Slide preparation**: stain, mount, check under the microscope and call the examiner to see it.
step: **Spotting**: about one minute per spot; write the name first, then two or three points.
step: **Finish** with the result, precautions and labelled diagrams; hand in the record and project for the viva.
```

:::tip Lab safety
Warm HCl and stains gently and never boil them; keep the spirit lamp away from ethanol; wash hands after handling chemicals.
:::

**In one breath:** Carry signed record and project, pencil and ruler; read the slip; start the waiting steps first; write the table before readings; show the slide; one minute per spot; end with result, precautions and diagrams.
