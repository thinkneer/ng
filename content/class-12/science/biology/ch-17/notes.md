# Practical · Isolating DNA from plant material

This is the kitchen-lab version of the first step of recombinant DNA technology (Ch 9: "isolation of the genetic material"). With soap, salt, an enzyme and cold alcohol you can see DNA as white threads and lift it out on a glass rod.

## Aim and principle

**Aim.** To isolate DNA from available plant material such as spinach, green pea seeds, papaya or banana.

**Principle.** DNA lies inside the nucleus, wrapped in membranes and bound to proteins. We remove these barriers one by one:
- **Grinding** breaks the cell walls.
- A **detergent** dissolves the lipid membranes (cell and nuclear membranes), releasing DNA.
- **Salt (NaCl)** supplies Na⁺ ions that neutralise the negative charge of DNA's phosphate groups, so the strands clump together and later come out of solution.
- A **protease** (meat tenderiser or fresh pineapple or papaya juice) breaks down the **histones** and other proteins bound to DNA.
- DNA is **insoluble in chilled ethanol**, so when cold ethanol is layered on top, DNA **precipitates as fine white threads**, which can be **spooled** on a glass rod.

:::tip Why "chilled"?
Cold ethanol lowers the solubility of DNA further and slows enzymes that could break it, so more threads come out.
:::

**In one breath:** Grind to break walls; detergent dissolves membranes; salt neutralises DNA's charge; protease removes histones; chilled ethanol makes DNA come out as white threads that you spool.

## Materials

- Spinach leaves, green pea seeds, papaya or banana (about 10 g)
- Mortar and pestle (or a blender), beaker, test tube, glass rod
- Extraction mixture: water + a little liquid detergent (or shampoo) + a pinch of table salt
- Protease source: meat tenderiser, or fresh pineapple or papaya juice
- Filter paper or muslin cloth, funnel; **ice-cold 95% ethanol** (or isopropanol), ice bath

**In one breath:** Plant material, mortar and pestle, detergent–salt water, a protease, a filter, and ice-cold ethanol.

## Procedure

```ng-steps
id: dna-procedure
title: Isolating DNA, step by step
step: **Grind** about 10 g of the plant material with a little water in a mortar.
step: **Add** the extraction mixture (water + detergent + salt) and grind or stir gently for a few minutes.
step: **Filter** the mixture through muslin or filter paper into a beaker; keep the filtrate.
step: **Add the protease** (a pinch of meat tenderiser or a few drops of pineapple juice) to the filtrate and mix gently; wait about 5–10 minutes.
step: Pour some filtrate into a test tube and slowly run **ice-cold ethanol down the side** to form a layer on top (about equal volume).
step: **Wait** without shaking: white, thread-like DNA appears at the boundary of the two layers.
step: **Spool** the threads by gently twisting a glass rod at the boundary.
```

## Result and precautions

**Result.** White, fibrous, thread-like **DNA** precipitates at the boundary between the filtrate and the chilled ethanol and can be spooled on a glass rod.

**Precautions**
1. Use **ice-cold** ethanol.
2. Pour the ethanol **slowly along the side** of the tube so it forms a layer.
3. Do **not shake** the tube after adding ethanol; shaking breaks the DNA strands.
4. Grind well but **stir gently** after adding detergent, to avoid foam and broken DNA.
5. Spool by **gently twisting** the rod at the boundary.
