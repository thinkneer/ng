## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Aim of the experiment:** isolate DNA from spinach, pea, papaya or banana.
- **Role of grinding:** breaks the cell walls.
- **Role of detergent:** dissolves lipid cell and nuclear membranes.
- **Role of salt (NaCl):** Na⁺ neutralises DNA's negative phosphate groups.
- **Role of protease:** digests histones and other DNA-bound proteins.
- **Role of chilled ethanol:** DNA is insoluble in it and precipitates.
- **Why ethanol is chilled:** lowers DNA solubility; slows DNA-breaking enzymes.
- **Appearance of isolated DNA:** white, fine, thread-like fibres.
- **Spooling of DNA:** winding the threads on a glass rod.
- **Kitchen protease sources:** papaya (papain), pineapple (bromelain), meat tenderiser.
- **Why not shake after ethanol:** shaking shears the long DNA strands.

## Key formulas

:::formula DNA extraction sequence
Grind → detergent + salt → filter → protease → chilled ethanol → spool.

When to use: writing the procedure. Common slip: adding ethanol before filtering.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- DNA is precipitated from solution by: chilled ethanol.
- Enzyme to remove RNA in lab isolation: ribonuclease.
- Enzyme to remove protein: protease.
- Enzyme to break plant cell walls: cellulase.
- DNA carries negative charge because of: phosphate groups.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Shaking the tube
Shaking after adding ethanol breaks DNA into fragments that do not spool; let the layers stand.
:::

:::tip Warm ethanol
Room-temperature ethanol gives few or no threads; it must be ice-cold.
:::
