## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Biodiversity (term popularised by):** Edward Wilson.
- **Genetic diversity example:** *Rauwolfia vomitoria*; varying reserpine across Himalayan ranges.
- **Rice and mango diversity in India:** over 50,000 rice strains; 1,000 mango varieties.
- **Species diversity example:** Western Ghats have more amphibian species than Eastern Ghats.
- **Ecological diversity example:** India has more ecosystem diversity than Norway.
- **Described species (IUCN 2004):** a little over 1.5 million.
- **Estimated global species (Robert May):** about 7 million.
- **Animals among recorded species:** over 70%.
- **Insects among animals:** over 70%; 7 of every 10 animals.
- **India's biodiversity share:** 2.4% of world land, 8.1% of species; one of 12 mega-diversity countries.
- **Latitudinal gradient:** diversity decreases from equator to poles.
- **Richest biodiversity on Earth:** Amazonian rain forest.
- **Species–area relationship:** Alexander von Humboldt.
- **Z (normal range):** 0.1 to 0.2.
- **Z for whole continents:** 0.6 to 1.2.
- **Z for frugivorous birds and mammals in tropical forests:** 1.15.
- **Tilman's finding:** more species → less yearly variation in biomass, higher productivity.
- **Rivet popper hypothesis:** Paul Ehrlich; species as rivets holding an airplane.
- **Extinctions in last 500 years (IUCN 2004):** 784 species.
- **Sixth extinction:** current human-driven extinction, 100–1,000 times pre-human rate.
- **Evil Quartet:** habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, alien invasions, co-extinctions.
- **Most important cause of loss:** habitat loss and fragmentation.
- **Lungs of the planet:** Amazon rain forest.
- **Alien invasion example:** Nile perch in Lake Victoria → over 200 cichlid species lost.
- **Co-extinction, meaning:** associated species die out with their host or partner.
- **Bioprospecting, meaning:** exploring biodiversity for products of economic importance.
- **Biodiversity hotspots:** regions of high species richness and endemism.
- **Endemism, meaning:** species found only in one region.
- **Number of hotspots:** 34; three cover India.
- **Hotspots in India:** Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya.
- **Sacred groves:** forest patches protected for religious reasons.
- **In situ conservation:** protecting species in their natural habitat.
- **Ex situ conservation:** protecting species outside their habitat (zoos, gardens, seed banks).
- **Cryopreservation of gametes:** preserving gametes in viable condition at very low temperatures.
- **Earth Summit:** Rio de Janeiro, 1992; Convention on Biological Diversity.
- **World Summit on Sustainable Development:** Johannesburg, 2002; 190 countries, target 2010.

## Key formulas

:::formula Species–area relationship
$\log S = \log C + Z\log A$ (or $S = CA^Z$); Z = slope.
:::

:::formula Z values
Normal regions 0.1–0.2 · whole continents 0.6–1.2 · frugivorous birds and mammals 1.15.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- **Bird species by latitude:** Colombia ≈ 1,400; New York 105; Greenland 56; India > 1,200.
- Amazon: > 40,000 plants, 3,000 fishes, 1,300 birds, 427 mammals.
- Plants among recorded species: no more than 22%.
- Fungal species exceed: fishes, amphibians, reptiles and mammals combined.
- Recent extinctions: dodo (Mauritius), quagga (Africa), thylacine (Australia), Steller's sea cow (Russia).
- Extinct tiger subspecies: Bali, Javan, Caspian.
- **Threatened species (IUCN):** 12% birds, 23% mammals, 32% amphibians, 31% gymnosperms.
- Rain forest cover: once over 14% of land, now no more than 6%.
- Amazon oxygen contribution: about 20% of atmospheric oxygen.
- Drugs from plants: over 25% of drugs sold worldwide.
- Plants in traditional medicine: 25,000 species.
- Hotspots cover: less than 2% of Earth's land; protection could cut extinctions by ≈ 30%.
- Sacred groves: Khasi and Jaintia Hills (Meghalaya), Aravalli (Rajasthan), Western Ghats (Karnataka, Maharashtra), Sarguja–Chanda–Bastar (MP).
- NCERT protected areas: 14 biosphere reserves, 90 national parks, 448 wildlife sanctuaries.
- Invasive weeds: *Parthenium*, *Lantana*, water hyacinth (*Eichhornia*).
- African catfish threatening Indian catfish: *Clarias gariepinus*.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Z for continents
Z is 0.1–0.2 for most regions but 0.6–1.2 for whole continents; don't swap them.
:::

:::tip In situ vs ex situ
National parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves = in situ; zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks = ex situ.
:::

:::tip Most important cause
Habitat loss and fragmentation is the most important cause, not over-exploitation.
:::

:::tip Who said what
Humboldt: species–area; Tilman: diversity and stability; Ehrlich: rivet popper; May: 7 million species.
:::
