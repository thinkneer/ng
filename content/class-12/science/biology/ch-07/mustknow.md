## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Health, as defined:** complete physical, mental and social well-being.
- **Typhoid pathogen:** *Salmonella typhi*; confirmed by Widal test.
- **Pneumonia pathogens:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*.
- **Common cold pathogen:** rhinoviruses; infects nose and respiratory passage, not lungs.
- **Malaria pathogens:** *Plasmodium vivax*, *P. malariae*, *P. falciparum*.
- **Malignant malaria:** caused by *Plasmodium falciparum*.
- **Malaria vector:** female *Anopheles* mosquito.
- **Infectious form of Plasmodium:** sporozoite.
- **Haemozoin in malaria:** toxin from burst RBCs; causes chill and fever every 3–4 days.
- **Sexual stage of Plasmodium:** gametocytes; fertilisation in mosquito's gut.
- **Amoebiasis pathogen:** *Entamoeba histolytica*; houseflies are carriers.
- **Ascariasis pathogen:** *Ascaris*, roundworm.
- **Filariasis pathogens:** *Wuchereria bancrofti*, *W. malayi*.
- **Ringworm fungi:** *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton*.
- **Dengue and chikungunya vector:** *Aedes* mosquito.
- **Gambusia fish:** fish that eats mosquito larvae.
- **Innate immunity:** non-specific defence present at birth.
- **Physiological barriers:** stomach acid, saliva, tears.
- **Interferons (cytokine barrier):** proteins from virus-infected cells protecting other cells.
- **Acquired immunity:** pathogen-specific; has memory.
- **Anamnestic response:** strong secondary response on re-exposure.
- **Humoral immunity:** antibody-mediated, by B-lymphocytes.
- **Cell-mediated immunity:** by T-lymphocytes; causes graft rejection.
- **Antibody structure:** two heavy and two light chains, H₂L₂.
- **Antibody types:** IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG.
- **Active immunity:** body makes its own antibodies; slow, long-lasting.
- **Passive immunity:** ready-made antibodies given; quick, short-lived.
- **Colostrum antibody:** IgA.
- **Hepatitis B vaccine:** made in yeast by recombinant DNA technology.
- **Allergy antibody:** IgE; mast cells release histamine and serotonin.
- **Autoimmune disease example:** rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Primary lymphoid organs:** bone marrow and thymus.
- **Secondary lymphoid organs:** spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix.
- **MALT, full form:** mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, about 50% of lymphoid tissue.
- **HIV virus type:** retrovirus with RNA genome; causes AIDS.
- **Reverse transcriptase:** makes viral DNA from HIV RNA.
- **HIV factory:** infected macrophage.
- **Cells reduced in AIDS:** helper T-lymphocytes.
- **AIDS test:** ELISA.
- **Contact inhibition:** normal cells stop dividing on contact; lost in cancer.
- **Metastasis in cancer:** spread of malignant tumour cells to distant sites.
- **Proto-oncogenes (c-onc):** cellular oncogenes that can cause cancer when activated.
- **Immunotherapy agent:** α-interferon, a biological response modifier.
- **Heroin (smack) source:** diacetylmorphine from morphine of *Papaver somniferum*; depressant.
- **Cannabinoid source:** inflorescence of *Cannabis sativa*; affects cardiovascular system.
- **Cocaine source:** *Erythroxylum coca*; interferes with dopamine transport.
- **Effect of nicotine:** stimulates adrenal gland to release adrenaline, noradrenaline.
- **Withdrawal syndrome:** unpleasant symptoms when a drug is stopped.

## Key formulas

:::formula Malaria fever interval
Chill and high fever recur every 3–4 days, when RBCs burst and release haemozoin.
:::

:::formula Antibody chains
1 antibody = 2 heavy + 2 light chains = 4 polypeptides (H₂L₂).
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton Plasmodium life cycle (3 marks)
Sporozoites injected by female *Anopheles* → liver cells → RBCs burst, haemozoin, fever [1] → gametocytes in blood taken up by mosquito [1] → fertilisation in gut → sporozoites in salivary glands [1]
:::

:::skeleton HIV to AIDS (3 marks)
HIV enters macrophage; reverse transcriptase → viral DNA → host DNA; macrophage = HIV factory [1] → infects and kills helper T cells, numbers fall [1] → immunodeficiency; opportunistic infections; diagnosed by ELISA [1]
:::

:::skeleton Active vs passive immunity (2 marks)
Own antibodies vs ready-made; slow and long-lasting vs quick and short [1] → examples: vaccine/infection vs colostrum/antitoxin [1]
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Typhoid fever range: sustained 39–40 °C.
- Typhoid carrier in history: Mary Mallon, "Typhoid Mary".
- Pneumonia sign: lips and fingernails grey to bluish.
- Common cold duration: 3–7 days.
- AIDS first reported: 1981.
- Gap between HIV infection and symptoms: months to 5–10 years.
- Indian AIDS agency: NACO.
- Pathogen overwhelming AIDS patients: *Mycobacterium*, *Toxoplasma* and others.
- Thymus size: large at birth, shrinks with age.
- Spleen: filters blood; reservoir of erythrocytes.
- CT uses: X-rays; MRI uses: strong magnetic fields, non-ionising radiation.
- Hallucinogenic plants: *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura*.
- Adolescence age range: 12–18 years.
- Chronic alcohol damage to liver: cirrhosis.
- Smoking raises blood: carbon monoxide, lowering haem-bound oxygen.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Common cold and lungs
Rhinoviruses infect the nose and respiratory passage, not the lungs.
:::

:::tip Where Plasmodium is fertilised
Fertilisation happens in the mosquito's gut, not in human blood.
:::

:::tip HIV factory vs target
Macrophages are the HIV factory; helper T cells are the cells whose number falls.
:::

:::tip Active vs passive examples
Vaccination is active immunity; colostrum and antitoxin injections are passive.
:::

:::tip Heroin is a depressant
Heroin (opioid) slows body functions; cocaine is the stimulant.
:::

:::tip Allergy antibody
Allergy involves IgE, not IgA (colostrum) or IgG.
:::
