## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Anther (typical):** bilobed, dithecous, tetrasporangiate.
- **Wall layers of microsporangium (out → in):** epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, tapetum.
- **Tapetum:** innermost wall layer; nourishes developing pollen; cells often multinucleate.
- **Microsporogenesis:** formation of microspores from a pollen mother cell by meiosis.
- **Sporopollenin:** material of exine; resists heat, acids, alkalis; no enzyme degrades it.
- **Germ pore:** gap in the exine without sporopollenin; pollen tube emerges here.
- **Intine:** inner pollen wall of cellulose and pectin.
- **Mature pollen grain:** large vegetative cell plus small spindle-shaped generative cell.
- **Pollen shed 2-celled:** in over 60% of angiosperms.
- **Megasporogenesis:** formation of megaspores from the megaspore mother cell by meiosis.
- **MMC position:** micropylar region of the nucellus.
- **Monosporic development:** embryo sac formed from a single functional megaspore.
- **Mature embryo sac:** 7-celled, 8-nucleate.
- **Egg apparatus:** two synergids and one egg cell at the micropylar end.
- **Filiform apparatus:** thickenings at micropylar tip of synergids; guide the pollen tube.
- **Geitonogamy:** pollen to another flower of the same plant; genetically autogamy.
- **Xenogamy:** pollen to a flower of a different plant; brings genetic variation.
- **Cleistogamous flowers:** never open; assured seed set without pollinators.
- **Self-incompatibility:** genetic block to self-pollen germination or tube growth in the pistil.
- **Syngamy:** male gamete + egg → zygote (2n).
- **Triple fusion:** male gamete + two polar nuclei → primary endosperm nucleus (3n).
- **Double fertilisation:** syngamy + triple fusion in one embryo sac.
- **Coconut water:** free-nuclear endosperm.
- **Coconut kernel:** cellular endosperm.
- **Perisperm:** persistent nucellus in the seed (black pepper, beet).
- **Scutellum:** the single lateral cotyledon of a grass embryo.
- **Coleoptile:** sheath over shoot apex in monocot embryo.
- **Coleorhiza:** sheath over radicle and root cap in monocot embryo.
- **False fruit:** thalamus also forms the fruit (apple, strawberry, cashew).
- **Parthenocarpy:** fruit without fertilisation; seedless (banana).
- **Apomixis:** seed without fertilisation; asexual reproduction mimicking sexual.
- **Polyembryony:** more than one embryo in a seed (citrus, mango).

## Key formulas

:::formula Embryo sac count
3 antipodals + 1 central cell + 3 (egg apparatus) = **7 cells**; 3 + 2 polar nuclei + 3 = **8 nuclei**.
:::

:::formula Ploidy in the ovule
Nucellus, integuments, seed coat, pericarp, zygote, embryo = 2n · egg, synergids, antipodals, polar nucleus = n · PEN, endosperm = 3n.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton Embryo sac development (3 marks)
MMC (2n) → meiosis → 4 megaspores; 3 degenerate [1]<br>
Functional megaspore → 3 free-nuclear mitoses → 8 nuclei [1]<br>
Egg apparatus + 3 antipodals + central cell with 2 polar nuclei → 7 cells, 8 nuclei; monosporic [1]
:::

:::skeleton Artificial hybridisation (2 marks)
Emasculation before anther dehiscence (stops selfing) [½] → bagging with butter paper (stops unwanted pollen) [½] → dust chosen pollen on receptive stigma [½] → rebag [½]
:::

:::skeleton Double fertilisation (2 marks)
Pollen tube → micropyle → synergid; releases 2 male gametes [½]<br>
Gamete 1 + egg → zygote 2n (syngamy) [½]; gamete 2 + 2 polar nuclei → PEN 3n (triple fusion) [½]; both in one sac = double fertilisation [½]
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Pollen grain size: about 25–50 micrometres.
- Pollen storage in pollen banks: liquid nitrogen at −196 °C.
- Pollen viability of rice and wheat: about 30 minutes.
- Water pollination: rare, about 30 genera, mostly monocots.
- Water-pollinated examples: *Vallisneria*, *Hydrilla*, *Zostera*.
- Aquatic but insect or wind pollinated: water hyacinth, water lily.
- Bird pollinators named in NCERT: sunbirds and hummingbirds.
- Tallest flower, egg-laying reward: *Amorphophallus*.
- Plants with both chasmogamous and cleistogamous flowers: *Viola*, *Oxalis*, *Commelina*.
- Monoecious (prevents autogamy only): castor, maize.
- Dioecious (prevents autogamy and geitonogamy): papaya.
- Pollen allergy weed: *Parthenium* (carrot grass).
- Albuminous seeds: wheat, maize, barley, castor.
- Non-albuminous seeds: pea, groundnut.
- Seed moisture at maturity: 10–15% by mass.
- Oldest viable seed: *Lupinus arcticus*, about 10,000 years.
- 2000-year-old viable seed: date palm, *Phoenix dactylifera*.
- Tiny seeds in thousands: orchids, *Orobanche*, *Striga*.
- Embryo develops at: micropylar end of the embryo sac.
- Apomixis found in: some Asteraceae and grasses.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip 8-celled embryo sac
Wrong. It is 7-celled and 8-nucleate: the central cell holds two polar nuclei.
:::

:::tip Endosperm ploidy
Endosperm is 3n (triploid), not 2n. Only the zygote and embryo are 2n among fertilisation products.
:::

:::tip Geitonogamy is cross-pollination
Only functionally. Genetically it equals autogamy because pollen comes from the same plant.
:::

:::tip Pollen tube enters the egg
It enters a synergid through the filiform apparatus, after passing the micropyle.
:::

:::tip Coleoptile vs coleorhiza
Coleoptile covers the shoot apex; coleorhiza covers the radicle and root cap.
:::

:::tip Parthenocarpy gives seeds
No: parthenocarpy gives seedless fruit; apomixis gives seeds without fertilisation.
:::

:::tip Emasculation timing
Anthers must be removed before they dehisce, or self-pollen may already be on the stigma.
:::
