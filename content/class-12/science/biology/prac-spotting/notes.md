# Practical · Spotting (study and observe)

In spotting you get about a minute per spot: **identify it, then write two or three identification points**. Marks come from the name plus the points the examiner can tick, so learn each item as "name → features I can see". The eleven items below are the 2026–27 study-and-observe list, grouped by the theory unit they come from.

**2. Pollen germination on the stigma** (permanent slide or electron micrograph). Points: pollen grains on the stigma surface; pollen tubes growing down into the style; tubes come out through germ pores.

**8. Controlled pollination: emasculation, tagging and bagging.**

```ng-steps
id: spot-controlled-pollination
title: Controlled pollination as you would show it
step: **Emasculation**: remove the anthers from a bisexual bud before they dehisce.
step: **Bagging**: cover the emasculated flower with a butter-paper bag to keep stray pollen away.
step: **Pollination**: when the stigma is receptive, dust it with pollen from the chosen male parent.
step: **Rebagging and tagging**: bag the flower again and tie a tag with the parents' names and the date.
```

**In one breath:** Wind flowers are dull with masses of light pollen and feathery stigmas; insect flowers are bright, scented, with nectar; bird flowers are bright with much nectar. Controlled pollination: emasculate, bag, pollinate, rebag and tag.

## Gametes and early embryo

**3a. T.S. of testis** (mammal, e.g. mouse). Points:
- many **seminiferous tubules** cut across;
- inside each, cells in layers: **spermatogonia** at the wall, then **spermatocytes** and **spermatids**, with **sperms** in the lumen;
- **Sertoli cells** among the germ cells; **Leydig (interstitial) cells** in the spaces between tubules.

**3b. T.S. of ovary.** Points:
- **follicles at different stages** in the cortex: primary, secondary, tertiary (with a fluid-filled **antrum**) and a large **Graafian follicle** holding the oocyte;
- a **corpus luteum** may be seen after ovulation;
- connective-tissue **stroma** around them.

**4. Meiosis in onion bud cells or grasshopper testis.** Identify the stage from what the chromosomes are doing:
- **Prophase I**: chromosomes long and paired (bivalents); **chiasmata** visible in later stages.
- **Metaphase I**: **bivalents** line up at the equator.
- **Anaphase I**: **homologous chromosomes** separate and move to opposite poles (sister chromatids stay together).
- **Telophase I / II and anaphase II**: two (then four) groups of chromosomes; in anaphase II sister chromatids separate.

## Inheritance

**6. Mendelian inheritance using seeds** of different colours or sizes (e.g. yellow and green, or round and wrinkled pea seeds). Count each kind in a mixed sample and compare with Mendel's ratios:
- one trait (monohybrid F2): about **3 : 1**;
- two traits (dihybrid F2): about **9 : 3 : 3 : 1**.
Points: name the dominant and recessive forms, give your counts and the ratio they approach.

**7. Pedigree charts of a genetic trait** (tongue rolling, blood groups, ear lobes, widow's peak, colour blindness). Points:
- read the symbols: square = male, circle = female, shaded = affected, double line = consanguineous mating;
- decide the pattern: if affected children have two unaffected parents, the trait is **recessive**; if it appears in every generation, often **dominant**; if mostly males are affected through carrier mothers, **X-linked recessive** (colour blindness);
- tongue rolling, free ear lobes and widow's peak are treated as dominant traits in the lab manual; blood groups show multiple alleles.

**11. Flash cards or models of homologous and analogous organs.** Points: name the pair, say "same structure, different function" (homologous, divergent evolution) or "different structure, same function" (analogous, convergent evolution). Examples: forelimbs of whale, bat, cheetah and human (homologous); wings of butterfly and bird, potato and sweet potato (analogous).

## Disease organisms and symbiosis

**9. Common disease-causing organisms** (slides, models, virtual images or specimens). Write the name, the disease and its symptoms.

```ng-fill
id: spot-pathogens
title: Pathogens, diseases and symptoms
cols: Organism | Disease | Symptoms
row: *Ascaris* (roundworm) | [[ascariasis]] | [[internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anaemia, blockage of the intestinal passage]]
row: *Entamoeba histolytica* (protozoan) | [[amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery)]] | [[constipation, abdominal pain and cramps, stools with excess mucus and blood clots]]
row: *Plasmodium* (protozoan) | [[malaria]] | [[chill and high fever recurring every 3–4 days]]
row: *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, *Epidermophyton* (fungi) | [[ringworm]] | [[dry, scaly lesions on skin, nails and scalp, with intense itching]]
```

Identification points: *Ascaris* — cylindrical, unsegmented worm; the male has a curved tail, the female a straight one. *Entamoeba* — a single cell with pseudopodia. Ringworm fungi — seen as hyphae and spores, or as round, scaly patches on skin.

**10. Symbiosis and parasitism (models or specimens).**
- **Lichen**: a mutualistic association of a **fungus** and an **alga or cyanobacterium**; a crusty or leafy growth on bark or rocks.
- **Root nodules** of a leguminous plant: small swellings on roots containing nitrogen-fixing ***Rhizobium***.
- ***Cuscuta*** on a host: a yellow, leafless, thread-like stem twining around the host; it has no chlorophyll and draws food through **haustoria**; a parasite.

**In one breath:** *Ascaris* → ascariasis; *Entamoeba* → amoebiasis; *Plasmodium* → malaria; ringworm fungi → ringworm. Lichen = fungus + alga (mutualism); root nodules = *Rhizobium*; *Cuscuta* = leafless parasite with haustoria.
