## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Quadrat (sampling plot):** small square plot of fixed size used to sample plants.
- **Quadrat size for herbs:** 1 m × 1 m.
- **Population density:** individuals of a species per unit area.
- **Population frequency:** percentage of quadrats in which a species occurs.
- **Random placement reason:** avoids bias towards thick or thin patches.
- **Boundary plant rule:** count only if more than half is inside.
- **Minimum quadrats for reliability:** about ten.
- **High density, low frequency:** species clumped in a few quadrats.
- **Uniformly spread species:** high frequency.

## Key formulas

:::formula Population density
$\text{Density}=\dfrac{\text{total individuals}}{\text{quadrats studied}}$

When to use: counts of one species in all quadrats. Unit: individuals per m². Common slip: dividing by only the quadrats where the species occurred.
:::

:::formula Population frequency
$\text{Frequency (\%)}=\dfrac{\text{quadrats with species}}{\text{quadrats studied}}\times100$

When to use: presence or absence per quadrat. Unit: %. Common slip: using the number of plants instead of quadrats.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Frequency unit: per cent.
- Density unit: individuals per m² (or per quadrat).
- Quadrats in one study: equal size, random placement.
- Tiger density is estimated from: pugmarks and faecal pellets.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Wrong denominator
Density divides by **all** quadrats studied, including those where the species was absent.
:::

:::tip Plants versus quadrats
Frequency counts **quadrats** in which the species occurred, not the number of plants.
:::
