## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Aim of the experiment:** temporary mount to observe pollen germination.
- **Role of sucrose:** gives energy and the right concentration, as on a stigma.
- **Role of boric acid:** boron helps pollen-tube growth.
- **Pollen tube exit:** through a germ pore of the exine.
- **Germ pore of pollen:** gap in the exine where sporopollenin is absent.
- **Pollen shed stage:** two-celled in over 60% of angiosperms.
- **Tube nucleus position:** near the tip of the growing pollen tube.
- **Male gametes in tube:** two, formed from the generative cell.
- **Moist chamber use:** stops the drop of medium drying out.
- **Good flowers to use:** *Catharanthus*, pea, *Hibiscus*, balsam.
- **Germination time in medium:** about 15–30 minutes.
- **Pollen–pistil interaction:** events from pollen landing to tube entering the ovule.

## Key formulas

:::formula Percentage germination
$\text{Germination (\%)}=\dfrac{\text{germinated}}{\text{total counted}}\times100$

When to use: from the observation-table totals. Unit: %. Common slip: dividing by the ungerminated grains instead of the total.
:::

:::formula Germination medium
About 10% sucrose + a trace (about 0.01%) of boric acid in distilled water.

When to use: writing the materials. Common slip: calling it salt solution.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Pollen tube is an outgrowth of: the intine.
- Exine is made of: sporopollenin.
- Pollen of rice and wheat stays viable for: about 30 minutes.
- Pollen of Rosaceae and Solanaceae stays viable for: months.
- Tube guided into a synergid by: the filiform apparatus.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Wrong concentration
Too much sugar stops germination; too little can burst the grains. Write "about 10% sucrose".
:::

:::tip Too much pollen
A thick layer of pollen hides the tubes; dust lightly.
:::
