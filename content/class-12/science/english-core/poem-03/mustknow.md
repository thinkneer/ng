## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Poet:** John Keats, Romantic poet; lines from the opening of Endymion (1818).
- **Opening claim:** a thing of beauty is a joy for ever.
- **Why for ever:** its loveliness increases; it never passes into nothingness.
- **Gifts of beauty:** a quiet bower, sleep full of sweet dreams, health, quiet breathing.
- **Bower:** a shady shelter in a garden — beauty as refuge.
- **Flowery band:** garland we weave each morning that binds us to the earth (metaphor: beauty ties us to life).
- **What we live despite:** despondence, inhuman dearth of noble natures, gloomy days, unhealthy and over-darkened ways.
- **Pall:** cloth over a coffin — metaphor for gloom that beauty removes from our dark spirits.
- **Things of beauty — nature:** sun, moon, trees old and young, daffodils, clear rills, musk-rose blooms in the forest brake.
- **Shady boon for simple sheep:** trees sheltering the humble.
- **Cooling covert:** streams making a cool shelter against the hot season.
- **Things of beauty — human:** grandeur of the dooms imagined for the mighty dead; lovely tales heard or read.
- **Mighty dead:** heroes of the past whose noble deaths we imagine with awe.
- **Endless fountain:** beauty as immortal drink pouring from heaven's brink — inexhaustible, divine.
- **Form:** rhymed (heroic) couplets, iambic pentameter, enjambment.
- **Devices:** metaphor, imagery, alliteration, antithesis, inversion.
- **Themes:** permanence of beauty, consolation, nature, Romantic faith in beauty.

## Key formulas

:::formula Why beauty is a joy for ever — value points
Loveliness increases with each return · never passes into nothingness · memory keeps and grows it · gives bower, sweet sleep, health, quiet breathing · source is an endless heavenly fountain.
:::

:::formula Flowery band and pall — value points
Flowery band: garland woven daily, binds us to earth = beauty keeps us attached to life · Pall: funeral cloth over our dark spirits = gloom and despair · some shape of beauty moves the pall away.
:::

:::formula Sources of suffering — value points
Despondence · dearth of noble natures · gloomy days · unhealthy, over-darkened ways of seeking happiness · beauty is the remedy that lifts the gloom.
:::

:::formula Catalogue of beauty — value points
Sun, moon · trees sheltering sheep · daffodils in the green world · clear rills, cooling covert · musk-rose in the forest brake · mighty dead's grandeur · lovely tales · all give shelter, coolness, refreshment, inspiration.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- The poem is from Keats's Endymion.
- Beauty gives us a quiet bower and sweet sleep.
- The flowery band binds us to the earth.
- The pall stands for gloom or despair.
- Trees provide a shady boon for simple sheep.
- Clear rills make a cooling covert.
- Musk-roses bloom in the mid-forest brake.
- The mighty dead are remembered for the grandeur of their dooms.
- Beauty is an endless fountain of immortal drink.
- The poem is written in rhymed couplets.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Pall is a funeral cloth
Not a "pal" or a curtain; it covers a coffin, so it means deathly gloom.
:::

:::tip Mighty dead
Heroes of the past and their noble ends, not the poet's dead relatives.
:::

:::tip Increases, not fades
Beauty's loveliness grows in memory; do not write that beauty fades but is remembered.
:::

:::tip Human beauty too
Include tales and the mighty dead in the list; nature alone is incomplete.
:::
