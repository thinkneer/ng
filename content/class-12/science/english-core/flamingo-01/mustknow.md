## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** Alphonse Daudet (French).
- **Setting:** a village school in Alsace after France lost the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71).
- **Order from Berlin:** only German to be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine.
- **Narrator:** Franz, a schoolboy who dreaded participles and loved the outdoors.
- **M. Hamel:** French master of the village for forty years; leaving the next day.
- **Franz's temptations:** warm morning, chirping birds, Prussians drilling behind the sawmill.
- **Bulletin board:** source of all bad news for two years — battles lost, the draft, orders.
- **Wachter:** blacksmith who told Franz there was no hurry to reach school.
- **Changes Franz noticed:** silence like Sunday, M. Hamel's Sunday clothes, villagers on the back benches.
- **M. Hamel's Sunday clothes:** green coat, frilled shirt, embroidered black silk cap.
- **Back benchers:** old Hauser with his primer, the former mayor, the former postmaster.
- **Franz's regret:** lost lessons spent seeking birds' eggs and sliding on the Saar.
- **Blame for neglect:** parents (sent children to farms and mills), children (put off learning), M. Hamel himself (flower-watering, fishing holidays).
- **M. Hamel on French:** the most beautiful, clearest, most logical language; guard it.
- **Key to the prison:** an enslaved people who keep their language keep their way to freedom.
- **New copies:** words France, Alsace in round hand, like little flags.
- **Pigeons:** Franz wonders if even they will be made to sing in German — linguistic chauvinism.
- **Hauser's moment:** spelling ba, be, bi, bo, bu with the little ones, voice trembling.
- **Ending:** noon Angelus, Prussian trumpets; M. Hamel writes "Vive La France!" and dismisses the class by a gesture.
- **Theme words:** language and identity, procrastination, patriotism, linguistic chauvinism.

## Key formulas

:::formula Changes Franz noticed — value points
Silence instead of bustle · M. Hamel gentle, not scolding · Sunday clothes · villagers on back benches · Hauser with an old primer → signified the last French lesson and a farewell to the teacher and the lost province.
:::

:::formula Franz's change of heart — value points
Before: hated school, dreaded participles, feared the ruler · After the announcement: books feel like old friends, regrets truancy, pities and admires M. Hamel, understands the lesson easily · Cause: the sudden loss of what he took for granted.
:::

:::formula Language as key to the prison — value points
Language = identity, culture, memory · Conqueror attacks language to erase a people · Holding on to it keeps freedom alive inside · Hence M. Hamel's plea to guard French · Example: writing lesson with France, Alsace.
:::

:::formula M. Hamel's character — value points
Devoted (40 years) · strict but fair (ruler, yet kind on the last day) · honest and self-critical (blames himself) · passionate about French · dignified in grief (Vive La France!, gesture) · a true patriot.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Franz was late and had not learnt the rule for participles.
- The order came from Berlin.
- M. Hamel had taught in the village for forty years.
- Old Hauser brought an old primer to the class.
- M. Hamel called French the clearest and most logical language.
- The new copies carried the words France and Alsace.
- The clock struck twelve and the Angelus sounded before the trumpets.
- M. Hamel's last words were written, not spoken.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Who issued the order
Berlin (Prussia), not Paris; Alsace and Lorraine were taken from France, not given to it.
:::

:::tip Villagers' reason
They came to thank M. Hamel and honour their lost country, and because they regretted not learning; not to protest or to see the new master.
:::

:::tip Blame was shared
M. Hamel blamed parents and children and himself; answers that name only the children lose a mark.
:::

:::tip Ending detail
M. Hamel wrote "Vive La France!" on the blackboard and dismissed the class with a gesture; he did not make a speech.
:::
