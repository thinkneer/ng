## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Part I author:** Christopher Silvester, from his introduction to The Penguin Book of Interviews.
- **Age of the interview:** about 130 years; a commonplace of journalism.
- **Favourable view:** a source of truth and, in practice, an art.
- **Hostile view:** an unwarranted intrusion that diminishes the subject, like a photograph stealing the soul.
- **V. S. Naipaul:** some people are wounded by interviews and lose a part of themselves.
- **Lewis Carroll:** had a just horror of the interviewer; never consented.
- **Rudyard Kipling:** interviewing is immoral, a crime, an assault; yet he interviewed Mark Twain.
- **Caroline Kipling's diary:** Boston, 1892 — two reporters ruined the day.
- **H. G. Wells:** called it an ordeal (1894); interviewed Stalin in 1934.
- **Saul Bellow:** interviews felt like thumbprints on his windpipe.
- **Denis Brian:** news reaches us through one person asking another questions; the interviewer has unprecedented power.
- **Silvester's verdict:** a supremely serviceable medium of communication.
- **Part II:** Mukund Padmanabhan (The Hindu) interviews Umberto Eco.
- **Eco's post:** professor at the University of Bologna, scholar of semiotics.
- **Eco's output:** over forty scholarly works, five novels, children's books, newspaper columns.
- **Interstices:** empty spaces of time (visitor in the lift) used for writing.
- **Universe image:** without its empty spaces the universe would shrink to a fist.
- **One set of interests:** same philosophical and ethical concerns across all his writing.
- **Dissertation at 22:** told the story of his research with trials and errors; professor praised and published it.
- **Lesson learnt:** scholarly books can be written by telling a story — hence his narrative style.
- **Novels at fifty:** started almost by accident, with nothing to do one day.
- **Self-image:** an academic who writes novels at the weekend; attends academic conferences, not Pen clubs.
- **The Name of the Rose:** 1980; detective story with metaphysics, theology and medieval history; sold 10–15 million.
- **Publisher's expectation:** about three thousand copies in Italy.
- **Eco on the success:** a mystery; perhaps timing; readers want difficult experiences too.

## Key formulas

:::formula Views on the interview — value points
For: source of truth, an art, serviceable medium, interviewer's power (Brian) · Against: intrusion, diminishes, wounds (Naipaul), horror (Carroll), assault (Kipling), ordeal (Wells), windpipe (Bellow) · Paradox: Kipling and Wells interviewed others.
:::

:::formula Eco's productivity — value points
Works in interstices (lift example) · universe-to-fist image · same interests in many forms · narrative scholarship since the dissertation · novels at fifty by accident · academic first, novelist second.
:::

:::formula Success of The Name of the Rose — value points
Serious detective novel with theology and medieval history · expected 3,000 copies, sold 10–15 million · Eco calls it a mystery · timing — ten years earlier or later might have failed · readers also want difficult books.
:::

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Who said what
Naipaul — wounds; Carroll — horror; Kipling — assault; Wells — ordeal; Bellow — windpipe. Mixing them costs the mark.
:::

:::tip Interstices
Empty spaces of time, not of place; give the lift example.
:::

:::tip Dissertation lesson
The professor praised Eco for telling the story of his research; it was not a criticism.
:::

:::tip Eco's identity
He calls himself an academic first; do not describe him mainly as a novelist.
:::
