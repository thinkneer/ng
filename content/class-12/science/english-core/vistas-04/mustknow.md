## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** Pearl S. Buck, American Nobel laureate who grew up in China.
- **Setting:** Japan's coast during the Second World War.
- **Dr Sadao Hoki:** Japanese surgeon trained in America; famous; perfecting a discovery to clean wounds.
- **Why not at the front:** the old General might need surgery; his research was valuable.
- **Hana:** Sadao's wife, met in America; he waited to be sure she was Japanese; marriage arranged the old way.
- **Household:** two children; servants — gardener, cook, Yumi the nurse.
- **The prisoner:** wounded white man washed ashore; gunshot wound in the back; cap marked U.S. Navy; name Tom.
- **The dilemma:** shelter him = treason; hand him over or push him back = his death.
- **First decision:** operate, then hand him to the police when well.
- **Yumi:** refused to wash the white man; Hana washed him herself.
- **Gardener's warning:** healing what the gun and sea did would bring revenge on them all.
- **Servants' exit:** left the house within a week, without informing on the family.
- **The operation:** bullet lodged near the kidney, removed; Hana assisted and vomited.
- **The letter:** to the police chief, written but never sent.
- **The General:** self-centred; trusted only Sadao as surgeon; promised private assassins.
- **Three nights:** Sadao lay awake; prisoner alive each morning; assassins never came.
- **Why no assassins:** the General forgot, absorbed in his own illness; asked Sadao not to mention it.
- **Escape plan:** boat with food and clothes; row to the uninhabited island; wait for a Korean fishing boat.
- **Flashlight signals:** two flashes = needs food; one flash = safe; none = gone.
- **Disguise:** Japanese clothes, black cloth over blond hair.
- **Ending:** Sadao recalls prejudice in America, calls white faces repulsive, yet asks why he could not kill him.
- **Themes:** humanity vs nationalism, duty vs conscience, prejudice on both sides, self-interest, courage.

## Key formulas

:::formula Sadao's dilemma — value points
Doctor: a wounded man must be saved · Citizen: an enemy POW must be reported · Options: hand over (death), push back (death), shelter (treason) · Chooses to operate first · delays the letter · confesses to the General · finally helps him escape.
:::

:::formula The escape — value points
Boat on the shore at night · food, water, extra clothing · row to the unpatrolled island · wait for a Korean fishing boat · flashlight: two = food, one = safe · Japanese clothes, cloth over hair · no signal → gone.
:::

:::formula The General — value points
Old, ill, dependent on Sadao · pure self-interest · promises assassins · forgets them · admits dereliction · asks for silence · irony: his selfishness saves two lives.
:::

:::formula Hana — value points
Educated in America yet traditional · fears treason yet supports Sadao · washes the prisoner when Yumi refuses · assists the operation, vomits, returns · faces the servants · loyal, brave, compassionate.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Sadao studied surgery in America from the age of twenty-two.
- The prisoner's cap bore the letters U.S. Navy.
- The bullet was lodged near the prisoner's kidney.
- Yumi refused to wash the prisoner.
- The servants left the house within a week.
- The General promised to send private assassins.
- The assassins never came because the General forgot.
- The prisoner was to wait on the island for a Korean fishing boat.
- Two flashes of the torch meant he needed food.
- The prisoner's name was Tom.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Sadao's prejudice remains
At the end he still dislikes Americans; the humanity is in his actions, not a conversion.
:::

:::tip Why no assassins
The General forgot; he did not change his mind out of mercy.
:::

:::tip The servants did not inform
They left out of fear and disapproval but stayed loyal enough to keep silent.
:::

:::tip Signals
Two flashes = food needed, one = safe; do not reverse them.
:::
