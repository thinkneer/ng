## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** Selma Lagerlöf, Swedish, first woman Nobel laureate in literature.
- **Protagonist:** a nameless vagabond who sells wire rattraps, begs and steals.
- **Rattrap theory:** the world is a rattrap baiting people with riches, joys, food, shelter, heat, clothes.
- **Why the theory pleased him:** the world had been unkind; thinking ill of it and imagining others trapped consoled him.
- **The crofter:** old man, alone, former Ramsjö worker, lives off his cow.
- **Crofter's hospitality:** hot porridge, tobacco, a game of cards, talk.
- **Thirty kronor:** three ten-kronor notes in a leather pouch on a nail by the window frame.
- **The theft:** peddler returned, broke the window pane, took the notes, hung the pouch back.
- **The forest:** avoided the highway, walked in circles, lost; realised he was caught in the rattrap.
- **Ramsjö Ironworks:** followed the hammer sound; master smith ignored him; ironmaster on his nightly round.
- **Mistaken identity:** ironmaster took him for Captain von Stahle, an old regimental comrade.
- **First refusal:** peddler feared exposure and the sheriff; declined but did not correct the error.
- **Edla Willmansson:** ironmaster's daughter; not pretty, modest, compassionate; noticed his fear.
- **Edla's promise:** stay for Christmas Eve only; free to leave whenever he likes.
- **Truth revealed:** at breakfast in daylight, after bath, shave and the ironmaster's suit.
- **Ironmaster's reaction:** called him a cheat, wanted the sheriff.
- **Peddler's defence:** never claimed to be a captain; the world is a rattrap with fine baits.
- **Edla's plea:** let him have one day of peace; she had invited him; keep Christmas.
- **At church:** news that a crofter was robbed of thirty kronor by a rattrap seller.
- **The gift:** a rattrap with three ten-kronor notes and a letter for Miss Willmansson.
- **The letter:** thanks for treating him like a captain; return the money to the crofter; signed Captain von Stahle.
- **Raised to captain:** treated with dignity, he gained the power to clear himself.
- **Themes:** world as rattrap, compassion redeems, loneliness, appearance vs reality.

## Key formulas

:::formula Rattrap theory — value points
World = big rattrap · baits: riches, joys, food, shelter, heat, clothing · touch the bait → trap closes · pleasure: bitter revenge on an unkind world, picturing others trapped · tested when he steals and gets lost.
:::

:::formula Edla's role — value points
Notices his fear yet speaks kindly · promises freedom to leave · persuades him to come · pleads for one day of peace · gives the suit as a gift, invites him back · her trust awakens his dignity → money returned, letter signed as captain.
:::

:::formula Crofter vs Edla's kindness — value points
Crofter: generous but careless trust, shows the money · peddler repays with theft · Edla: trust given knowingly to a frightened man · peddler repays with honesty → kindness that sees the person transforms.
:::

:::formula Humour and irony — value points
Thief lectures host on the rattrap · ironmaster fooled by firelight · grumbles about the silver · the "captain" is a vagabond · the vagabond behaves like a captain · gift of a rattrap to Edla.
:::

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Nameless peddler
Do not call the peddler "Nils Olof" or "Captain von Stahle" as his name; those belong to the mistaken identity.
:::

:::tip Who persuaded him
The ironmaster failed; Edla succeeded. Keep the two invitations separate.
:::

:::tip The forest realisation
He felt caught in the rattrap in the forest, before the ironworks, not after being exposed.
:::

:::tip Church, not sheriff
The ironmaster learnt of the crofter's robbery at church; no sheriff was ever called.
:::
