## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Poet:** Pablo Neruda, Chilean, Nobel laureate (1971).
- **The request:** count to twelve, then keep still — no speaking, no moving arms.
- **Twelve:** hours on a clock, months of a year — a complete cycle, a small measurable pause.
- **No language:** languages divide; silence unites.
- **Not moving arms:** stop the restless activity that does harm.
- **Exotic moment:** rare, never experienced — without rush or engines.
- **Sudden strangeness:** the shared feeling that would unite everyone for once.
- **Fishermen:** would stop harming whales — end of exploitation of nature.
- **Man gathering salt:** would look at his hurt hands — the worker finally notices his own wounds.
- **Green wars:** wars against the environment / wars made to look new.
- **Wars with gas, with fire:** chemical and conventional weapons.
- **Victory with no survivors:** total war in which winning is meaningless.
- **Clean clothes:** free of blood and guilt.
- **Walk with brothers in the shade:** enemies become brothers at rest.
- **Not total inactivity:** the poet wants no truck with death; life is what it is about.
- **Single-minded:** obsessed with keeping life moving; never understand ourselves.
- **The sadness:** of never understanding ourselves and threatening ourselves with death.
- **Earth's lesson:** seems dead in winter, proves alive in spring — stillness hides life.
- **Ending:** count to twelve again, keep quiet, the poet goes.
- **Devices:** symbolism, imagery, paradox/irony, repetition, antithesis, free verse, direct address.
- **Themes:** introspection, peace, brotherhood, environment, critique of haste, renewal.

## Key formulas

:::formula Significance of twelve — value points
Clock hours · months of the year · a complete cycle · short, measurable, achievable · frames the poem at start and end.
:::

:::formula The two scenes — value points
Fishermen stop harming whales = exploitation of nature halts · salt-gatherer looks at hurt hands = the exploited worker notices himself · ceaseless activity hurts others and self · stillness brings awareness.
:::

:::formula The wars stanza — value points
Green wars (environment) · gas and fire (chemical, conventional) · victory with no survivors (total war, hollow win) · clean clothes (no blood, no guilt) · brothers in the shade (peace, brotherhood).
:::

:::formula Stillness vs death — value points
Not total inactivity · no truck with death · life is the point · we are single-minded about motion · never understand ourselves · Earth: dead in winter, alive in spring · pause = renewal.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Pablo Neruda was a Chilean poet.
- The poet asks us to count to twelve.
- The moment of stillness would be without engines.
- Fishermen would stop harming whales.
- The salt-gatherer would look at his hurt hands.
- The warriors would put on clean clothes.
- The poet does not want total inactivity.
- The Earth teaches that apparent death hides life.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Stillness is not death
The poet explicitly rejects total inactivity; answers that say he wants the world to stop forever are wrong.
:::

:::tip Green wars
Give the environmental reading; add the "fresh/new wars" reading as a second meaning.
:::

:::tip Twelve
Clock and calendar, not an arbitrary number; mention the completed cycle.
:::

:::tip Who is "we"
The poet includes himself and the reader; the poem is an invitation to all humanity, not a scolding.
:::
