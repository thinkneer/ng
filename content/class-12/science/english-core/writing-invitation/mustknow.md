# Invitations and replies — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates: ★★★ most years · ★★ often · ★ sometimes. Marks in square brackets; about 50 words.

1. ★★★ Formal card: school Annual Day / inauguration / prize distribution / seminar, with chief guest and RSVP. [4]
2. ★★ Formal card: wedding or family function from the parents of the bride/groom. [4]
3. ★★ Informal invitation to a friend: birthday, housewarming, family function, trip. [4]
4. ★★ Formal reply accepting or regretting an invitation (as a parent, teacher or guest). [4]
5. ★ Informal reply to a friend, accepting or declining with a reason. [4]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Formal invitation:** a boxed, centred card in third person, about 50 words, no signature.
- **Informal invitation:** a short friendly letter in first person with address, date, salutation, closing.
- **Formula of invitation:** "request(s) the pleasure of your company" or "cordially invite(s) you".
- **Card details:** host, occasion, day and date, time, venue, chief guest, RSVP.
- **RSVP:** reply requested; name and contact at the bottom left of a card.
- **Formal reply:** third person, boxed; thanks, occasion, accept or regret, reason, good wishes.
- **Informal reply:** first person letter; thanks, clear yes/no, brief reason, warm close.
- **Regret formula:** "regret(s) their inability to attend owing to…".
- **Acceptance formula:** "will be happy to attend" / "accept with pleasure".
- **Marks split:** format 1, content 2, expression 1.

## Key formulas

:::formula Formal invitation card
Host (who) → *request the pleasure of your company* → occasion → day, date, time → venue → chief guest → RSVP (name, contact). Centred, boxed, third person, no salutation, no signature.
:::

:::formula Informal invitation letter
Sender's address → date → *Dear …,* → body (occasion, date, time, venue, request to come / confirm) → *Your friend / Yours lovingly,* → name. First person, warm.
:::

:::formula Formal reply (accept / regret)
*Mr and Mrs X thank … for the kind invitation to [occasion] on [date]* → *and will be happy to attend* **or** *regret their inability to attend owing to [reason]* → good wishes. Third person, boxed.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton 4-mark formal card
1. Box; host line(s) centred — format ½<br>
2. Formula line in third person; RSVP at bottom left — format ½<br>
3. Occasion + day, date, time — content 1<br>
4. Venue + chief guest / special feature — content 1<br>
5. Dignified tone, no I/we, correct grammar, ~50 words — expression 1
:::

:::skeleton 4-mark reply
1. Correct shell (card in third person / letter in first person) — format 1<br>
2. Thanks + name the occasion and date — content 1<br>
3. Clear acceptance or regret with brief reason; good wishes — content 1<br>
4. Tone matching the invitation, correct grammar, ~50 words — expression 1
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- A formal invitation card is written in the third person.
- A formal card has no salutation and no signature.
- RSVP stands for a request to reply; it goes bottom left.
- An informal invitation is a short letter in the first person.
- A formal reply is also in the third person and boxed.
- A regret reply gives a brief reason and good wishes.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Signing a card
"Yours sincerely" or a name at the end of a formal card is a format error; end with RSVP.
:::

:::tip Mixing persons
"We request the pleasure of your company" mixes first and third person; use "The Principal and staff request…".
:::

:::tip Reply that forgets the occasion
A reply must name the function and date it is replying to; a bare "thank you, I will come" loses a content mark.
:::

:::tip Missing time or venue
Every card needs day, date, time and venue; invent a plausible one if the question is silent.
:::
