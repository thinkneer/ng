# Invitations and replies — four formats, one set of rules

In Section B you choose between a **notice and an invitation** for 4 marks. Invitations come in two shapes — the **formal card** and the **informal letter** — and the question may instead ask for a **reply** (accepting or regretting). Each has its own look, but the content checklist is the same every time: *who is inviting, whom, to what, when, where*, and for replies, *yes or no, and why*. This chapter gives you all four so you can take the question whatever form it comes in.

## Formal invitation: the printed card

A formal invitation is what a school, a company or a family sends for a function: an annual day, an inauguration, a seminar, a wedding. It is written **in the third person** ("The Principal and staff request the pleasure of…"), **in a box**, with the lines **centred** and no signature at the end. It has **no date of writing** and no salutation — it is a card, not a letter. The word limit is about **50 words**.

The parts, top to bottom:

1. **Who is inviting** — the name of the host (institution or family).
2. **The formula of invitation** — *request(s) the pleasure of your company / cordially invite(s) you* …
3. **The occasion** — *at the inauguration of … / on the occasion of … / to the Annual Day celebrations.*
4. **Date, time, venue** — each on its own line or in one clear sentence.
5. **Chief guest or special feature** (if given in the question).
6. **RSVP** — name and phone/designation of the person to reply to, bottom left.

:::skeleton Formal invitation card — annotated model
**The Principal, Staff and Students** ← host
**of Springdale Senior Secondary School, Lucknow** ← host's institution
**request the pleasure of your company** ← formula, third person
**at the Annual Day celebrations** ← occasion
**on Saturday, 20 December 2026, at 5 p.m.** ← date and time
**in the school auditorium.** ← venue
**Chief Guest: Dr Meera Nair, Vice-Chancellor, State University** ← chief guest if given
RSVP ← bottom left
Cultural Secretary
98xxxxxx10
:::

Everything is in third person: *your company* is allowed (it is the fixed formula), but never "we invite you" or "I".

:::tip No signature on a card
A formal card ends with RSVP, not with "Yours sincerely" or a name. Adding a signature or a salutation ("Dear Sir") is a format error.
:::

## Informal invitation: a short letter

An informal invitation is written to a friend or relative for a birthday, a party, a family function, a trip. It takes the shape of a **short personal letter**: sender's address, date, salutation (*Dear Anita,*), body in **first person** with a warm tone, and a closing (*Yours lovingly / Your friend,* and the name). Word limit about **50 words** for the body.

The body does three things: tells the occasion warmly, gives date, time and venue, and asks the person to come (sometimes with a request to confirm). Contractions and a friendly voice are fine; slang and emojis are not.

:::skeleton Informal invitation — annotated model
24, Lake View Road ← sender's address
Bhopal 462001
14 November 2026 ← date
Dear Rohan, ← salutation
I'm turning eighteen on 28 November and my parents are hosting a small dinner at home at 7 p.m. that evening. It would mean a lot to me if you could come. Do let me know by the 25th so that I can plan the seating. ← occasion, date, time, venue, request to confirm
Your friend,
Isha ← closing and name
:::

## Replies: accepting or regretting

A reply mirrors the invitation. A **formal reply** is in the third person, in a box, with no salutation and no signature: *Mr and Mrs Sharma thank the Principal for the kind invitation to … and will be happy to attend* (acceptance), or *…regret their inability to attend owing to a prior engagement* (regret). An **informal reply** is a short letter in the first person, warm, giving the yes or the no with a brief reason and a good wish.

Key points for any reply:
- Begin by **thanking** for the invitation and **naming the occasion** (the examiner checks that you have read the invitation).
- Give a **clear yes or no**. For a regret, give a **brief, polite reason** (prior commitment, travel, exams) — never a long story.
- For acceptance, end with a line of anticipation (*look forward to being present*). For regret, end with good wishes for the event.

:::skeleton Formal regret — annotated model
Mr and Mrs Verma thank the Principal and staff of Springdale Senior Secondary School for the kind invitation to the Annual Day celebrations on 20 December 2026. ← thanks + occasion + date
They regret their inability to attend owing to a prior commitment out of town. ← clear no + brief reason
They wish the function every success. ← good wishes
:::

## How the 4 marks are given

- **Format — 1 mark:** card or letter layout matching the type asked; third vs first person; box and RSVP for formal; address, date, salutation and closing for informal.
- **Content — 2 marks:** host, occasion, date, time, venue (and chief guest/RSVP if given); for replies, thanks + occasion + decision + reason.
- **Expression — 1 mark:** grammar, spelling, appropriate tone (formal and dignified vs warm and personal), about 50 words.

:::tip Read "formal" or "informal" in the question
The question always says which it wants, or makes it obvious (Principal → formal; friend → informal). Writing a letter when a card was asked loses the format mark and often a content mark too, because the formula lines are missing.
:::

:::tip Day and date together
Formal cards write *Saturday, 20 December 2026*. Giving only "20th" or forgetting the time is a content deduction; invent a plausible time if the question does not give one.
:::

## Exam routine

Underline in the question: *who invites, whom, occasion, date, time, venue, chief guest, RSVP/reply* — and whether it is formal or informal, invitation or reply. Write the body in about 50 words, then fit it into the card (centred lines) or the letter shell. Check person (third/first) and the word limit. Four minutes.

For the official pattern see the CBSE 2026–27 sample paper on [cbseacademic.nic.in](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/).

**In one breath:** underline the details, pick the shell, write 50 words, check the person and the limit.
