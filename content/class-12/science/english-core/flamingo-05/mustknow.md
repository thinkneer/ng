## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** Louis Fischer, American journalist; from *The Life of Mahatma Gandhi*.
- **Year and place:** 1917, Champaran district, Bihar, near Nepal.
- **Rajkumar Shukla:** poor, illiterate but resolute sharecropper who followed Gandhi till he agreed.
- **Meeting point:** Congress session at Lucknow, December 1916.
- **Shukla's trail:** Lucknow → Cawnpore → Ahmedabad → Calcutta → Patna.
- **Rajendra Prasad's servants:** took Gandhi for a peasant or an untouchable; no water from the well.
- **Muzzafarpur hosts:** Professor J. B. Kripalani (station welcome), Professor Malkani (lodging).
- **Gandhi to the lawyers:** courts are useless for fear-stricken peasants; they need freedom from fear.
- **Sharecropping clause:** 15 per cent of land under indigo, whole indigo harvest as rent.
- **Synthetic indigo:** developed in Germany; made natural indigo unprofitable.
- **Compensation:** landlords charged peasants to release them from the 15 per cent clause.
- **Tirhut commissioner:** bullied Gandhi, advised him to leave.
- **Motihari:** capital of Champaran; Gandhi's base.
- **Order to quit:** served after Gandhi set out on an elephant to see a maltreated peasant; he wrote that he would disobey.
- **Courthouse crowd:** thousands of peasants; officials powerless; Gandhi helped regulate the crowd.
- **Plea in court:** guilty; conflict of duties; obeyed the higher law of conscience.
- **Bail:** refused; released without bail; judgement postponed.
- **Lawyers' vow:** to follow Gandhi into jail; Gandhi: battle of Champaran is won.
- **Case dropped:** by order of the Lieutenant-Governor.
- **Inquiry:** depositions from about ten thousand peasants; Sir Edward Gait; official commission with Gandhi as peasants' representative.
- **Refund:** Gandhi asked 50 per cent, accepted the landlords' 25 per cent.
- **Why 25 per cent:** money less important than landlords surrendering money and prestige; peasants learnt courage.
- **Result:** within a few years planters abandoned estates; indigo sharecropping ended.
- **Social work:** schools in six villages; Kasturba on cleanliness and sanitation; Dr Dev for six months.
- **Volunteers:** Mahadev Desai, Narhari Parikh and wives, Devdas.
- **Self-reliance:** declined C. F. Andrews's help — no Englishman as a prop for a free India.

## Key formulas

:::formula Why Gandhi accepted 25% — value points
Amount less important than principle · landlords forced to surrender money and prestige · peasants saw they had rights and defenders · fear broken, courage learnt · planters left within years · system ended.
:::

:::formula Champaran as turning point — value points
First civil disobedience in India · ordinary peasants defied British power · lawyers chose jail over fees · officials powerless before a peaceful crowd · Gandhi's model: facts, presence, non-violence, social uplift · Indians learnt fearlessness and self-reliance.
:::

:::formula Gandhi's leadership — value points
Listens to the humblest (Shukla) · gathers facts before acting · fearless but courteous · wins over lawyers · accepts practical settlement · stays to build schools and health · refuses outside props.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Gandhi met Rajkumar Shukla at the Lucknow Congress of 1916.
- Champaran is in Bihar, near Nepal.
- Peasants had to grow indigo on 15 per cent of their land.
- Synthetic indigo came from Germany.
- Motihari was the capital of Champaran.
- Gandhi pleaded guilty in court.
- About ten thousand peasants gave depositions.
- Gandhi accepted a 25 per cent refund.
- Schools were opened in six villages.
- Dr Dev volunteered for six months.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Who taught whom
Gandhi chided the lawyers for taking fees; later they vowed to go to jail. Give both halves when asked about the lawyers.
:::

:::tip 50 vs 25
Gandhi asked for 50 per cent and accepted 25; do not swap the figures.
:::

:::tip The case was dropped
The Lieutenant-Governor ordered the case withdrawn; Gandhi was never sentenced.
:::

:::tip Andrews was a friend
Gandhi refused C. F. Andrews's help not out of dislike but to keep the struggle self-reliant.
:::
