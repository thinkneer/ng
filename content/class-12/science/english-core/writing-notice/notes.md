# Notice — the 4-mark format you can get fully right

A notice is the easiest 4 marks in the English paper, because the examiner is checking a *format* and a *checklist*, not your imagination. In the 2026–27 pattern Section B gives you a choice: write **a notice or an invitation** (4 marks). Most students pick the notice. Learn the box once, practise it five times, and these marks are yours before the exam even starts.

## What a notice is and who reads it

A notice is a short public announcement put up on a board (school, office, housing society, club) so that a *group* of people gets one message: an event is happening, something is lost or found, a meeting is called, a change is announced. Because many people will read it quickly, a notice is **brief, formal and complete**: every reader should know *what* is happening, *when*, *where*, *who* it is for and *whom to contact* — without reading it twice.

In the exam you are given a situation ("You are the Cultural Secretary of your school. Write a notice inviting students to…"). You write **as that person**, in the box format, in about **50 words** (the word limit applies to the body only; the heading, date, title and signature block are not counted).

**In one breath:** a notice is a 50-word public announcement in a box; one message, every detail a reader needs, no chat.

## The format, part by part

Draw a box. Inside the box, in this order:

1. **Name of the issuing institution / organisation** — centred at the top (e.g. the school's name, the society's name). Never your own name here.
2. **The word NOTICE** — centred, in capitals.
3. **Date** — on the left, under NOTICE (write it in full: 14 November 2026).
4. **Title / heading** — a short phrase in capitals or bold that says what the notice is about (INTER-HOUSE DEBATE COMPETITION, LOST: SCHOOL BAG).
5. **Body** — one paragraph of about 50 words that answers the five Ws: *what, when, where, who (for whom), whom to contact*. Write in third person or passive voice; never use "I" or "you".
6. **Signature block** — your name, then your designation (Head Boy / Cultural Secretary / Secretary, RWA), one below the other, on the left. Use the name given in the question; if none is given, use a neutral one like "XYZ".

:::skeleton Notice — annotated model
**ABC PUBLIC SCHOOL, DELHI** ← issuing body, centred

**NOTICE** ← the word itself, centred

14 November 2026 ← date in full, left side

**INTER-HOUSE DEBATE COMPETITION** ← title in caps: what the notice is about

All students of Classes XI and XII are informed that an inter-house debate competition will be held on 28 November 2026 in the school auditorium from 9 a.m. to 12 noon. The topic is "Online learning should replace classrooms". Interested students may register with the undersigned by 21 November. ← body: what (debate), when (date, time), where (auditorium), who (XI–XII), contact + deadline — about 50 words

Rohan Mehta ← name
Cultural Secretary ← designation
:::

**In one breath:** issuing body → NOTICE → date → title → 50-word body with the five Ws → name and designation; all inside a box.

## Writing the body: the five Ws in one paragraph

Open with a formula sentence that fixes the audience and the event in one line:

- *All students are hereby informed that…* (announcing)
- *…is organising a … on … at …* (event)
- *A … has been lost/found in … on …* (lost and found)
- *A meeting of … will be held on … at … to discuss …* (meeting)

Then add the details in a natural order: date, time, venue, who may take part, what to bring or how to register, the deadline, and the contact ("the undersigned" is the standard phrase for yourself). End with a short closing line if words allow: *For further details, contact the undersigned.*

:::tip Keep it impersonal
Writing "I request you all to come" or "You should register soon" loses expression marks. A notice speaks for the institution: "Students are requested to…", "Interested students may…". No "I", no "you", no slang, no exclamation marks.
:::

:::tip Count the body only
About 50 words means 45–55. The heading, date, title and signature are not counted. Going to 70–80 words is the commonest reason a neat notice gets 3 instead of 4: the examiner sees a paragraph instead of a notice.
:::

**In one breath:** one formal paragraph, third person, five Ws plus a contact, about 50 words.

## How the 4 marks are given

The marking is a split, and knowing it tells you where to spend your attention:

- **Format — 1 mark:** the box, the issuing body, the word NOTICE, date, title, and name + designation. Miss two of these and the mark goes.
- **Content — 2 marks:** the five Ws. Each missing W costs you; invented details are fine as long as they are *plausible* and *consistent* (a school event does not run at 11 p.m.).
- **Expression — 1 mark:** grammar, spelling, formal tone, coherence; no "I/you", no cutting-pasting of the question wording with errors.

Because format is only 1 mark but content is 2, the biggest gain is the *checklist*: before you move on, tick the five Ws against your body paragraph.

**In one breath:** format 1, content 2, expression 1 — the five Ws are worth half the question.

## The common question types

- **Event / competition / camp:** debate, quiz, blood donation camp, tree plantation, cleanliness drive, book fair, farewell, annual day, excursion. Details: date, time, venue, eligibility, registration, deadline.
- **Lost and found:** what was lost/found, description (colour, contents, marks), where and when, whom to return it to / claim it from. Keep the description short and specific.
- **Meeting:** of the Eco Club, Student Council, residents of the society; date, time, venue, agenda ("to discuss…"), request to attend.
- **Change / information:** change in school timings, closure for holidays, new library rules. State the change, the date it takes effect, and whom to contact.
- **Appeal:** donations for flood relief, volunteers for a drive. State the cause, what to give, where and by when.

:::tip The question tells you who you are
Read the role in the question twice — "Secretary of the Eco Club", "Head Girl", "Secretary, Residents' Welfare Association". Your designation in the signature must match it exactly, and the issuing body at the top must fit (club → school name; RWA → society name). A mismatch is a format error the examiner spots in one second.
:::

**In one breath:** event, lost/found, meeting, change, appeal — same box, different body; match your role to the question.

## Working with the word limit in the exam

Plan for about 4 minutes. Draw the box last (write the content, then box it, so the box fits). Write the five Ws as a quick margin note first: *what – when – where – who – contact*. Then write one paragraph that uses all five. Read it once for "I/you" and for the date and time. Done.

For the official pattern and a model question, see the CBSE 2026–27 sample paper on [cbseacademic.nic.in](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/).

**In one breath:** margin note of five Ws → one paragraph → box → check tone and numbers; four minutes, four marks.
