## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Poet:** Adrienne Rich, American poet and feminist.
- **Form:** three quatrains in rhyming couplets (aabb).
- **The tigers:** embroidered on a screen (panel) by Aunt Jennifer.
- **Prance:** move proudly and playfully — confidence and freedom.
- **Bright topaz:** golden-yellow gem — the tigers' brilliant colour and vitality.
- **Denizens of a world of green:** inhabitants of the jungle — their own free world.
- **Do not fear the men beneath the tree:** fearless of male power, unlike the aunt.
- **Chivalric certainty:** knightly confidence — noble, brave, graceful.
- **Fingers fluttering through wool:** nervous, weak, trapped-bird movement.
- **Ivory needle hard to pull:** even a light needle is an effort — physical and emotional exhaustion.
- **Massive weight of Uncle's wedding band:** burden of marriage and male domination; she is his possession.
- **Terrified hands:** transferred epithet — the aunt is terrified, not her hands.
- **Ringed with ordeals she was mastered by:** even in death the trials of marriage mark and dominate her.
- **Tigers go on prancing, proud and unafraid:** art outlives the artist; courage survives in the work.
- **Tigers symbolise:** freedom, strength, fearlessness, the aunt's suppressed self.
- **Wedding band symbolises:** oppressive marriage, patriarchy, bondage.
- **Contrast:** timid, mastered aunt vs proud, free tigers.
- **Themes:** patriarchy, marriage as constraint, fear, art as escape and permanence.
- **Tone:** sympathetic, admiring, quietly protesting.

## Key formulas

:::formula Aunt vs tigers — value points
Aunt: fluttering fingers, terrified, weighed down by wedding band, mastered by ordeals, will die · Tigers: prance, bright topaz, fearless of men, chivalric certainty, proud, unafraid, go on for ever · tigers = her alter ego.
:::

:::formula The wedding band — value points
Physically light, "massive weight" · burden of marriage and male domination · Uncle's band = his ownership · makes even needlework hard · stays on her hand in death · symbol of patriarchy.
:::

:::formula Art in the poem — value points
Embroidery = the aunt's only free space · tigers carry the courage she cannot live · art outlives the artist · proud and unafraid for ever · both triumph and indictment.
:::

:::formula Devices — value points
Symbolism (tigers, ring, screen, needle) · imagery (topaz, green, heavy ring) · alliteration · metaphor (weight of band) · transferred epithet (terrified hands) · contrast and irony · rhyming couplets.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Aunt Jennifer's tigers are embroidered on a screen.
- The tigers are described as bright topaz.
- The tigers do not fear the men beneath the tree.
- The tigers pace in chivalric certainty.
- Aunt Jennifer finds the ivory needle hard to pull.
- The wedding band is Uncle's and sits heavily on her hand.
- 'Terrified hands' is a transferred epithet.
- The tigers will go on prancing after the aunt's death.
- The poem has three stanzas in rhyming couplets.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip The tigers are not real
They are embroidered; do not describe Aunt Jennifer as owning tigers.
:::

:::tip Weight is symbolic
The ring is light; its massive weight is the burden of marriage.
:::

:::tip Death does not free her
Her hands stay ringed with ordeals; only the tigers are free.
:::

:::tip Name the contrast
Every answer about the tigers should set them against the aunt; that contrast is the poem.
:::
