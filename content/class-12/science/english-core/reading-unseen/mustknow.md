# Unseen passage — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates of how often each item type appears in Q1: ★★★ every year · ★★ most years · ★ sometimes. [marks]

1. ★★★ MCQ on the writer's main idea, purpose or tone. [1]
2. ★★★ Vocabulary: word/phrase from a given paragraph meaning X (or its opposite). [1]
3. ★★★ Inference with justification: pick the correct statement / explain why the writer says Y. [1–2]
4. ★★ True/false or complete-the-sentence item on a detail. [1]
5. ★★ Short answer in a phrase or sentence lifted from the text. [1]
6. ★★ 2-mark short answer in about 40 words (two points or point + reason). [2]
7. ★ "Why does the writer use this example/statistic?" (function of a detail). [1]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Section A:** 22 marks — Q1 unseen passage 12, Q2 case-based/factual passage 10.
- **Q1 passage:** discursive or narrative, about 400–450 words, about ten items.
- **Time for Q1:** 18–20 minutes; do Section A first.
- **Pass 1 (skim):** first paragraph, first sentence of each paragraph, last paragraph; label paragraphs.
- **Pass 2 (scan):** read questions, underline keywords, locate each in the text, read around it.
- **Main-idea MCQ:** choose the option the whole passage supports; reject too broad/too narrow/detail options.
- **Tone words:** critical, nostalgic, humorous, persuasive, cautionary, reflective, matter-of-fact.
- **Inference:** not stated, but the only sensible conclusion; give evidence when asked.
- **Vocabulary rule:** same part of speech; test the option in the sentence.
- **True/false rule:** partly true = false; check exact wording.
- **1-mark short answer:** lift the phrase from the text; no rewriting.
- **2-mark short answer:** about 40 words, two sentences, two points.
- **Negatives to check:** not, except, least, never, only.
- **Function of an example:** to illustrate, contrast, emphasise, make concrete, appeal to experience.

## Key formulas

:::formula Two-pass reading
Skim (2 min): P1 → first lines → last P; label paragraphs → Read questions, underline keywords → Scan to each keyword → read two sentences around it → answer.
:::

:::formula Main idea vs detail
Main idea = supported by every paragraph · Detail = supported by one · Too broad = beyond the passage · Pick the option your paragraph labels all point to.
:::

:::formula Inference answer (2 marks)
Point (what must be true) — 1 · Evidence (phrase or fact from the text that shows it) — 1.
:::

:::formula Vocabulary item
Go to the named paragraph → find a word of the same part of speech → substitute it in the clue → check the meaning holds.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton 1-mark short answer
1. Locate the keyword; lift the phrase; write it as a phrase or one sentence — 1
:::

:::skeleton 2-mark (about 40 words)
1. First point in one sentence (from the text) — 1<br>
2. Second point or the reason/explanation — 1
:::

:::skeleton MCQ on tone or purpose
1. Eliminate options contradicted by any paragraph; choose the one matching the writer's adjectives and conclusion — 1
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Section A carries 22 marks.
- The unseen passage (Q1) carries 12 marks.
- A discursive passage presents an argument or opinion.
- Inference means drawing a conclusion not directly stated.
- Vocabulary answers must match the part of speech of the clue.
- A partly true statement is marked false.
- Negatives such as "not" and "except" reverse what the item asks.
- Section A should take about 30–35 minutes.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Answering from memory
Every answer has a location in the text; find it before writing.
:::

:::tip Missing the negative
"Which is NOT a reason…" — circle the negative before reading the options.
:::

:::tip Over-writing 1-mark items
A phrase is enough; a paragraph wastes time and invites errors.
:::

:::tip Half-true statements
Check each clause of a true/false statement; one wrong clause makes it false.
:::

:::tip Leaving Section A for last
Read the passages first, fresh, with full attention.
:::
