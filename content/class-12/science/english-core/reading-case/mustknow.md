# Case-based passage — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates of how often each item type appears in Q2: ★★★ every year · ★★ most years · ★ sometimes. [marks]

1. ★★★ Data item: highest/lowest category, a value or a difference from the table/chart. [1]
2. ★★★ Fact item: what the study measured, who, when, how many respondents. [1]
3. ★★★ Inference from data: what the figures suggest; which statement the data supports. [1–2]
4. ★★ Trend item: what rose, fell or peaked over the period. [1]
5. ★★ Vocabulary from a named paragraph. [1]
6. ★★ 2-mark: the writer's conclusion with its supporting figure, or two recommendations. [2]
7. ★ "Why might this figure be surprising / what explanation does the writer offer?" [1]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Q2:** case-based or factual passage, about 300–350 words, with a table/chart/graph; about 8–10 items; 10 marks.
- **Time:** 12–15 minutes, straight after Q1.
- **Three layers:** factual introduction, visual, interpretation and conclusion.
- **Visual first:** title, headings, units, largest and smallest, trend, footnotes.
- **Data answer rule:** always quote the figure with its unit.
- **Percentage change:** say "from 48% to 60%" or "by 12 percentage points", never "by 12".
- **Trend words:** rose, fell, remained steady, peaked, doubled, halved, fluctuated.
- **Inference rule:** only what the numbers support; no opinions.
- **Correlation vs cause:** a survey shows a link, not that one thing causes another.
- **Rows vs columns:** check the table's direction before copying a value.
- **Line graph rule:** follow the point down to the x-axis for the year.
- **Vocabulary rule:** same part of speech; substitute to check.
- **2-mark item:** two points, about 40 words.
- **Common conclusion items:** the writer's recommendation; the limitation the writer admits.

## Key formulas

:::formula Reading a table or chart
Title → what is measured → unit → groups/period → largest, smallest → direction of change → footnote. Then answer data items straight from the visual.
:::

:::formula Difference and change
Difference = larger − smaller (same unit) · Change over period = last − first · "Almost doubled" ≈ ×1.8–2 · Percentage figures: use percentage points.
:::

:::formula Inference from data
Numbers show X higher/lower in group A → say "the data suggests A does X more than B" → stop; do not add a cause the passage does not give.
:::

:::formula 2-mark conclusion answer
Writer's conclusion (1) + the figure or reason that supports it (1), in about 40 words.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton Data item (1 mark)
1. Name the category/year + the figure with unit — 1
:::

:::skeleton Inference item (2 marks)
1. The inference the data supports — 1<br>
2. The figures that support it — 1
:::

:::skeleton Vocabulary item (1 mark)
1. Go to the named paragraph; same part of speech; write the single word — 1
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Q2 of Section A carries 10 marks.
- A case-based passage usually includes a table, chart or graph.
- Data answers should quote the figure and its unit.
- A percentage change is expressed in percentage points.
- A trend describes the direction of change over time.
- A survey shows correlation, not proof of cause.
- The visual should be read before the questions.
- Q2 should take about 12–15 minutes.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Row for column
Check which way the table runs before copying a number.
:::

:::tip Wrong unit
Hours, per cent, number of people — match the chart's unit exactly.
:::

:::tip Over-inference
Say what the numbers show; do not add causes or judgements the passage does not give.
:::

:::tip Value vs trend
"Which year was highest" wants a year and figure; "how did it change" wants a direction.
:::

:::tip Mental arithmetic
Do subtractions on paper; a wrong difference loses the mark even with correct reasoning.
:::
