## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** Asokamitran; from *My Years with Boss*; worked at Gemini Studios, Madras.
- **Boss:** S. S. Vasan, head of Gemini Studios.
- **Pancake:** brand name of the make-up material bought in truckloads.
- **Make-up department location:** once Robert Clive's stables.
- **Make-up room look:** salon with mirrors and incandescent lights; unbearable heat.
- **Make-up's job:** turn decent faces into crimson-hued monsters presentable under studio lights.
- **National integration:** Bengali chief, then Maharashtrian; Dharwar Kannadiga, Andhra, Madras Indian Christian, Anglo-Burmese, local Tamils.
- **Hierarchy:** chief → stars; senior assistant → second hero/heroine; junior → comedian; office boy → crowd players.
- **Office boy:** a forty-year-old frustrated poet; painted crowd faces; wanted to be a star, writer, director or lyricist.
- **Office boy's grudge:** blamed Kothamangalam Subbu for his wasted talent.
- **Author's job:** cutting newspaper clippings and filing them; looked idle, so everyone advised him.
- **Subbu's position:** Story Department officially; No. 2 at Gemini in practice.
- **Subbu's gift:** loyalty — identified completely with the Boss.
- **Subbu's versatility:** four ways to shoot a scene, fourteen more if needed; poet, novelist (*Thillana Mohanambal*), actor.
- **Subbu's generosity:** fed and housed dozens of relatives and friends.
- **Subbu's enemies:** envy of his success and closeness to the Boss.
- **The opposite:** the legal adviser (lawyer) in the Story Department; cold logic among dreamers.
- **Lawyer's dress:** pants, tie, sometimes a coat, while others wore khadi.
- **Actress incident:** lawyer recorded her outburst and played it back; she lost her voice and career.
- **Studio politics:** Gandhian khadi-wearers, vaguely but firmly anti-communist.
- **MRA:** Moral Re-Armament Army, 1952, 200 people, 20 nationalities, led by Frank Buchman.
- **MRA plays:** Jotham Valley and The Forgotten Factor.
- **MRA effect:** sunrise and sunset scenes copied in Tamil plays for years.
- **English visitor:** Stephen Spender, poet, editor of The Encounter; accent nobody understood.
- **The God That Failed:** six essays by ex-communists including Spender; explained the visit.

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Pancake was a make-up brand.
- The make-up department was in Robert Clive's stables.
- The office boy was about forty years old.
- The author's job was cutting newspaper clippings.
- Subbu wrote the novel Thillana Mohanambal.
- The legal adviser was called the opposite.

- The God That Failed has six essays by former communists.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Office boy's age
He was not a boy; a man of forty with a poet's frustration.
:::

:::tip Whom the office boy blamed
Subbu, not the Boss or the author; the author merely had to listen.
:::

:::tip Lawyer's intent
The author suggests no malice — a lawyer's instinct for evidence — yet it ended a career. Keep the irony.
:::

:::tip Spender's identity
Three facts: poet who visited, editor of The Encounter, ex-communist in The God That Failed.
:::
