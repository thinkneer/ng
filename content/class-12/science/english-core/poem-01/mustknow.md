## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Poet's name:** Kamala Das (also wrote as Madhavikutty).
- **Poem's situation:** driving from her parent's home to Cochin airport on a Friday morning.
- **Mother in the car:** dozing, mouth open, face ashen like a corpse.
- **Ashen face:** grey, colourless like ash — a sign of age and lifelessness.
- **Poet's response:** pain; looks out of the window to escape the thought.
- **Outside the car:** young trees sprinting; merry children spilling out of their homes.
- **Meaning of trees and children:** youth, energy, life — contrast to the mother's age.
- **Why the trees sprint:** the moving car makes them seem to race; personification of youthful energy.
- **At the airport:** after the security check, mother stands a few yards away, wan and pale.
- **Second comparison:** pale as a late winter's moon — dim, fading, the year's end.
- **The ache:** the old familiar childhood fear of losing her mother.
- **The ending:** poet smiles again and again, promising to see her soon.
- **Why the repeated smile:** to hide fear, to comfort her mother, to hold on to hope.
- **Poetic form:** one long sentence, free verse, no stanza break, enjambment.
- **Poem's tone:** quiet, reflective, tender, anxious.
- **Literary devices:** simile, imagery, contrast, personification, repetition, symbolism.
- **Main themes:** ageing, mortality, fear of separation, love and helplessness, brave face.

## Key formulas

:::formula Two images of the mother — value points
Car: dozing, open mouth, ashen, like a corpse — shock of recognising death · Airport: wan, pale, like the late winter's moon — dim, fading, end of the year · both = age and approaching death.
:::

:::formula Contrast of inside and outside — value points
Inside: stillness, age, death-like face · Outside: sprinting trees, merry children, movement, youth, life · purpose: escape pain, sharpen the sense of the mother's age, cycle of life.
:::

:::formula The repeated smile — value points
Hides her pain and fear · brave face to comfort the mother · promise of meeting again = hope · repetition shows effort, not spontaneity · love that spares the beloved.
:::

:::formula Poetic devices — value points
Simile (corpse, winter's moon) · personification (trees sprint) · contrast (age vs youth) · repetition (smile) · imagery (visual) · symbolism (moon, children, airport) · free verse, single sentence.
:::

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Not illness
The mother is old, not sick; use ashen, corpse-like, wan, pale.
:::

:::tip Trees and children are contrast
They stand for youth and life against the mother's age; do not describe them as mere scenery.
:::

:::tip The smile is forced
The repetition shows effort to hide fear; a spontaneous happy smile is the wrong reading.
:::

:::tip Fear, not grief
The mother is alive; the poet feels fear of future loss, not mourning.
:::
