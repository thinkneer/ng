# Formal letters — editor, complaint, job application

The 5-mark letter is the one Section B question where *what you say* matters as much as *how it looks*. The shell is fixed and worth 1 mark; the two body marks come from covering the points the question asks for; the last two come from clear, formal English. The 2026–27 paper asks for one formal letter of **120–150 words** with an internal choice, usually between a **letter to the editor**, a **letter of complaint / enquiry / placing an order**, and a **job application with bio-data**. Learn one shell, three bodies.

## The shell every formal letter shares

Write these in order, each on its own line or block, all aligned to the left:

1. **Sender's address** (two or three lines; no name on top).
2. **Date** in full (14 November 2026).
3. **Receiver's designation and address** (The Editor, *The Times of India*, New Delhi / The Manager, Customer Care, …).
4. **Subject** — one line, specific, in sentence case or bold (*Subject: Poor condition of roads in Sector 12*).
5. **Salutation** — *Sir / Madam,* or *Dear Sir / Madam,* (for the editor simply *Sir,*).
6. **Body** — three paragraphs (see below).
7. **Complimentary close** — *Yours sincerely,* (when you have addressed by designation, *Yours faithfully,* is also accepted; be consistent).
8. **Name**, and below it anything the question gives you (designation, class, address line if a "concerned citizen").

## The three-paragraph body

Whatever the type, the body follows one shape, and the examiner rewards it:

- **Paragraph 1 — purpose.** Say in one or two sentences why you are writing. *Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper I wish to draw attention to…* / *I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with…* / *With reference to your advertisement in … dated …, I wish to apply for the post of…*
- **Paragraph 2 — details.** The heart of the letter. For an editor: the problem, its causes and effects (two or three concrete points). For a complaint: what you bought/booked, when, what went wrong, the inconvenience. For a job application: your qualification, experience and the qualities that fit the post.
- **Paragraph 3 — action and close.** What you want: the authorities to act, a replacement/refund within a period, an interview. End politely: *I hope the concerned authorities will take prompt action.* / *I look forward to a positive response.*

Count the words of the body only: 120–150. Three paragraphs of about 40–50 words each lands you there naturally.

:::tip Specific beats general
"Many problems are faced by people" earns nothing. "Street lights on the main road have not worked for three weeks, making the walk from the bus stop unsafe after 7 p.m." earns the content mark. Give two or three concrete points with places, times and effects.
:::

## Letter to the editor

Written by a citizen to a newspaper to raise a public issue (civic problems, pollution, safety, exam stress, screen time, road accidents). You write *to the editor* but you *address the authorities and the public* through the paper. The tone is concerned and constructive, never abusive.

Standard moves: open with the "columns of your esteemed newspaper" line; describe the problem with specifics; mention who suffers and why it matters; suggest solutions and name who should act (municipal corporation, traffic police, school authorities); close by hoping for prompt action. Sign as a name with "a concerned citizen / student" if the question says so.

:::tip "Dear Editor" is not the salutation
The receiver is *The Editor, [newspaper], [city]*; the salutation is simply *Sir,* or *Sir/Madam,*. "Dear Editor" or "Respected Sir" both look wrong to the examiner.
:::

**In one breath:** editor letter = public problem + specifics + who should act + hope for action; salutation *Sir,*.

## Letter of complaint

Written to a shop, company, service provider or office about a defective product, poor service, a billing error or a delayed delivery. Keep it **firm and polite**: facts, not anger.

Paragraph 2 must have the **transaction details**: what, when (date), where, order/bill number, price if given. Then what exactly is wrong (the product stopped working, the wrong model was delivered, the service was not provided) and the inconvenience caused. Paragraph 3 asks for a specific remedy — replacement, repair, refund — **within a time frame** (within a week / at the earliest), and may mention that you are enclosing a copy of the bill.

:::tip Ask for one remedy, politely
"I demand you return my money immediately or face legal action" loses the expression mark for tone. "I request you to replace the product or refund the amount within ten days" gets it.
:::

**In one breath:** complaint = transaction details + exactly what went wrong + inconvenience + one specific remedy within a time; firm, polite.

## Job application with bio-data

Two parts: a **covering letter** (short) and a **bio-data / résumé** (tabular, below the letter or as an enclosure). The question gives the advertisement details and the post.

The covering letter: Paragraph 1 — refer to the advertisement (newspaper, date) and name the post. Paragraph 2 — your suitability in two or three sentences (qualification, experience, key strengths that match the job). Paragraph 3 — mention the enclosed bio-data, express willingness for an interview, close politely. Keep the letter body around **80–100 words** so the whole answer stays near the limit.

The bio-data: a neat block under a heading **BIO-DATA**, with these rows in order — **Name · Father's/Mother's name (optional) · Date of birth · Address · Contact (phone/email) · Educational qualifications (table: exam, board/university, year, marks) · Experience · Skills/strengths · Languages known · References (optional)**. Use invented but plausible details; never real personal data.

:::tip Do not put the bio-data inside the letter
Write the letter fully, close it, then write "Encl.: Bio-data" and give the bio-data block below. Mixing qualifications into every paragraph of the letter makes both parts weak.
:::

## How the 5 marks are given

- **Format — 1 mark:** address, date, receiver, subject, salutation, close, name — in order, left-aligned.
- **Content — 2 marks:** the specific points the question asks for are covered, in the right paragraph, with details.
- **Expression — 2 marks:** grammar, spelling, sentence variety, formal register, coherence, within 120–150 words.

Spend about 10 minutes. Underline the points in the question, write them as a margin list, then write the three paragraphs around them. Read once for tone (no slang, no anger, no "I want") and for the word count.

For the official pattern and a model question, see the CBSE 2026–27 sample paper on [cbseacademic.nic.in](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/).
