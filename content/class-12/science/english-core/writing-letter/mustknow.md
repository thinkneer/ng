# Formal letters — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates: ★★★ most years · ★★ often · ★ sometimes. Marks in square brackets; 120–150 words.

1. ★★★ Letter to the editor on a civic or social issue: pollution, traffic, road safety, garbage, stray animals, exam stress, screen addiction. [5]
2. ★★★ Letter of complaint about a defective product or poor service, asking for replacement or refund. [5]
3. ★★ Job application with bio-data in response to an advertisement (teacher, content writer, receptionist, trainee). [5]
4. ★★ Letter to the Principal / Municipal Commissioner / Police Commissioner requesting action or permission. [5]
5. ★ Letter of enquiry or placing an order (coaching course, books, sports equipment). [5]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Formal letter order:** sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body, close, name.
- **Subject line:** one specific line stating the purpose; placed before the salutation.
- **Salutation to editor:** "Sir," or "Sir/Madam," — never "Dear Editor".
- **Receiver for editor letter:** The Editor, newspaper name, city.
- **Complimentary close:** "Yours sincerely," (or "Yours faithfully,") followed by name.
- **Body shape:** paragraph 1 purpose, paragraph 2 details, paragraph 3 action and close.
- **Word limit:** 120–150 words for the body.
- **Editor-letter opening:** "Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper I wish to draw attention to…"
- **Complaint essentials:** item, date of purchase, bill/order number, defect, inconvenience, remedy with time frame.
- **Job application parts:** short covering letter plus a separate bio-data block.
- **Bio-data rows:** name, date of birth, address, contact, qualifications table, experience, skills, languages.
- **Marks split:** format 1, content 2, expression 2.

## Key formulas

:::formula Formal letter shell
Sender's address → Date → Receiver's designation and address → Subject → Salutation → Body (3 paragraphs) → Yours sincerely, → Name (+ role). Left-aligned.
:::

:::formula Three-paragraph body
P1 purpose (why writing, reference) → P2 details (two or three concrete points) → P3 action requested + polite close. 120–150 words.
:::

:::formula Editor letter — standard lines
Open: *Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to…*<br>
Close: *I hope the concerned authorities will take prompt action.*
:::

:::formula Complaint letter — standard lines
Open: *I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with…* / *This is to bring to your notice that…*<br>
Close: *I request you to replace the product / refund the amount within … days. A copy of the bill is enclosed.*
:::

:::formula Job application — standard lines
Open: *With reference to your advertisement in … dated …, I wish to apply for the post of…*<br>
Close: *My bio-data is enclosed. I shall be grateful for an opportunity to appear for an interview.*
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton 5-mark editor letter
1. Shell: address, date, The Editor + paper + city, subject, Sir, — format 1<br>
2. P1: esteemed-newspaper line + the issue in one sentence — content ½<br>
3. P2: two or three specific points (where, how often, who suffers, causes) — content 1<br>
4. P3: suggestions + who should act + hope for prompt action; close and name — content ½<br>
5. Formal register, varied sentences, grammar, 120–150 words — expression 2
:::

:::skeleton 5-mark complaint letter
1. Shell with The Manager / Customer Care, subject, Sir/Madam, — format 1<br>
2. P1: purpose; P2: item, date, bill number, defect, inconvenience — content 1½<br>
3. P3: one remedy within a time frame; bill enclosed; polite close — content ½<br>
4. Firm, polite tone; correct grammar; 120–150 words — expression 2
:::

:::skeleton 5-mark job application
1. Shell with The Manager/Principal, subject naming the post, Sir/Madam, — format ½<br>
2. P1: advert reference + post; P2: suitability; P3: enclosure + interview request — content 1½<br>
3. Bio-data block: name, DOB, address, contact, qualifications table, experience, skills — format ½ + content ½<br>
4. Formal register, grammar, overall length in range — expression 2
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- The subject line comes before the salutation in a formal letter.
- The sender's address is written without the sender's name.
- A letter to the editor is addressed to The Editor of a named newspaper.
- A complaint letter states the date of purchase and bill number.
- "Yours sincerely," is followed by a comma, then the name on the next line.
- A job application encloses a bio-data; the letter mentions "Encl.".

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip "Dear Editor" / "Respected Sir"
Use "Sir," for the editor and "Sir/Madam," for offices. Over-formal or chatty salutations lose the format mark.
:::

:::tip General complaints
"There are many problems" earns nothing; name the place, the date, the frequency and who suffers.
:::

:::tip Angry tone
Threats and demands cost the expression marks; request a specific remedy within a time frame politely.
:::

:::tip Bio-data inside the letter
Keep the covering letter short and put the bio-data as a separate block after "Encl.".
:::

:::tip Word count
Under 120 words looks thin; over 170 wastes time and risks errors. Three paragraphs of 40–50 words each.
:::
