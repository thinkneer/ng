## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Poet:** Robert Frost, American poet of rural New England.
- **The stand:** a little new shed built by farmers at the edge of the highway.
- **What they sell:** wild berries in wooden quarts; crook-necked golden squash with silver warts.
- **What they want:** not bread (charity) but a little city money — a share of the flower of cities.
- **Flower of cities:** urban prosperity and the good life promised by films and papers.
- **Polished traffic:** shiny cars of the rich, passing with a mind ahead.
- **Why cars stop:** to complain about signs (N and S turned wrong) and the shed spoiling the view; to ask the way; to turn the car round; to ask for a gallon of gas.
- **Childish longing:** the farmers' innocent, unrealistic hope that a car will stop and pay.
- **Pitiful kin:** Frost's name for the poor rural folk.
- **Greedy good-doers / beneficent beasts of prey:** oxymorons for false philanthropists who exploit while pretending to help.
- **Their plan:** buy out the farmers, relocate them next to the theatre and store, make them dependent.
- **Enforcing benefits:** calculated to soothe the poor out of their wits; teach them to sleep all day.
- **Poet's pain:** cannot bear the open window waiting all day, the sadness that lurks.
- **The ending:** poet imagines ending their pain at one stroke, then asks whether he would accept such 'mercy' for his own pain.
- **Devices:** imagery, alliteration, personification (traffic with a mind ahead), transferred epithet (selfish cars), oxymoron, metaphor, irony.
- **Themes:** rural–urban divide, inequality, false philanthropy, indifference, limits of pity.

## Key formulas

:::formula Why the cars stop — value points
Complain about crude signs (N, S wrong) and the shed spoiling the view · ask the way · use the yard to turn round · ask for a gallon of gas · never to buy.
:::

:::formula Greedy good-doers — value points
Oxymoron: kind-sounding words + predatory words · pretend charity, real exploitation · buy out and relocate farmers near theatre and store · destroy independence · soothe them out of their wits · sleep all day.
:::

:::formula Farmers' longing — value points
Not begging bread · want city money, flower of cities · berries and squash for sale · wait for the squeal of brakes · childish, innocent, in vain · sadness lurks near the open window.
:::

:::formula The ending — value points
Poet cannot bear their vain hope · thought of ending their pain at one stroke · turns on himself · would he accept such mercy? · irony and self-reflection · pity is easy, change is hard.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- The roadside stand is a little new shed by the highway.
- The farmers sell wild berries and golden squash.
- The farmers want city money, not bread.
- The polished traffic passes with a mind ahead.
- The signs at the stand have N and S turned wrong.
- 'Greedy good-doers' is an oxymoron.
- The do-gooders plan to relocate the farmers near the theatre and store.
- 'Selfish cars' is a transferred epithet.
- The poet ends by questioning his own thought of mercy.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Not charity
The farmers do not beg; they sell and want fair trade with city money.
:::

:::tip The cars never buy
List the four reasons they stop; buying is not one of them.
:::

:::tip Good-doers are the villains
The oxymoron exposes false help; do not describe them as genuine benefactors.
:::

:::tip The ending is ironic
Frost checks his own cruel 'mercy'; he does not wish the farmers dead.
:::
