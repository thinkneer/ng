## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Author:** William O. Douglas, American judge; from *Of Men and Mountains*.
- **First incident:** age three or four; California beach; waves knocked him down; father laughed.
- **Second incident:** age ten or eleven; YMCA pool, Yakima; thrown in by a bully.
- **Why YMCA pool:** safe (2–3 ft shallow, 9 ft deep); Yakima River treacherous, mother warned of drownings.
- **The bully:** about eighteen, big and muscular; called him Skinny; tossed him into the deep end.
- **Depth:** nine feet, which felt like ninety.
- **First attempt:** planned a big jump off the bottom; came up slowly; saw yellowish water; grabbed at nothing.
- **Second attempt:** jumped again; legs paralysed, lungs aching, could not scream.
- **Third sinking:** effort ceased; felt peaceful, drowsy; lost consciousness.
- **After rescue:** vomiting by the pool; shaking; cried; could not eat for days.
- **Long-term effect:** haunting fear near water; ruined fishing, canoeing, boating, swimming.
- **Places he loved but avoided:** Cascades, Tieton, Bumping River, Warm Lake, Columbia.
- **Instructor's schedule:** five days a week, one hour a day; October to April.
- **Step 1:** belt, rope through a pulley on an overhead cable; back and forth across the pool; three months.
- **Step 2:** face in water, exhale; nose up, inhale — hundreds of times.
- **Step 3:** kicking with legs at the pool side, week after week.
- **Step 4:** all pieces combined; could swim the pool in April.
- **Self-test 1:** Lake Wentworth, New Hampshire — dived at Triggs Island, swam two miles to Stamp Act Island.
- **Self-test 2:** Warm Lake — camped at Conrad Meadows, swam across and back; shouted with joy.
- **Lesson:** death itself has peace; only the fear of death is terrible; conquering fear sets one free.
- **Quotation idea:** Roosevelt — the only thing we have to fear is fear itself.

## Key formulas

:::formula Douglas's near-drowning — value points
Tossed into 9 ft · swallowed water, sank · plan: jump off bottom, lie flat, paddle · 1st rise: yellowish water, nothing to grab · 2nd: paralysed legs, no scream · 3rd: effort stops, peace, blackness · revived vomiting by the pool.
:::

:::formula Instructor's method — value points
Belt + rope + pulley across the pool (3 months) · face under water, exhale/inhale practice · leg kicks at the side · integrate strokes · Oct→April · then self-tests at Lake Wentworth and Warm Lake.
:::

:::formula Lesson of Deep Water — value points
Faced death twice (terror then peace) · realised it is fear of death, not death, that terrifies · fear can be conquered by method and will · freedom to live intensely · Roosevelt's idea on fear.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Douglas was about three or four at the California beach.
- The YMCA pool was in Yakima.
- The bully was about eighteen years old.
- The deep end of the pool was nine feet.
- Douglas made three attempts to come up.
- He trained with the instructor from October to April.
- The rope passed through a pulley on an overhead cable.
- He swam two miles across Lake Wentworth.
- Warm Lake was his final test.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Two incidents, not one
Fear began at the beach (age 3–4) and became terror at the pool (age 10–11); give both when asked how the fear developed.
:::

:::tip Three attempts
Do not merge the three attempts; each has its own detail.
:::

:::tip Why not satisfied
After the instructor he still had to test himself alone — Lake Wentworth and Warm Lake — to be sure the terror had gone.
:::

:::tip Lesson wording
It is the fear of death, not death, that he conquered; answers that say "he learnt to swim" miss the message.
:::
