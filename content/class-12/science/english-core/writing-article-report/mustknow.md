# Article and report — must know

## Most-repeated board questions

Stars are estimates: ★★★ most years · ★★ often · ★ sometimes. Marks in square brackets; 120–150 words.

1. ★★★ Article on a social issue: pollution, mobile addiction, women's safety, stress of exams, junk food, importance of sports / reading / discipline. [5]
2. ★★★ Report on a school event: annual day, science exhibition, sports day, cleanliness drive, tree plantation, blood donation camp. [5]
3. ★★ Article on a topical theme: digital India, online learning, climate change, road safety, value of yoga. [5]
4. ★★ Newspaper report by a staff reporter: accident, fire, flood relief, festival celebration, inauguration. [5]
5. ★ Report of a visit / excursion / workshop for the school magazine. [5]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Article:** a composition that discusses an issue, gives a view and suggests solutions.
- **Article format:** title and by-line, then introduction, body, conclusion.
- **Article introduction:** a hook (fact, question, scene) plus the issue in one sentence.
- **Article conclusion:** your view, two or three suggestions, a memorable closing line.
- **Report:** a factual account of an event that has already happened.
- **Report format:** headline, by-line, place and date line (newspaper), body in inverted pyramid.
- **Inverted pyramid:** most important facts first (5 Ws), details later, outcome last.
- **Report voice:** past tense, third person, passive where natural, no personal opinion.
- **By-line:** the writer's name (article) or "By a Staff Reporter" / name and designation (report).
- **5 Ws of a report:** what, who, when, where, why.
- **Word limit:** 120–150 words for both.
- **Marks split:** format 1, content 2, expression 2.

## Key formulas

:::formula Article structure
**Title** → **By-line** → P1 hook + issue → P2 causes and effects with examples → P3 view + suggestions → closing line. 120–150 words.
:::

:::formula Report structure
**HEADLINE** → By-line → *Place, date:* → P1 the 5 Ws → P2 sequence, highlights, numbers → P3 outcome, winners, response, vote of thanks. Past tense, passive.
:::

:::formula Linking words for expression marks
Adding: moreover, in addition, besides · Contrast: however, on the other hand · Result: as a result, consequently, therefore · Sequence: first, later, finally
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton 5-mark article
1. Title + by-line — format 1<br>
2. P1 hook and the issue stated — content ½<br>
3. P2 two or three causes/effects with concrete examples — content 1<br>
4. P3 your view, suggestions, strong closing line — content ½<br>
5. Cohesion, vocabulary, varied sentences; grammar and spelling; 120–150 words — expression 2
:::

:::skeleton 5-mark report
1. Headline + by-line (+ place and date for a newspaper) — format 1<br>
2. P1 what, who, when, where, why — content 1<br>
3. P2 sequence and highlights with numbers; P3 outcome and closing — content 1<br>
4. Past tense, passive, factual, third person; grammar; 120–150 words — expression 2
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- An article carries a title and a by-line.
- A report carries a headline and a by-line.
- A newspaper report begins with a place and date line.
- A report is written in the past tense.
- A report does not include the writer's personal opinion.
- The inverted pyramid puts the most important facts first.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip No title or by-line
The format mark depends on them; write both before the first paragraph.
:::

:::tip Opinion in a report
A report states facts; save "I feel" and suggestions for the article.
:::

:::tip Present tense in a report
The event is over: "was held", "were displayed", "was given away".
:::

:::tip Article without suggestions
Describing a problem without a way forward loses content; always end with two or three measures.
:::

:::tip Missing numbers in a report
Participants, schools, visitors, money raised — one or two figures make the report credible.
:::
