## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Part I author:** Zitkala-Sa (Gertrude Simmons Bonnin), Native American of the Dakota people.
- **Setting I:** Carlisle Indian School; her first day; bitter cold, snow.
- **Discipline by bells:** one to pull chairs, one to sit, one to eat.
- **Eating by formula:** a meal drilled by bells and rules — mechanical regimentation.
- **Alien dress:** stiff shoes, tight clinging dresses; shame and discomfort.
- **Judewin:** friend who knew some English; warned that their hair would be cut; advised submission.
- **Hair in her culture:** shingled hair = cowards; hair cut short = mourners; long hair = dignity.
- **Resistance:** hid under a bed in a large room upstairs.
- **Capture:** dragged out kicking and scratching; carried downstairs; tied to a chair.
- **The shears:** heard them gnaw off a thick braid; lost her spirit.
- **Image:** felt like one of many little animals driven by a herder.
- **Part II author:** Bama, Tamil Dalit writer; from her autobiography Karukku.
- **Bama's age:** third standard.
- **Walk home:** ten minutes stretched to an hour — monkey, snake charmer, cyclist, temple, Pongal, stalls, speeches, puppet show, stunts.
- **The incident:** an elder from her street carried a packet of vadais by its string, bowed low, gave it to the landlord.
- **Bama's first reaction:** laughed at the funny way of carrying it.
- **Annan's explanation:** different castes; upper caste would not eat food touched by a low caste — untouchability.
- **Bama's second reaction:** anger, sadness, provocation; wanted to touch the packet herself.
- **Annan's experience:** asked which street he lived on — a way of finding his caste.
- **Annan's advice:** study hard, make progress; people will come to you of their own accord.
- **Bama's response:** studied hard, stood first in class; many became her friends.
- **Shared theme:** discrimination (race, caste) seen through a child's eyes; dignity; resistance through learning.

## Key formulas

:::formula Cutting of the hair — value points

:::

Dawdling walk home · elder carries vadai packet by its string · bows to landlord · Bama laughs · Annan: untouchability, caste · laughter turns to anger · Annan: study, progress, people will come · tops class, gains friends.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Zitkala-Sa was a Native American writer.
- Her first day at the school was bitterly cold.
- Judewin warned her that their hair would be cut.
- In her culture shingled hair was worn by cowards.
- She hid under a bed before being captured.
- Bama is a Tamil Dalit writer; the extract is from Karukku.
- Bama was in the third standard at the time.
- The elder carried a packet of vadais by its string.
- Annan was Bama's elder brother.
- Bama stood first in her class after Annan's advice.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Two authors
Zitkala-Sa wrote Part I; Bama wrote Part II. Do not merge them into one narrator.
:::

:::tip Why hair
Cultural meaning (cowards, mourners), not vanity.
:::

:::tip Bama laughed first
Her first reaction was laughter at the odd way of carrying the packet; understanding and anger came after Annan explained.
:::

:::tip Annan's advice
Study and progress, not confrontation; Bama's success is the proof.
:::
