## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Electric charge:** scalar; additive, conserved, quantised as $q=ne$.
- **Charging by induction:** charging without contact by rearranging free electrons with a nearby charge.
- **Electrical conductor:** has free electrons; charge given to it spreads over the surface.
- **Coulomb's law:** force between point charges ∝ $q_1q_2$, ∝ $1/r^2$, along the joining line.
- **Dielectric constant K:** ratio of force in vacuum to force in the medium.
- **Electric field:** force per unit positive test charge at a point.
- **Electric dipole:** two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
- **Dipole moment:** $p=q\times2a$, directed from −q to +q; unit C m.
- **Electric flux:** $\vec E\cdot\vec S$, the number of field lines through a surface; scalar.
- **Gauss's law:** total flux through a closed surface equals $q_\text{enclosed}/\varepsilon_0$.
- **Field lines:** start on +, end on −, never cross, never form closed loops.
- **Field lines never cross:** the field cannot have two directions at one point.
- **Uniform field:** equally spaced parallel field lines.

## Key formulas

:::formula Coulomb's law
$F=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r^2}$, $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}=9\times10^9$ N m² C⁻²; in a medium divide by $K$.
:::

:::formula Field of a point charge
$E=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r^2}$, outward for $+q$; unit N C⁻¹ = V m⁻¹.
:::

:::formula Dipole field on the axis
$E=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{2pr}{(r^2-a^2)^2}\approx\dfrac{2kp}{r^3}$, along $\vec p$; $r$ from the centre.
:::

:::formula Dipole field on the equatorial line
$E=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{p}{(r^2+a^2)^{3/2}}\approx\dfrac{kp}{r^3}$, opposite to $\vec p$.
:::

:::formula Torque on a dipole
$\vec\tau=\vec p\times\vec E$, $\tau=pE\sin\theta$; net force zero in a uniform field.
:::

:::formula Gauss's law
$\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec S=\dfrac{q_\text{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$; flux unit N m² C⁻¹.
:::

:::formula Field of a long straight wire
$E=\dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}$, radial; $E\propto1/r$.
:::

:::formula Field of an infinite plane sheet
$E=\dfrac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}$, independent of distance; two opposite sheets: $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$ between.
:::

:::formula Field of a thin spherical shell
$E=\dfrac{kq}{r^2}$ outside ($r\ge R$); $E=0$ inside.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton Dv · Dipole, axial point (3 marks)
Diagram: −q at A, +q at B, $AB=2a$, P at distance $r$ from centre [½] → $E_{+q}=\dfrac{kq}{(r-a)^2}$ along $\vec p$, $E_{-q}=\dfrac{kq}{(r+a)^2}$ opposite [1] → subtract: $\dfrac{4kqar}{(r^2-a^2)^2}=\dfrac{2kpr}{(r^2-a^2)^2}$ [1] → $r\gg a$: $\dfrac{2kp}{r^3}$, along $\vec p$ [½]
:::

:::skeleton Dv · Dipole, equatorial point (3 marks)
Diagram with both fields at Q [½] → each $=\dfrac{kq}{r^2+a^2}$ [½] → perpendicular components cancel, axial ones add: $2E\cos\theta$, $\cos\theta=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{r^2+a^2}}$ [1] → $\dfrac{kp}{(r^2+a^2)^{3/2}}$, opposite $\vec p$ [½] → $r\gg a$: $kp/r^3$ [½]
:::

:::skeleton Dv · Gauss: infinite sheet (3 marks)
Pillbox of end area $A$ through the sheet (diagram) [½] → curved surface: $\vec E\perp$ normal, flux 0 [½] → two ends: $2EA$ [½] → $q_\text{enc}=\sigma A$ [½] → $2EA=\sigma A/\varepsilon_0$ [½] → $E=\sigma/2\varepsilon_0$, independent of distance [½]
:::

:::skeleton Dv · Torque on a dipole (2 marks)
Forces $+q\vec E$ and $-q\vec E$: net force zero [½] → couple arm $2a\sin\theta$ [½] → $\tau=qE\cdot2a\sin\theta=pE\sin\theta$, $\vec\tau=\vec p\times\vec E$ [1]
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- **Rubbing glass with silk:** electrons move from glass to silk; glass becomes positive.
- **Charge outside a closed surface:** adds zero to the total flux.
- **Closed surface around a dipole:** total flux zero.
- **Doubling the radius of a Gaussian sphere:** flux unchanged.
- **Dipole field at large r:** falls as $1/r^3$; point charge as $1/r^2$.
- **Same distance, short dipole:** $E_\text{axial}=2E_\text{equatorial}$.
- **Dipole in a non-uniform field:** feels both a net force and a torque.
- **Stable equilibrium of a dipole:** $\vec p$ parallel to $\vec E$ ($\theta=0^\circ$).
- **Field inside a charged thin shell:** zero.
- **SI unit of electric field:** N C⁻¹ or V m⁻¹.
- **Water's dielectric constant:** about 80, so Coulomb forces in water are about 80 times weaker.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Charge at a cube corner
Flux through the whole cube is $q/8\varepsilon_0$ (8 cubes share the corner); $q/24\varepsilon_0$ through each of the 3 far faces, zero through the 3 touching faces.
:::

:::tip r in dipole formulas
$r$ is measured from the centre of the dipole, and $2a$ is the full separation.
:::

:::tip Direction of the dipole field
Axial: along $\vec p$. Equatorial: opposite to $\vec p$. A missing direction costs ½ mark.
:::

:::tip Flux versus field
Flux depends only on charge inside; the field on the surface depends on all charges.
:::

:::tip No Gaussian surface drawn
Draw and label the Gaussian surface, and justify the flux through each part, or lose ½ mark.
:::
