1. ★★★ Define Social Science; how is it different from the natural sciences? [1–2]

3. ★★★ Kinds of historical sources with one example each. [3]

## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Social Science, defined:** the systematic study of human society.
- **Questions Social Science asks:** why and how, not only what and where.
- **Natural sciences study:** the natural world (Physics, Chemistry, Biology).
- **Social Science studies:** society, institutions, cultures and human interaction.
- **Four core disciplines:** Geography, History, Political Science, Economics.
- **Wider social science family:** Sociology, Anthropology, Psychology, Philosophy.
- **Geography studies:** places, distributions and human–environment interaction.
- **History studies:** the human past and change over time, from sources.
- **Political Science studies:** power, decision-making, constitutions, laws and policies.
- **Economics studies:** how limited resources meet needs; production, exchange, distribution.
- **Why a group of disciplines:** society is complex; together they give a holistic view.
- **Geographer's tools:** maps, globes, atlases, GIS, infographics, fieldwork.
- **GIS, in full:** Geographic Information Systems; computer maps built in layers.
- **Literary sources:** texts, travelogues, memoirs, folklore, revenue and family records.
- **Archaeological sources:** monuments, buildings, tools, pottery, sculptures, paintings.
- **Epigraphic sources:** inscriptions on rock, pillars, walls or copper plates.
- **Numismatic sources:** coins.
- **Genealogical records:** family trees and life-event records; a literary source.
- **Carbon-14 dating:** scientific method that finds the age of old remains.
- **Empirical evidence:** information gathered by observation or experiment.
- **Panchamahābhūtas idea:** five elements (earth, water, fire, air, space); early systems view.
- **Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam:** "the world is one family"; societies are interconnected.
- **Arthaśāstra author and age:** Kauṭilya; about 2,300 years ago.
- **Arthaśāstra deals with:** administration, taxation, economy and duties of rulers.
- **Itihāsa–Purāṇa tradition:** stories preserving cultural memory and historical information.
- **Rājadharma, meaning:** the ruler's duty towards the people.
- **Panchayati Raj:** local self-government continuing grassroots decision-making.

## Key formulas

:::formula Social Science in one line
**Systematic study of human society**: what · where · **why** · **how**.
When to use: any "define" or "what is" question. Slip: calling it "the study of the past".
:::

:::formula Four lenses on one event
Geography (where, environment) · History (when, change) · Political Science (who decides) · Economics (limited resources).
When to use: "why many disciplines?" answers; add the drought example.
:::

:::formula Sources of history
Literary · Archaeological · Epigraphic (inscriptions) · Numismatic (coins) · Scientific (carbon-14, genetics).
Slip: epigraphy and numismatics swapped.
:::

## Answer skeletons

:::skeleton Why is Social Science a group of disciplines? (3 marks)
1. Society is complex; no single subject explains it. (1)
2. Each discipline gives one angle: Geography, History, Political Science, Economics. (1)
3. Example: a drought (rain, past droughts, relief policy, crop prices); together a holistic picture. (1)
:::

:::skeleton Why study Social Science? (5 marks)
Intro line (definition) → understand everyday systems → empathy for diversity → informed democratic citizen → link past, present, future → today's issues (climate change, migration) → conclusion. One mark per explained point.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Study of coins: numismatics.
- Study of inscriptions: epigraphy.
- Author of the Arthaśāstra: Kauṭilya.
- "The world is one family": Vasudhaiva Kuṭumbakam.
- Five great elements: Panchamahābhūtas.
- Layered computer maps: GIS.
- Age of ancient bones: carbon-14 dating.
- Physics, Chemistry, Biology: natural sciences.
- Information from observation or experiment: empirical evidence.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Leaving out "systematic"
"Study of society" alone is incomplete. Write "**systematic** study of human society".
:::

:::tip Coins vs inscriptions
Coins = **numismatic**; inscriptions = **epigraphic**, even when a coin has writing on it.
:::

:::tip Listing without explaining
"Why a group of disciplines" needs a reason (society is complex) and an example, not just four names.
:::
