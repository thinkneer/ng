## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Landform, defined:** natural surface feature made by crustal movement, weathering, erosion, deposition.
- **Internal (endogenic) forces:** heat-driven forces that build up the surface.
- **External (exogenic) forces:** weathering, erosion, deposition that wear the surface down.
- **Continental crust thickness:** about 30–40 km.
- **Oceanic crust thickness:** about 5–7 km.
- **Outer core state:** liquid, mainly iron and nickel.
- **Inner core state:** solid, hottest and densest part.
- **Lithosphere, defined:** rigid crust plus upper mantle, about 100 km thick.
- **Asthenosphere, defined:** hot, partly molten mantle layer on which plates move.
- **Tectonic plates:** pieces of the lithosphere moving a few centimetres a year.
- **Plate types:** continental, oceanic, mixed.
- **Seven major plates:** Pacific, Eurasian, African, N American, S American, Indo-Australian, Antarctic.
- **Cause of plate movement:** convection currents in the mantle driven by core heat.
- **Convergent boundary:** plates collide; fold mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes.
- **Himalaya formed by:** Indian plate colliding with the Eurasian plate (convergent).
- **Divergent boundary:** plates move apart; magma forms new crust, mid-ocean ridges.
- **Divergent example:** Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
- **Transform boundary:** plates slide past; no crust made or lost; earthquakes.
- **Transform example:** San Andreas Fault, USA.
- **Ring of Fire:** earthquake and volcano belt around the Pacific Ocean.
- **Gujarat earthquake:** 26 January 2001, Kutch (Bhuj).
- **Baratang Island feature:** rare mud volcano, Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
- **Weathering, defined:** breaking down of rock in place, without transport.
- **Erosion, defined:** wearing away and carrying away of rock and soil.
- **Biological weathering example:** roots growing in rock cracks.
- **Agents of gradation:** running water, waves, glaciers, wind, groundwater.
- **Upper-course landforms:** V-shaped valleys, waterfalls, rapids.
- **Middle-course landforms:** meanders, oxbow lakes, floodplains.
- **Lower-course landforms:** deltas, levees, alluvial fans.
- **Waterfall forms where:** hard rock lies over softer rock.
- **Oxbow lake:** cut-off loop of a meander.
- **Coastal erosion sequence:** cave → arch → stack.
- **Glacial valley shape:** U-shaped.
- **Moraine, defined:** rock and debris deposited by a glacier.
- **Three kinds of moraine:** lateral, medial, terminal.
- **Fjord, defined:** glacial valley drowned by the sea.
- **Wind erosion landforms:** yardangs, ventifacts, deflation hollows, desert pavements.
- **Crescent-shaped dune:** barchan.
- **Karst topography:** landforms from groundwater dissolving limestone.
- **Stalactite position:** hangs from the cave ceiling.
- **Stalagmite position:** rises from the cave floor.
- **GLOF, in full:** glacial lake outburst flood.
- **Zabo system:** Nagaland's traditional soil and water conservation farming.

## Key formulas

:::formula Earth's layers, outside in
Crust (30–40 km land, 5–7 km ocean) → mantle → outer core (liquid) → inner core (solid).
Slip: lithosphere = crust + **upper mantle**, not crust alone.
:::

:::formula Plate boundaries
Convergent = collide → fold mountains (Himalaya) · Divergent = apart → new crust (Mid-Atlantic Ridge) · Transform = slide → earthquakes (San Andreas).
:::

:::formula River course
Upper: V-valley, waterfall, rapids → Middle: meander, oxbow lake, floodplain → Lower: delta, levee, alluvial fan.
:::

:::formula Weathering vs erosion
Weathering = breaks **in place** · Erosion = breaks **and moves**.
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Boundary that formed the Himalaya: convergent (fold mountains).
- Mid-Atlantic Ridge: divergent boundary.
- San Andreas Fault: transform boundary.
- Outer core: liquid; inner core: solid.
- Glacier valley: U-shaped; river valley: V-shaped.
- Cave → arch → stack: wave erosion.
- Crescent-shaped sand dune: barchan.
- Delta with the Sundarbans: Ganga–Brahmaputra delta.
- Borra Caves: limestone karst caves in Andhra Pradesh.
- Terminal moraine: deposited at the glacier's snout.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Himalaya and divergence
Himalaya formed at a **convergent** boundary. Divergent boundaries make ridges and new crust.
:::

:::tip Weathering does not move rock
Any mention of transport means erosion, not weathering.
:::

:::tip Stalactite vs stalagmite
stala**C**tite = **C**eiling; stala**G**mite = **G**round.
:::

:::tip Landslide causes need people too
Give natural **and** human causes (deforestation, mining, road building, construction).
:::
