## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Tangent to a circle:** a line that meets the circle at exactly one point.
- **Point of contact:** the common point of a tangent and the circle.
- **Secant of a circle:** a line that meets the circle at two points.
- **Tangents through a point inside the circle:** none.
- **Tangents through a point on the circle:** exactly one.
- **Tangents through a point outside the circle:** exactly two.
- **Theorem 10.1:** the tangent is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
- **Theorem 10.2:** tangents drawn from an external point are equal in length.
- **Line from external point to centre:** bisects the angle between the two tangents.
- **Tangents at the ends of a diameter:** parallel to each other.
- **Parallelogram circumscribing a circle:** is a rhombus.
- **Chord of outer circle touching inner circle:** is bisected at the point of contact.
- **Congruence rule in the equal-tangents proof:** RHS.

## Key formulas

:::formula Length of a tangent
$\sqrt{d^2-r^2}$ ($d$ = distance from centre, $r$ = radius)
:::

:::formula Angle between two tangents
$\angle APB+\angle AOB=180^\circ$
:::

:::formula Quadrilateral circumscribing a circle
$AB+CD=AD+BC$
:::

:::formula Chord of outer circle touching inner circle
$2\sqrt{R^2-r^2}$
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Tangent length from a point 13 cm from the centre, radius 5 cm: 12 cm.
- Tangent length from a point 25 cm from the centre, radius 7 cm: 24 cm.
- Angle between a radius and the tangent at its end: $90^\circ$.
- If $\angle APB=60^\circ$ between two tangents, then $\angle AOB$: $120^\circ$.
- Number of tangents a circle can have altogether: infinitely many.
- Number of tangents parallel to a given secant: two.
- A tangent and the circle have in common: one point only.
- If $\angle APB=50^\circ$, then $\angle OAB$: $25^\circ$.
- Distance between two parallel tangents of a circle of radius $r$: $2r$.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Right angle not marked
Join the centre to the point of contact and mark $90^\circ$ before anything else.
:::

:::tip Hypotenuse mix-up
The hypotenuse is the line from the centre to the outside point, not the tangent.
:::

:::tip Half the chord
With concentric circles Pythagoras gives half the chord: double it.
:::

:::tip Missing reasons
Write "radius ⟂ tangent" and "tangents from an external point" as reasons.
:::

:::tip RHS, not SAS
The equal-tangents proof uses RHS congruence, then CPCT.
:::
