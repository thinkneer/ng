## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Prime number:** has exactly two factors, 1 and itself.
- **Composite number:** has more than two factors.
- **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:** every composite number is a unique product of primes, apart from order.
- **HCF by prime factors:** product of the smallest power of each common prime.
- **LCM by prime factors:** product of the greatest power of every prime involved.
- **Coprime numbers:** their HCF is 1.
- **Rational number:** can be written as $\dfrac pq$, with $p$, $q$ integers and $q\ne0$.
- **Irrational number:** cannot be written as $\dfrac pq$.
- **Number ending in 0:** its prime factors include both 2 and 5.
- **Rational plus irrational:** always irrational.
- **Non-zero rational times irrational:** always irrational.
- **Product of two irrationals:** may be rational, e.g. $\sqrt2\times\sqrt2=2$.
- **Word-problem cue for HCF:** largest, greatest, maximum.
- **Word-problem cue for LCM:** smallest, least, together again.

## Key formulas

:::formula Product rule for two numbers
$\text{HCF}(a,b)\times\text{LCM}(a,b)=a\times b$
:::

:::formula Prime dividing a square
If prime $p$ divides $a^2$, then $p$ divides $a$.
:::

:::formula LCM of two numbers from their HCF
$\text{LCM}=\dfrac{a\times b}{\text{HCF}}$
:::

:::formula HCF and LCM of two coprime numbers
HCF $=1$, LCM $=a\times b$
:::

## Answer skeletons

Step marks in square brackets are the usual CBSE split (estimates).

:::skeleton P · √p is irrational (3 marks)
Assume $\sqrt p=\dfrac ab$, $a$, $b$ coprime [1] → $pb^2=a^2$, so $p$ divides $a$; put $a=pc$ [1] → $p$ divides $b$ [½] → contradiction and conclusion [½].
:::

:::skeleton P · a + b√p is irrational (2 marks)
Assume it is rational, $=\dfrac mn$ [½] → isolate $\sqrt p$ [½] → right side rational, so $\sqrt p$ rational: contradiction [½] → conclusion [½].
:::

:::skeleton S · HCF and LCM (2–3 marks)
Prime factorisation of each number [1] → HCF [½–1] → LCM [½–1] (verify with the product rule if asked).
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- HCF of two consecutive natural numbers: 1.
- LCM of two coprime numbers: their product.
- HCF of two different primes: 1.
- If HCF(26, 91) = 13, then LCM(26, 91): 182.
- Prime factorisation of 144: $2^4\times3^2$.
- Smallest prime number: 2, the only even prime.
- Smallest composite number: 4.
- HCF of the smallest prime and the smallest composite number: 2.
- The number $\pi$: irrational ($\tfrac{22}{7}$ is only an approximation).
- The HCF of two numbers always: divides their LCM.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Missing "coprime"
The irrationality proof needs "$a$ and $b$ coprime" in the first line.
:::

:::tip Product rule for three numbers
HCF × LCM = product works for two numbers only.
:::

:::tip HCF and LCM swapped
HCF: smallest powers of common primes. LCM: greatest powers of all primes.
:::

:::tip Remainders in HCF problems
Subtract the remainders from the numbers before finding the HCF.
:::

:::tip Euclid's algorithm
Not in boards: use prime factorisation unless the question says otherwise.
:::
