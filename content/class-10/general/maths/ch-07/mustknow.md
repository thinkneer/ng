## Must-know lines and definitions

- **Abscissa of a point:** its $x$-coordinate.
- **Ordinate of a point:** its $y$-coordinate.
- **Point on the x-axis:** has the form $(x,0)$.
- **Point on the y-axis:** has the form $(0,y)$.
- **Distance of $(x,y)$ from the x-axis:** $\lvert y\rvert$.
- **Distance of $(x,y)$ from the y-axis:** $\lvert x\rvert$.
- **Collinear points test:** the two shorter distances add up to the longest.
- **Equidistant point method:** equate the squares of the two distances.
- **Unknown ratio method:** take the ratio as $k:1$.
- **Points of trisection:** divide the segment in $1:2$ and $2:1$.
- **Diagonals of a parallelogram:** bisect each other, so their midpoints coincide.
- **Square test:** four equal sides and two equal diagonals.
- **Rhombus test:** four equal sides, diagonals not necessarily equal.
- **Rectangle test:** opposite sides equal and diagonals equal.
- **Right triangle test:** square of the longest side equals the sum of the other two squares.

## Key formulas

:::formula Distance formula
$\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$
:::

:::formula Distance from the origin
$\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$
:::

:::formula Section formula (internal, ratio m : n)
$\left(\dfrac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\ \dfrac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n}\right)$
:::

:::formula Midpoint formula
$\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$
:::

## Quick 1-mark facts (MCQ, A–R)

- Distance of $(-6,8)$ from the origin: 10 units.
- Distance of $(3,-4)$ from the x-axis: 4 units.
- Midpoint of $(-2,8)$ and $(-6,-4)$: $(-4,2)$.
- Distance between $(a,0)$ and $(0,b)$: $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.
- Centre of a circle: the midpoint of any diameter.
- Distance between $(0,5)$ and $(-5,0)$: $5\sqrt2$ units.
- The point $(0,-7)$ lies: on the y-axis.
- Ratio in which the midpoint divides a segment: $1:1$.
- Area of a triangle from coordinates: not in boards.

## Mark-losing traps

:::tip Sign inside the bracket
$x_2-x_1$ with a negative coordinate: $5-(-1)=6$, not 4.
:::

:::tip m and n swapped
In the section formula $m$ multiplies the second point, $n$ the first.
:::

:::tip Distance from an axis
Distance from the x-axis is $\lvert y\rvert$, not $\lvert x\rvert$.
:::

:::tip Sides only
Four equal sides prove a rhombus; a square also needs equal diagonals.
:::

:::tip Missing word "units"
A distance is written with "units"; a ratio and a point are not.
:::
