# Ch 9 · Some Applications of Trigonometry — formulas and key facts

## Terms

- **Line of sight:** eye to object.
- **Angle of elevation:** line of sight above the horizontal. **Angle of depression:** line of sight below the horizontal.
- Angle of depression from the top = angle of elevation from the ground point (alternate angles).

## Relations

:::formula Heights and distances
$\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{ground distance}}$   so $h=d\tan\theta$ and $d=\dfrac{h}{\tan\theta}$<br>
Slant length $l$ at angle θ to the ground: height $=l\sin\theta$, foot distance $=l\cos\theta$<br>
Sun's elevation: $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{shadow}}$
:::

| θ | tan θ | h ÷ d |
|---|---|---|
| 30° | $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}$ | 0.577 |
| 45° | 1 | 1 |
| 60° | $\sqrt3$ | 1.732 |

## Standard pictures

| Picture | What to do |
|---|---|
| One point, one tower | one triangle, one ratio |
| Two points on the same side | two tan equations with a shared height; distances subtract |
| Two points on opposite sides | two tan equations; distances add |
| Up and down from one point | draw the horizontal through the observer; add the two vertical parts |
| Flagstaff or tower on a building | same ground distance, two heights; subtract |
| Broken tree | standing part $=d\tan\theta$, broken part $=\dfrac{d}{\cos\theta}$; add |

Use $\sqrt3=1.732$ only when the question says so.
